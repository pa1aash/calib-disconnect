# Playing Psychic: Using Thought Trees to Predict Reasoning Models Accuracy on Coding Tasks (2604.16931)
Status: preprint (arXiv v1 2026-04-18, cs.AI; venue not checked). Source: raw/ft/2604.16931.txt. Text basis: FULL TEXT.
Measured: whether structural and semantic features of a reasoning trace, organized as an LLM-built "thought-tree", predict whether the trace solves a programming task (Sections 3.2, 3.3); a retry intervention on traces flagged by those features (Section 4.3.2).
Models and benchmarks: DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ-32B under test-time scaling; CRUXEval, SAFIM, and CodeLingua, with composed L2 and L3 variants (Section 4.1). Trees built with Gemini 2.0 Flash and Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct; Random Forest classifiers trained on 80% of L1 traces, tested on held-out L1, L2, and L3 (Section 4.1).
Findings: "our trained classifiers can correctly predict up to 89%" of outcomes across 3 benchmarks (Section 1; Figure 6); tree size, segment count, and trace length associate with failure, lexical diversity with success (Section 4.3.1, Figure 8); retry recovery is highest at L1 and decays at L2 and L3 (Section 4.3.2).
Leaves open: no comparison with the model's final stated confidence, token probability, or a hidden-state probe; accuracy and F1 rather than AUROC or calibration; tasks are output prediction, fill-in-the-middle, and translation, not NL-to-code generation.
