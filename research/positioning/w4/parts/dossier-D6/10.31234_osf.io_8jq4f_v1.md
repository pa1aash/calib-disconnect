# Reasoning ability versus metacognitive sensitivity (10.31234/osf.io/8jq4f_v1)
Status: preprint only (PsyArXiv/OSF, posted 2026-01-06; Crossref subtype "preprint"). Source: research/notes/8jq4f_v1.md. Text basis: ABSTRACT-ONLY (Crossref record; osf.io not an allowed host, no arXiv version found in the note).
Measured: item-level metacognitive sensitivity, "the ability to discriminate, on an item-by-item basis, between correct and incorrect inferences" (abstract).
Models and benchmarks: "five experiments across 17 models spanning five LLM series"; benchmarks not stated in the abstract. Non-code per the note summary.
Findings: "Although reasoning ability reliably scales with model size, metacognitive sensitivity does not; in some series, the smallest models even exhibit higher metacognitive sensitivity than their larger counterparts." Long reasoning traces "hinder accurate self-evaluation"; distillation "transfers the impairment" (abstract).
Leaves open: whether the reasoning-trace impairment holds for code with execution labels, and for probes versus verbal readouts (L2 reasoning vs non-reasoning).
