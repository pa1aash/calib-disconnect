# How LLMs detect and correct their own errors, internal confidence signals (2604.22271)
Status: preprint only (arXiv 2604.22271v2, 1 May 2026; first posting date not verified from the note). Source: research/notes/how-llms-detect-and-correct-their-own-errors-the-role-of-internal-confidence-sig.md. Text basis: FULL TEXT.
Measured: whether verbal confidence, answer log-probabilities, and a linear probe at the post-answer newline token (PANL) predict the model's own verification judgment and second-attempt correctness; activation patching and noising test causal sufficiency (Sec 3, Fig 3, Fig 5, App. E.5).
Models and benchmarks: Gemma 3 27B-it (primary; TriviaQA, n=7,227), Qwen 2.5 7B (n=3,500), plus MNLI; both instruction-tuned. No code.
Findings: Gemma scored "75.5% on the TriviaQA dataset"; verbal confidence and log-probabilities gave AUROC "0.74 and 0.76" with ECE "0.13 and 0.20" (Sec 3); within incorrect trials, "At layer 30, PANL achieves AUROC = .986 against the best behavioural baseline of .908" (Sec 3, Fig 3B).
Leaves open: the probe-beats-verbal pattern and causal patching are shown outside code with no execution labels and no base-versus-instruct comparison (L2 questions 1, 2, 12; A15). Whether the evaluative signal exists in code models is untested.
