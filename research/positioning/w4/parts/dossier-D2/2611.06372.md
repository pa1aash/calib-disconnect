# Priors govern test-time compute allocation (2611.06372)
Status: concurrent (preprint only, posted November 2026 per arXiv id; no venue). Source: research/notes/give-up-to-get-more-priors-not-policies-govern-test-time-compute-allocation.md. Text basis: FULL TEXT.
Measured: how a shared budget of repeated attempts is divided across inputs, against uniform, round-robin (RR), and oracle allocation, on stored samples (Sections 2, 4, 5; Tables 1 to 3).
Models and benchmarks: Large Language Monkeys samples (Llama-3-8B, Llama-3-70B, Pythia, Gemma-2B) on MATH, GSM8K, CodeContests, MiniF2F; new Qwen2.5 samples (Sections 4 and 5).
Findings: the best difficulty-proportional rule captures "at most 13% on hard workloads, even given true pass rates" (Abstract). With a verifier and a related-model history prior, Thompson Sampling raises solved share "from 14% to 27%"; "without such a prior, no policy we tested beats round-robin" (Abstract). Code appears only as CodeContests.
Leaves open: signals are pass-rate priors, not verbal confidence or probes (searched: verbalized, probe, ECE). It supplies oracle and RR comparators, but no elicited-confidence routing. It cites a rule that samples until a confidence threshold is met (Huang et al., 2025, Section 3).
