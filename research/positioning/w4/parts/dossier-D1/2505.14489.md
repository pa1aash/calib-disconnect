# Reasoning models better express their confidence (2505.14489)
Status: preprint only (arXiv ID 2505, May 2025; v2 dated 2025-10-22 carries a NeurIPS checklist, acceptance not stated in the text). Source: research/notes/reasoning-models-better-express-their-confidence.md. Text basis: FULL TEXT.

Measured: verbalized confidence calibration of reasoning models vs non-reasoning counterparts, along the chain of thought, with slow-thinking ablations. Models and benchmarks: six reasoning models from four 32B-scale backbones (R1-Distill-Qwen, OR1-Preview, QwQ vs Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct; GLM-Z1 vs GLM-4; EXAONE-Deep vs EXAONE-3.5; Qwen3 thinking vs non-thinking); TriviaQA, NonambigQA, MMLU-Pro and SuperGPQA (math and non-math subsets), 1,000 samples each (Section 3.1). Findings: reasoning models achieve "strictly better confidence calibration than their non-reasoning counterparts in 33 out of the 36 settings" (Abstract); calibration improves along the CoT, and removing slow-thinking behaviors lowers it (Section 4). Leaves open: no code, no probes, no execution labels; the code reasoning-on vs reasoning-off contrast for L2 is untested here.

Breadth:
- confidence families: verbalized only.
- model families: Qwen, GLM, EXAONE, Skywork-OR1.
- scales: 32B backbones.

- elicitation formats: verbalized confidence; format comparison not found (searched: prompt template, format ablation).
- label source: answer correctness on QA; no execution.
- label independence: not present.

- calibration metrics: ECE, Brier, AUROC.
- granularity: answer level; confidence tracked across CoT segments.

Bears on: L2 (reasoning vs non-reasoning, calibration of stated confidence): NARROWS, general-domain precedent; the residual is code with execution labels plus probe readouts. L1, L3, L4: no effect.
