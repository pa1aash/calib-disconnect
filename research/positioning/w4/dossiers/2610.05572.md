# Task difficulty versus run-level failure prediction (2610.05572)
Status: concurrent (preprint only, posted October 2026 per arXiv id; no venue found). Source: research/notes/disentangling-task-difficulty-from-run-level-failure-in-agent-failure-prediction.md. Text basis: FULL TEXT.
Measured: decomposition of pooled AUROC of agent failure predictors into within-unit and cross-unit pairs; early run-level discrimination of trajectory predictors, released monitors, and hidden-state probes; token-budget policies (RQ1 to RQ3).
Models and benchmarks: SWE-agent (three Llama-3 models), OpenHands (Qwen3-Coder-480B), Laguna-XS.2 and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B for probes; SWE-bench repeated attempts, 10 per task (Table 1; Section on corpora).
Findings: "more than 99.93% of the underlying positive-negative pairs are cross-unit"; a difficulty oracle reaches "AUROC up to 0.945" while "at chance within task" (0.500); task-level allocation beats abort-only under fixed budgets; early stopping helps only when within-task AUROC is "roughly 0.84-0.93", versus "0.50-0.55" for early monitors (Abstract; RQ3).
Leaves open: agent trajectories, not single-shot code; no verbal confidence (searched: verbalized, self-report). Bears on L2 question 13 (between versus within problem discrimination), L3 (allocation versus oracle), and F (evaluation validity).
