# Open the Oyster, code reasoning confidence in LLMs (2511.02197)
Status: preprint only (arXiv 2511.02197v1, 4 Nov 2025). Source: research/notes/open-the-oyster-empirical-evaluation-and-improvement-of-code-reasoning-confidenc.md. Text basis: FULL TEXT.
Measured: verbal ("intrinsic") confidence on code reasoning, plus two prompt strategies (reassess with self-doubt; reflective, a fresh evaluator call) and Platt scaling (Sec 3.2, 3.3, RQ1 to RQ3).
Models and benchmarks: DeepSeek-Chat and DeepSeek-Reasoner, Qwen3 1.7B, 14B, 32B, GPT-3.5-Turbo; REval (3,152 items, subtasks CCP, PSP, EPP, OP) and CRUXEval (800; CRUXEval-I and -O) (Sec 4.2, Table 1). Tasks are output and input prediction, not program synthesis. Temperature 0.
Findings: "the intrinsic confidence ECE of DeepSeek-Reasoner on the CCP subtask is only 0.066, which is much lower than that of DeepSeek-Chat (0.143), Qwen3-1.7B (0.231), and GPT-3.5-Turbo (0.338)" (Sec 5.1.1, Table 2); reasoning model best by up to 0.680 ECE; reassess plus Platt scaling improves up to 0.541 ECE (Abstract).
Leaves open: no hidden-state, token-probability, or sampling readout, so no probe-versus-verbal gap; the reasoning contrast is across different models (V3 vs R1), not a toggle on one model (L2 questions 1, 2, 9); labels are answer correctness on prediction tasks, not execution of generated programs.
