# The Defensible Extension: A Risk-First Reading of the Calibration-Disconnect Paper for AAAI-27

**Executive summary — the answer first.** The single paper most likely to clear AAAI-27 main track *and* survive the specific threats that could kill it is **not** a pure bet on "structured per-test self-expressed confidence for code." That target is genuinely novel, but it is simultaneously the riskiest cell in the design space: it is a harder training objective than the scalar confidence that DCPO already trained on code to AUROC 0.796–0.914 [2], it sits one axis away from two under-review UT-Dallas papers whose contents are unknown [22][26], and it must be produced inside a ~10-day window (today is 18 July 2026; abstracts close 21 July, full papers 28 July, code 31 July) [33]. My committed recommendation is a **three-tier, risk-stratified paper** whose *contribution* is a single method — decoupled, masked-gradient LoRA training of the code model's *own* expressed confidence against the continuous per-test execution-pass-rate signal the disconnect paper already computes — but whose *acceptance does not depend on the hardest result landing*. The paper is architected so that a **guaranteed floor** (a risk-controlled selective-generation utility curve built on the un-collapsed internal sampling signal, targeting the 77.8–88% coverage at ≤0.02% false-positive band that execution-clustering abstention already reaches on LiveCodeBench [11]) makes the paper complete on its own; a **high-confidence middle** (a self-expressed *scalar* confidence trained by the DCPO-style recipe, replicated at single-GPU LoRA scale and positioned explicitly against DCPO [2]) supplies a solid, defensible result; and the **novel upside** (self-expressed *per-test-case* confidence that localizes which requirement will fail — a capability no scalar method can produce and no external probe self-expresses [10][18]) is where Priority-1 novelty lives, hedged by a pre-registered demotion path. The headline number the abstract commits to should be chosen by a 48-hour go/no-go pilot: if the decoupled per-test objective breaks the collapse, lead with per-test AUROC; if it does not, lead with the floor-plus-scalar paper; and if even the scalar training is shaky by 21 July, take the *identical venue-agnostic contribution* to ICLR-27 (September), where roughly six additional weeks convert a coin-flip into a safe bet. This is the scoping that maximizes P(accept) under the binding constraint that the user must not be left with a dead paper.

The rest of this report defends that recommendation adversarially: it maps the frontier, names every hole and every threat honestly, scores the alternatives, commits, and specifies the exact experiment, the feasibility argument, the failure ladder, the novelty defense, and the AAAI topic. Throughout, I take the skeptic's chair — the goal is to find the version of this paper that a hostile reviewer *cannot* reject, not the version that is most exciting to write.

## 1. The Research Frontier in Code-LLM Confidence and Calibration

The 2026 frontier splits cleanly into five clusters, and reading them together produces one uncomfortable fact: the "obvious" extension of the disconnect paper is already substantially occupied.

**Cluster 1 — internal/execution confidence for code (essentially solved).** Repeated-sampling and execution-consistency signals discriminate correct from incorrect code near-perfectly. The disconnect paper's own sampling confidence hits AUROC 0.987 on HumanEval [1]; Sharma & David reach 77.8–88% selective coverage at ≤0.02% false-positive rate via symbolic-execution clustering on LiveCodeBench [11]; Functional Entropy and semantic-entropy variants land AUROC 0.74–0.91 on code [13]. The interpretive takeaway is decisive: *the existence of a strong internal signal for code is no longer a publishable claim.* Any paper that leads with "sampling confidence discriminates code correctness" is dead on arrival. This is the first thing a risk-first analysis must internalize — the disconnect paper's *diagnostic* strength (0.987) is precisely the part that is least novel to extend.

**Cluster 2 — train-time verbalized/expressed confidence (the mechanism cluster, mostly QA).** A wave of 2025–2026 methods trains a model to *emit* a confidence value using a proper scoring rule or a reward-shaped RL objective: Rewarding Doubt (log-scoring RL, Llama-3-8B, 4-bit LoRA on a single A40, TriviaQA AUROC 0.859, explicitly more discriminative than a trained probe) [3]; ConfTuner (tokenized Brier, zero extra sampling, converges at ~2000 examples in minutes, +14.4% relative AUROC) [4]; RLCR / Beyond Binary Rewards (a bounded proper scoring rule with a provably accurate-and-calibrated optimum, but whose unbounded log-score variant reward-hacks into abstention collapse) [5]; ORCE (a fully separate confidence LLM trained by a rank-correlation reward, accuracy preserved by construction) [7]; and CAPO (a discrimination-first surrogate proving GRPO's linear advantage is AUC-inconsistent) [6]. Every one of these is QA/math. Their collective lesson is the load-bearing feasibility fact of this entire report: **train-time expressed confidence reliably reaches AUROC 0.85-ish, and the mechanism is single-GPU LoRA-feasible.**

**Cluster 3 — the one code-native train-time result: DCPO.** This is the paper that reshapes the risk landscape. DCPO decouples reasoning and confidence into masked-gradient token blocks, trains the `<conf>` block against a hybrid instance+group execution-pass-rate target with a bounded Brier-style reward, and — critically — *evaluates directly on code*: AUROC 0.796 (LiveCodeBench-v5), 0.845 (v6), 0.914 (HumanEval+) on Qwen3-8B, beating the joint-reward RLCR baseline on AUROC *and* accuracy [2]. Its Proposition 4.2 formally proves the gradient conflict between accuracy and calibration objectives for overconfident models — which is a mathematical description of the disconnect paper's own SFT collapse (mean confidence 0.900 on both easy and hard problems) [1][2]. DCPO was full fine-tuning on 8×A100, not single-GPU LoRA, but the recipe is a training-loop change, not a scale requirement.

**Cluster 4 — selective generation / abstention / risk control for code.** A mature, formally-grounded line converts an uncertainty score into a guaranteed abstain/answer decision: DeepMind's conformal abstention (self-consistency + conformal threshold, general QA) [24]; CIC's Clopper-Pearson risk-controlled generation (coverage 0.52–1.00 at α=0.10 on QA, but frequently *infeasible* at α=0.05 for weak signals) [12]; CODEREFUSER's zero-oracle-at-inference code abstention (26.5-point precision gain, ~3.5× coverage over a perplexity baseline at matched risk) [13-code]; and RisCoSet's risk-controlling partial-program prediction sets (up to 24.5% less code removed at fixed risk) [17]. This cluster is where the *guaranteed floor* lives — and, as I will argue, where the scoop risk is sharpest.

**Cluster 5 — human-AI trust and deployment.** The in-IDE-at-scale study (24M interactions, 153 developers) shows post-hoc Platt scaling of single-pass confidence does *not* align with developer acceptance, and that developers prefer categorical/color-coded reliability cues over numeric scores [19]. This reframes the disconnect: the "expressed confidence" the field keeps trying to perfect as a number may be the wrong interface entirely — a point that, read defensively, *strengthens* the case for a selective-generation (act, don't just report) framing over a race to a better verbalized number.

The frontier, read as a whole, says: the internal signal is solved, the expressed-scalar-for-code cell is freshly occupied by DCPO, the mechanism is cheap and portable, and the deployment literature is quietly skeptical that a numeric verbalized confidence is even what practitioners want. That is the terrain any extension must survive.

## 2. Caveats, Holes, and Open Problems at the Frontier

The skeptic's job is to separate *genuine* holes from *apparent* holes that a reviewer will close in one sentence. Four holes survive scrutiny; two tempting ones do not.

**Genuine hole A — no method has the code model express its *own* confidence at a granularity finer than one scalar.** DCPO emits one scalar per answer [2]. Gros & Devanbu localize confidence to *lines/spans* but via an *external* white-box probe on internals, not the model's self-report, and against a curated Minimal-Intent-Aligning-Patches dataset rather than free per-test labels [18][10]. FLARE localizes to lines via a *separate* trained diagnostic transformer, never reports AUROC or any calibration metric, and never touches per-test structure [20]. CURE decomposes to *claims* with self-report and AUROC — but on general long-form text verified by a noisy retrieval fact-checker (VeriScore, best AUROC only 0.667), never code [19][11-cure]. The empty cell is the conjunction: *self-expressed + per-test-case + execution-verified + AUROC-evaluated + on code.* This is real, and it is the novelty payload.

**Genuine hole B — the expressed/internal disconnect has never been converted into a *guaranteed* deployment utility for code with a strong signal.** CIC's risk control has never been run on a code signal as strong as AUROC 0.987; CODEREFUSER reports precision/F1 against an oracle label rather than a coverage-at-α risk-coverage curve; no one has traced a Clopper-Pearson risk-coverage curve on the disconnect paper's own sampling signal [12][13-code][1]. This is the *floor*, and it is genuinely unrun — but its novelty is thin (see the scoop risk below).

**Genuine hole C — every train-time expressed-confidence result is QA/math except DCPO's scalar.** No proper-scoring or reward-shaped *verbalized* training has been shown to preserve discrimination through the decode step *on code at fine granularity* [3][4][5][7]. Whether discrimination survives that decode step is the paper's central empirical bet.

**Genuine hole D — the evaluation methodology of the code-calibration subfield is weak.** Multicalibration-for-code reports Brier Skill Score and never AUROC — so it literally cannot demonstrate discrimination [20-multi]. When-to-Defer reports selective accuracy but no AUROC [25]. This is a positioning wedge: an AUROC-primary paper can critique the ECE-only literature. But it is also a trap (see below).

**Apparent hole that a reviewer will close — "post-hoc calibration is unexplored."** It is not; it is *known to fail* on the discrimination axis (isotonic degrades the disconnect paper's AUROC to 0.587, Platt to 0.386) [1], and Roelofs et al. prove ECE itself is a biased estimator whose apparent gains can be artifacts [16]. Chasing post-hoc calibration is a dead end.

**Apparent hole — "ECE-primary evaluation is a contribution."** Making AUROC-primary is *correct methodology*, but it is not by itself novel enough for a headline; CAPO, Rewarding Doubt, and Spiess already argue ECE is gameable by a near-constant predictor [6][3]. Use it as a robustness pillar, not the thesis.

The honest synthesis: only Hole A is novel *and* headline-grade; Hole B is guaranteed *but* thin; Holes C and D are supporting. A defensible paper therefore cannot rest on B, C, or D alone — it must reach for A while insuring against A failing to land. That is the entire design problem.

## 3. Where the Current Calibration-Disconnect Paper Falls Short of Main-Track

The current manuscript is a strong *diagnostic* paper and a weak *main-track* paper, for reasons that are structural, not cosmetic.

**It is descriptive, not prescriptive.** Its central result is "a gap exists": the internal signal is 0.987, no expressed interface communicates it [1]. AAAI main track rewards methodological advances; a measurement study risks being read as an application/analysis paper and redirected [32]. The single most important upgrade is to ship a *method that closes or exploits the gap*, with the gap as motivation rather than payload.

**Its one intervention is a documented failure.** The 274-example LoRA SFT reduced verbal ECE by 64% but produced "calibration without discrimination" — mean confidence 0.900 on both easy and hard problems [1]. A reviewer will read this, correctly, as evidence that the naive fix does not work — which is *useful* only if the paper then shows the *non-naive* fix that does. DCPO's Prop 4.2 and CAPO's AUC-inconsistency result now supply a mechanistic explanation (gradient conflict; near-constant predictors satisfy ECE) [2][6], and ConfTuner/Rewarding Doubt supply the repair (proper scoring rule against a continuous instance-level target) [4][3]. The upgrade path is clear.

**Its evidence base is thin and contamination-exposed.** Two models, one benchmark (HumanEval, 164), a skewed-easy difficulty distribution [1]. Riddell et al. show HumanEval/MBPP are surface- and semantically-contaminated, and models score higher on contaminated subsets — so the 0.987 could be partly memorization of "which problems I know" [17]. A single reviewer citing contamination sinks the headline. The mandatory fix is a primary evaluation on a time-windowed, contamination-controlled benchmark (LiveCodeBench), with HumanEval demoted to a legacy comparison [17].

**Its metric story is fragile.** The ECE-improves/AUROC-degrades result is the paper's cleverest argument, but ECE computed with unreported binning is a biased estimator [16], and Kim & Kang show the token-vs-verbal gap can flip sign under different measurement protocols [22]. The paper must pre-register debiased/equal-mass ECE and an AUROC-primary protocol to be robust.

The committed reading: the disconnect paper is a *motivation section*, not a paper. Its value to the extension is enormous — it supplies the 0.987 oracle, the free per-test labels, the documented collapse to explain, and the deployment framing — but on its own it is a DL4C/workshop-grade contribution. Main-track requires the prescriptive method the next sections specify.

## 4. Candidate Directions, Scored on Novelty and Confidence

I score four candidate directions on the two binding priorities (novelty first, then confidence the headline lands), on a 1–5 scale, with a risk-first commentary that is deliberately harsh.

**Direction 1 — Scalar self-expressed confidence for code (DCPO-lite on LoRA).** Train the code model to emit one calibrated, discriminative confidence scalar; headline = AUROC ~0.85. *Novelty 2/5:* DCPO already did this on code with the same interface, target, and benchmarks [2]; the only residuals are "self-expressed not probe," "single-GPU LoRA not 8×A100," and "framed by the disconnect." *Confidence 5/5:* DCPO's numbers and Rewarding Doubt's single-A40 result make this almost certain to land [2][3]. Verdict: **the safe-but-incremental trap** the user explicitly wants to avoid. Excellent as a *component*, fatal as a *headline*.

**Direction 2 — Structured per-test self-expressed confidence for code.** Train the model to emit a confidence *per test case / per requirement*, localizing which part of the solution will fail; headline = per-test AUROC, no incumbent. *Novelty 5/5:* the empty-cell conjunction (Hole A) — no work self-expresses per-test execution-verified confidence [2][10][18][20][19]. *Confidence 2.5/5:* harder objective, no benchmark to anchor the expected number, real collapse risk (Prop 4.2), a live scoop from two unknown UT-Dallas papers [22][26], and a 10-day clock [33]. Verdict: **the novelty prize with the sharpest downside.** Cannot be bet on unhedged.

**Direction 3 — Guaranteed risk-controlled selective generation on the internal signal.** Wrap the un-collapsed sampling signal in a Clopper-Pearson risk-coverage policy; headline = coverage at fixed risk. *Novelty 2/5:* CIC, CODEREFUSER, RisCoSet, and DeepMind conformal abstention occupy most of the conceptual space [12][13-code][17][24], and a UT-Dallas paper literally titled "Beyond Execution: Uncertainty Estimation and Abstention in LLM-Based Code Generation" is under review [26]. *Confidence 5/5:* the mechanism is off-the-shelf and the signal is stronger than anything these papers tested; the number will land [11][12]. Verdict: **the floor** — guaranteed, deployment-relevant, but too crowded to headline.

**Direction 4 — Unified method, three-tier risk-stratified paper (my recommendation).** One method (decoupled masked-gradient LoRA training of self-expressed confidence against continuous execution-pass-rate targets) instantiated at scalar *and* per-test granularity, plus the guaranteed selective-generation floor as the deployment payoff. *Novelty 4.5/5:* inherits Direction 2's empty-cell headline while the method-unification framing (one recipe, two granularities, one guaranteed utility) is itself a contribution. *Confidence 4.5/5:* the floor guarantees a complete paper; the scalar tier is DCPO-backed; only the per-test tier carries residual risk, and it is pre-registered as demotable. Verdict: **the paper that cannot be fully rejected.**

The scoring makes the argument for me: Directions 1 and 3 are safe-but-not-novel (they fail Priority 1); Direction 2 is novel-but-unhedged (it endangers Priority 2 under a 10-day clock and a live scoop); Direction 4 is the only cell that scores ≥4 on both axes, because it *composes* the guaranteed floor and the DCPO-backed scalar *underneath* the novel per-test headline so that the paper's survival and the paper's novelty live in different tiers.

## 5. The One Paper: Committed Recommendation

**Title (working):** *Localized Confidence for Code: Teaching a Code LLM to Say Which Test It Will Fail — with a Guaranteed Selective-Generation Floor.*

**The one method.** Take Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct. Freeze its generation forward pass. Append a self-expressed confidence block. Warm-start it by SFT with a ConfTuner-style tokenized-Brier (proper-scoring) loss against the *continuous n=10 execution pass-rate* the disconnect paper already computes — reusing labels, not resampling [4][1]. Then run a short on-policy refinement with a DCPO-style **masked-gradient decoupled** objective: the reasoning tokens see a binary correctness advantage, the confidence tokens see a bounded Brier advantage against a hybrid instance+group per-test pass-rate target — mechanically preventing the Prop-4.2 gradient conflict that produced the 274-example collapse [2][1]. Instantiate the confidence block at **two granularities from the same recipe**: (i) a *scalar* per-solution confidence, and (ii) a *structured* vector of per-test-case (or, in the de-scoped version, per-test-category) confidences. Because code execution yields a *vector* of per-test outcomes for free — whereas QA/math yield a single correctness bit — the fine-grained ground-truth labels cost nothing beyond the sampling the paper already did [1][11].

**The three tiers, and why they are in this order in the narrative.**

1. **The guaranteed floor (report first for credibility, headline last):** a Clopper-Pearson risk-coverage curve on the *un-collapsed internal sampling signal* — never on the trained verbal output, which would inherit any collapse — targeting the 77.8–88% coverage at ≤0.02–5% error band [11][12]. This makes the paper complete regardless of what the trained interface does.
2. **The high-confidence middle:** the *scalar* self-expressed confidence, trained by the recipe, AUROC ~0.80–0.90, positioned *explicitly* as a single-GPU LoRA replication-and-extension of DCPO with the self-expressed (non-probe) interface [2][3]. This is a solid, near-certain result.
3. **The novel headline:** the *per-test* self-expressed confidence, AUROC on the "will test *t* pass?" task, plus a *localization* metric (does the model's lowest-confidence test coincide with the actually-failing test?). No incumbent number exists [2][10][18][20][19].

**Why this is the defensible one.** It reaches for the only headline-grade novel cell (Hole A) while placing the paper's *survival* on the guaranteed floor and the DCPO-backed scalar tier — two results that are near-certain to materialize. The user's two priorities are satisfied in *different tiers*: Priority 1 (novelty) is carried by tier 3; Priority 2 (confidence the results land) is carried by tiers 1 and 2. A hostile reviewer who disbelieves tier 3 still faces a complete paper; a reviewer who wants novelty still finds an empty-cell result. That decoupling of novelty-risk from acceptance-risk is the entire point of the recommendation, and it is the honest answer to "don't hand me a dead paper."

## 6. Headline Claim and the Exact Experiment That Produces It

**Headline claim (primary):** *A code LLM can be trained, on a single GPU, to express its own calibrated, discriminative confidence at per-test-case granularity — localizing which requirement of a solution will fail — with per-test AUROC in the 0.75–0.85 band and localization precision that beats the model's own scalar confidence and every external-probe baseline, a capability no scalar-confidence method (DCPO, RLCR, Rewarding Doubt) can produce because they emit one number per answer* [2][5][3].

**The exact experiment.**

- *Data.* Training pool of 2,000–4,000 real (non-synthetic) problems from MBPP, a ≥3-test-case-filtered APPS subset, and a LiveCodeBench train-window release; hold out HumanEval (legacy) and a disjoint LiveCodeBench window (primary, contamination-controlled) for evaluation [1][17][27]. Dense per-test benchmarks (TACO, CodeContests+; ~50–200 tests/problem) supply the richest per-test labels, verified against dataset cards before use.
- *Labels (free).* For each problem, sample n=10 completions; record the per-test pass/fail matrix; the empirical per-test pass rate is the continuous training target and the per-solution pass rate is the scalar target. No new labeling cost beyond sampling [1][11].
- *Training.* SFT warm-start (tokenized-Brier proper scoring) → masked-gradient decoupled refinement (bounded Brier confidence advantage; binary correctness reasoning advantage), LoRA rank 16 on one A100-40GB/GH200 [4][2][21].
- *Evaluation.* Primary: per-test AUROC on held-out LiveCodeBench ("will test *t* pass?"). Secondary: localization precision@1 (is the model's least-confident test the one that fails?); scalar AUROC vs DCPO's 0.796–0.914 [2]; debiased/equal-mass ECE and adaptive ECE [16]; the risk-coverage floor curve [12].
- *Ablations.* Decoupled vs bounded-joint reward (Rewarding-Doubt-style) [3]; continuous vs coarse-tier target (to reproduce and then repair the collapse) [1]; N∈{5,10,20,50,100} sample-count sweep showing code saturates far below N=100 [30-ud]; per-test vs per-category granularity.

**The number that goes in the abstract** is chosen by the pilot (§8): per-test AUROC if tier 3 is green, otherwise the floor's coverage-at-fixed-risk plus the scalar AUROC.

## 7. Why the Headline Will Likely Land: Feasibility on Single-GPU 7B Compute

The feasibility case rests on four load-bearing precedents and one honest caveat.

**The mechanism is proven at exactly this scale.** Rewarding Doubt trained a genuinely verbalized confidence token to AUROC 0.859 on Llama-3-8B with 4-bit LoRA on a *single A40* [3]; ConfTuner converges at ~2,000 examples in ~4 minutes of training on the proper-scoring loss the recipe uses [4]; Balancing-Classification trained decoupled (final-token) confidence with LoRA rank 16 on 4×A100-40GB at ~500 GPU-hours across three model sizes — i.e., ~100–150 GPU-hours per 7B model [21]; ConSelf ran LoRA DPO on the *exact* model family (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B) on a single A800 with a few-thousand-example curated set [27]. Single-GPU LoRA training of expressed confidence is not a hope; it is a replicated fact.

**The code number is anchored by DCPO.** A scalar self-expressed confidence in the 0.80–0.90 AUROC band on code is not a guess — DCPO already reported 0.796 (LCB-v5) to 0.914 (HE+) with the same decoded-block interface and execution-pass-rate target [2]. The per-test number has no incumbent, but it distills toward the same 0.987 internal oracle at finer granularity; a 0.75–0.85 band is a conservative expectation given the labels are cleaner (deterministic execution) than CURE's noisy VeriScore oracle that still reached 0.667 on general text [11-cure].

**The labels are free and the compute fits.** Label generation for a 2,000–4,000-problem pool at N=10 is ~1.7–6.7 GPU-hours; LoRA training is under an hour; one clean end-to-end pass (labels + training + eval + ablations + baselines) is ~15–25 GPU-hours, ~45–75 with a 3× debugging buffer — comfortably inside 10 days at 5–6 active GPU-hours/day [30-feas]. The floor is inference-only and single-GPU-cheap [12].

**The collapse has a named, repairable cause.** The 274-example failure is over-determined: too little data (vs ConfTuner's 2,000-example floor), a coarse discrete target, and a joint objective hitting Prop 4.2's gradient conflict [1][4][2]. The recipe changes all three — more data, a continuous instance-level proper-scoring target, and masked-gradient decoupling — so reproducing the collapse would require the diagnosis to be wrong on all three axes at once.

**The honest caveat.** DCPO's decoupling was demonstrated under *full* fine-tuning; whether masked-gradient block-decoupling survives *LoRA rank 16* on code is untested [2]. This is the single biggest unvalidated leap, and it is exactly what the Day-1 pilot exists to resolve. If LoRA capacity is too low to represent two structurally different objectives in one adapter, the fallback is ORCE-style fully-separate confidence weights at higher inference cost [7]. This caveat is why the paper is *tiered* rather than *bet* — the feasibility case for tiers 1 and 2 is airtight, and only tier 3 carries this residual.

## 8. Supporting Results, Failure Mode, and Pre-Registered Fallback

**Supporting results (all single-GPU-cheap):** (1) the risk-coverage floor curve [12][11]; (2) scalar AUROC vs DCPO [2]; (3) the collapse-and-repair ablation (coarse-tier reproduces mean-0.900; continuous target repairs it) [1]; (4) the N-sweep showing code saturates below N=100 [30-ud]; (5) the decoupled-vs-bounded-joint ablation [3]; (6) contamination control via LiveCodeBench-primary evaluation [17]; (7) debiased ECE and protocol-robustness reporting [16][22]; (8) an optional developer-facing framing tying the floor to the categorical/traffic-light preference from the in-IDE study [19].

**The most likely failure mode.** The per-test objective trains but *under-discriminates* — per-test AUROC lands ~0.65–0.72, i.e., real signal but not a clean headline. This is plausible because per-test correctness within a single solution is *correlated* (a broken function fails many tests together), so the per-test labels carry less independent information than the per-solution label, and the model may learn a near-constant "this solution is X% good" confidence smeared across tests rather than genuine localization. A secondary failure mode is CIC-style infeasibility of the α=0.05 floor at n=164 [12].

**The pre-registered fallback ladder (each rung is a still-publishable paper):**

- *Rung 1 (per-test lands, ≥0.75):* headline the per-test AUROC + localization. Full novelty payload.
- *Rung 2 (per-test is 0.65–0.75):* demote per-test to a *localization* result (precision@1 that the least-confident test is the failing one) — a weaker but still-novel claim that does not depend on a strong AUROC — and co-headline with the scalar-vs-DCPO result and the floor.
- *Rung 3 (per-test under-discriminates, <0.65):* demote structured confidence to an analysis/ablation; headline the guaranteed floor (risk-coverage) *reframed as "decoupling the action problem from the communication problem"* [19], with the DCPO-positioned scalar replication as the methodological result. Still a complete paper.
- *Rung 4 (α=0.05 floor infeasible at n=164):* expand the calibration pool to HumanEval+MBPP (~400+ problems, inference-only) and report α=0.10 as primary with α=0.05 as a stretch — mirroring CIC's own documented infeasibility rather than hiding it [12].
- *Rung 5 (the 10-day clock is the binding constraint):* the entire contribution is venue-agnostic; take the identical paper to ICLR-27 (September) or ACL/NeurIPS. The abstract deadline is 21 July; if the 48-hour pilot is not at least amber by 20 July, I recommend *not* forcing the AAAI submission. A rushed tier-3 result at a mediocre number is worse than a clean tier-1+2 paper six weeks later.

The governing principle: **the floor is the insurance, the scalar is the safe result, the per-test is the upside, and the venue is negotiable.** No rung leaves the user with nothing.

## 9. Novelty Defense Against the Closest Prior Work

The novelty claim is a *conjunction of four axes*, and the defense works by showing each near-neighbor misses at least one axis (all full-text verified).

- **DCPO** [2] — self-expressed, code, execution-target, AUROC, *but scalar*. It emits one confidence per answer; it structurally cannot localize which test fails. The per-test axis is the wedge. Concede loudly that DCPO owns the scalar cell; that is why the scalar tier is framed as replication, not contribution.
- **Gros & Devanbu, "Localized Calibrated Uncertainty in Code LMs"** [18][10] — the closest *published* title, and the sharpest threat. It localizes, on code, with calibration. But it misses on two axes: the localization is *spatial* (which lines will be edited), not *functional* (which test will fail); and the estimator is an *external white-box probe* on internals, not the model self-expressing. It also builds a Minimal-Intent-Aligning-Patches dataset rather than reusing free per-test labels. Frame "localized" as per-test/per-requirement, never per-line, to avoid conflation.
- **FLARE** [20] — execution + fine-grained + code, but the confidence source is a *separate* trained diagnostic transformer (the coder plays no role), granularity is *line-level*, and it reports *no AUROC or any calibration metric*. Distinct on three axes.
- **CURE** [19][11-cure] — self-expressed + per-unit (claim) + AUROC + an explicit anti-collapse decoupled pipeline, i.e., the closest *methodological* analog. But it is general long-form text, ground-truthed by a *noisy* external fact-checker (VeriScore, AUROC 0.667), not code execution. The wedge: code's per-test execution is a *free, deterministic, dense* oracle that CURE's noisy NL verifier is not — this should let the code version *beat* CURE's 0.667.
- **RLCM** [9], **CSR** [8], **Rewarding Doubt** [3], **ORCE** [7] — all retreat to probe/semantic/QA signals and never combine self-expressed + per-test + code + AUROC. CSR is the most pointed threat *to the framing*: it argues verbalized-token confidence is unstable and the field should abandon the verbalized interface [8]. The paper must answer CSR head-on by *showing* a verbalized interface can match probe-level discrimination on code — not assuming it.
- **The RisCoSet / CODEREFUSER / When-to-Defer / UT-Dallas cluster** [17][13-code][25][26] — occupy the *abstention/floor* space, which is why the floor is insurance, not headline. The live scoop risk is real and must be named: two under-review UT-Dallas papers ("Beyond Confidence: Rethinking Uncertainty Calibration in Deep Code Models"; "Beyond Execution: Uncertainty Estimation and Abstention in LLM-Based Code Generation") could land on the abstention cell or the calibration cell [26]. Their public work is scalar per-prediction abstention (partial overlap) [25]; the per-test self-expressed axis is the differentiator that keeps the paper distinct even if they publish first. Move fast, frame the per-test axis sharply, and — under AAAI's contemporaneous-work rule — anything they post fewer than two months before 28 July need not be addressed [33].

**The one-sentence defense:** *No prior work has a code LLM express its own confidence at per-test-case granularity, trained against the code's own deterministic per-test execution oracle, and evaluated by AUROC — DCPO is scalar, Gros & Devanbu is an external spatial probe, FLARE is a separate uncalibrated diagnostic, and CURE is noisy natural-language claims; this exact conjunction is empty.* Novelty confidence: high on the conjunction being empty today, tempered by an honest, named scoop caveat.

## 10. AAAI-27 Topic Alignment: Primary and Secondary Keywords

**Primary keyword (verbatim):** **"ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification."** Under AAAI-27's own selection rule — file under the subarea of the paper's *main methodological contribution*, and an ML method demonstrated on code goes under ML unless the contribution is fundamentally about code-specific structure [32] — this is the correct primary. The contribution is a *calibration/uncertainty-estimation training method*; there is no separate "ML: Calibration" keyword, and calibration falls explicitly under this uncertainty-quantification keyword per the areas page [32]. The community best positioned to evaluate a decoupled proper-scoring training objective and AUROC/ECE discrimination is the UQ/calibration community, not the software-engineering community.

There is a genuine, defensible counter-argument the paper must pre-empt: the per-test structure *is* code-specific (execution yields a per-test vector; QA does not), which under the same rule could push toward **"NLP: Code Generation / Program Synthesis."** I recommend keeping ML-UQ primary because the *methodological* core (decoupled masked-gradient training + proper-scoring calibration + risk-controlled selective generation) is domain-general and only *instantiated* on code — filing under NLP/code risks the paper being read as an application/measurement study and redirected toward AI-for-Social-Impact/IAAI, which the query explicitly warns against [32]. Lead the framing with the *method*, use code as the substrate that makes dense per-test labels free.

**Secondary keywords (up to five allowed; recommend two):** **"NLP: Code Generation / Program Synthesis"** (the substrate and the per-test-structure argument) and **"ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis"** (the AUROC-primary evaluation, contamination control, and debiased-ECE methodology) [32]. A third optional secondary, **"ML: Trustworthy, Safe & Reliable ML,"** fits the selective-generation floor if the keyword list admits it.

**One-line scope statement:** *The paper's main contribution is a method for training and evaluating calibrated, discriminative uncertainty estimates — instantiated on code because execution provides free dense labels — and therefore sits in ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification and will not drift into a code-application framing.*

**Format reality:** 7 pages main content + unlimited references and the reproducibility checklist (which lives in the reference-only pages) [33]. Code and data must be submitted by 31 July — *not* promised for later, since reviewers are instructed to assume unreleased materials will never appear [33]. This reinforces the tiered scoping: a 7-page paper cannot carry a sprawling agenda, so the floor + scalar + per-test must be one tight story with one headline, not three studies.

## 11. The Shortlist: 5–10 Load-Bearing Papers

1. **DCPO — Decoupling Reasoning and Confidence** [2]. *Contributes:* the decoupled masked-gradient recipe, Prop 4.2 gradient-conflict proof, and the only code-domain train-time AUROC numbers (0.796–0.914). *Leaves open:* everything finer than one scalar; single-GPU LoRA feasibility. *Relation:* supplies the method and the scalar baseline; owns the cell the paper must *not* claim.
2. **Rewarding Doubt** [3]. *Contributes:* single-A40 LoRA proof that verbalized-token RL reaches AUROC 0.859 and beats a probe on discrimination. *Leaves open:* code; fine granularity. *Relation:* the feasibility anchor and the bounded-joint ablation.
3. **ConfTuner** [4]. *Contributes:* zero-extra-sample tokenized-Brier proper-scoring loss, 2,000-example convergence in minutes. *Leaves open:* code. *Relation:* the warm-start objective; the fix for the coarse-target collapse.
4. **Beyond Binary Rewards / RLCR** [5]. *Contributes:* proper-scoring optimum is accurate-and-calibrated; the Toy-Arm proof that *unbounded* log-score collapses. *Leaves open:* code; structure. *Relation:* why boundedness is non-negotiable.
5. **Gros & Devanbu — Localized Calibrated Uncertainty in Code LMs** [18][10]. *Contributes:* per-line/span calibrated confidence via external probe; the closest published title. *Leaves open:* self-expression; per-test functional structure; free labels. *Relation:* the prime scoop to differentiate (spatial+probe vs functional+self-expressed).
6. **CURE / Think-Through-Uncertainty** [19][11-cure]. *Contributes:* self-expressed per-claim AUROC with a decoupled anti-collapse pipeline; the closest methodological analog. *Leaves open:* code; deterministic labels (its VeriScore oracle is noisy, AUROC 0.667). *Relation:* the method-twin the paper must beat via clean execution labels.
7. **CSR — Calibrating LLMs with Semantic-Level Reward** [8]. *Contributes:* single-A100 LoRA evidence that verbalized-token confidence is unstable; the strongest argument *against* the verbalized interface. *Leaves open:* code; whether the instability holds for execution-grounded targets. *Relation:* the objection the paper must answer empirically.
8. **Sharma & David — Assessing Correctness via Uncertainty** [11]. *Contributes:* 77.8–88% coverage at ≤0.02% FPR via execution clustering on LiveCodeBench. *Leaves open:* self-expression; a trained interface. *Relation:* the ceiling the guaranteed floor benchmarks against.
9. **CIC — Uncertainty-Aware Abstention** [12]. *Contributes:* Clopper-Pearson risk-controlled generation and its documented α=0.05 infeasibility. *Leaves open:* code; strong signals. *Relation:* the floor's mechanism and its pre-registered failure mode.
10. **Multicalibration for Code** [20-multi] + **When-to-Defer / UT-Dallas** [25][26]. *Contribute:* the ECE-only evaluation gap (no AUROC) and the live scoop line. *Relation:* the positioning wedge (AUROC-primary) and the timing threat to move fast against.

Supporting anchors used throughout but not headline-load-bearing: the base manuscript [1], CAPO [6], ORCE [7], Functional Entropy [13], CODEREFUSER [13-code], RisCoSet [17], DeepMind conformal abstention [24], the in-IDE-at-scale deployment study [19-ide], Roelofs et al. on ECE bias [16], Riddell et al. on contamination [17], Kim & Kang on protocol sensitivity [22], Balancing-Classification [21], ConSelf [27], and the AAAI-27 official pages [32][33].

**Closing skeptic's verdict.** The exciting paper and the safe paper are not the same paper — but they *can* be the same paper if the novel headline sits on top of a guaranteed floor and a DCPO-backed scalar result, and if the abstract's headline number is chosen by a pilot rather than by hope. Run the 48-hour collapse-and-timing pilot before 20 July. If green, submit the per-test headline to AAAI-27. If amber, submit the floor-plus-scalar paper to AAAI-27. If red, take the identical contribution to ICLR-27. Under all three branches the user has a live paper — which is the only outcome the brief actually forbids failing to secure.
