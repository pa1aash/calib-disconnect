# Which Test Will It Fail? A Committed AAAI-27 Proposal Extending the Calibration Disconnect

## Executive Summary

Write the paper that teaches a 7B code language model to say, in its own words, *which requirement of its own solution will fail* — and to be right about it. This is the committed recommendation, and it is decisive: it is the single direction that satisfies your binding priority order (Priority 1 = novelty, Priority 2 = confidence the results land) because it places novelty and safety in *different tiers of the same paper*. The genuine insurance against a weak or dead paper is two-fold — the DCPO-backed scalar tier, defensible even if incremental, and the ICLR-27 venue pivot (six extra weeks) — not the selective-generation floor, whose guarantee is completeness rather than main-track novelty.

The headline claim is a per-test-case / per-requirement failure-localization AUROC (area under the ROC curve — the probability the model ranks a passing requirement above a failing one) in a **target ~0.75–0.85 band, a number for which no incumbent exists** (pilot-set; plausible outcomes span ~0.65–0.85, since the closest structured precedent, CURE, reached only 0.667 [8] and §8B rates a 0.65–0.72 landing the top risk), produced by training the code model's *own* expressed confidence against the free per-test execution-pass-rate labels the diagnostic study already computes [1]. The mechanism is a decoupled, masked-gradient LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation — parameter-efficient fine-tuning) objective, trained with a bounded Brier reward (a proper scoring rule minimized only by honest probabilities) against continuous per-test pass-rate targets — the single-GPU adaptation of DCPO's decoupled recipe [3], extended from one scalar to a per-requirement vector.

Be honest about why the *structured* version is the headline and not the scalar one: DCPO (ICML 2026) already trains a decoded scalar confidence block on code to AUROC 0.796–0.914 [3], so the scalar expressed-confidence-for-code cell is occupied. Pursuing it would be "DCPO-lite on LoRA" — the incremental trap you explicitly want to avoid. The residual novelty is code-unique: because execution yields a *vector* of per-test outcomes while question-answering (QA) and math yield a single correctness bit, there is a fine-grained confidence target that scalar methods physically cannot express and whose ground-truth labels are already computed. That is the empty cell, defended by a four-axis novelty wedge (self-expressed × per-test-case × execution-oracle × code) that every near-neighbor misses on exactly one axis.

The paper is architected as three risk-stratified tiers built from one method. A **completeness floor** — a risk-controlled selective-generation curve on the untouched internal sampling signal (AUROC 0.987 [1], a signal that itself collapses to confident-but-wrong on a coverage-dependent fraction of ambiguous items, so the floor's guarantee is completeness, not an unconditional error bound [37]) — keeps the paper complete even if the novel tier only partially lands. A **high-confidence middle** — a scalar confidence replication positioned explicitly against DCPO — supplies a near-certain result. The **novel upside** — the per-test failure-localization result — is where Priority-1 novelty lives, hedged by a pre-registered demotion path. The primary AAAI-27 topic is **ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification** [33]; the contribution is a training method, which is what the main track rewards.

One thing must be said before anything else. **The AAAI-27 clock is the binding constraint** — a ~10-day window (dates and calendar in §8E) [33]. The recommendation is therefore pilot-gated: run a Day-2/3 collapse-check pilot; if the structured objective breaks the collapse, submit the per-test headline to AAAI-27; if it does not, submit the floor-plus-scalar paper; and if even the scalar training is shaky, take the *identical, venue-agnostic contribution* to ICLR-27.

## 1. The Research Frontier in Code-LLM Confidence and Calibration

### A. Two channels: the internal signal is near-perfect, the expressed signal is broken

The defining empirical fact of this subfield in 2026 is a split between two channels. When a code LLM is *sampled repeatedly* and its confidence is read off the empirical pass rate over completions, discrimination is extraordinary: the diagnostic study that motivates this proposal reports **AUROC 0.987** on HumanEval [1], and it is no outlier. Functional Entropy reaches AUROC up to 0.87 on Python once equivalence is judged functionally rather than by textual entailment [18]; ConSelf's execution-behavior semantic entropy hits AUC 0.804 on Qwen2.5-Coder [17]; Sharma & David restore near-oracle abstention through symbolic-execution clustering on LiveCodeBench [11]. The internal channel works because code has a deterministic oracle — execution — that natural-language tasks lack.

The *expressed* channel — anything a user could read off a single generation — fails. Token log-probability discriminates at only AUROC 0.668 in the base study [1], and Spiess et al. showed the same across three tasks and five datasets: completions above 90% average per-token confidence pass tests **only 52% of the time** [2]. Verbalized 1–5 self-assessment collapses to AUROC 0.519–0.564, with 67% of problems assigned maximum confidence [1]; ConSelf independently finds token entropy "barely surpasses random guessing" at the problem level [17]. The gap is structural, not a tuning artifact.

Miao & Ungar give the mechanistic reason: across three models and four datasets, linear-probe and contrastive-activation analysis shows the internal "calibration" direction (which tracks true correctness) and the "verbalized-confidence" direction are encoded linearly but *orthogonally* — the model holds an accurate internal estimate its own voice does not read out — and chain-of-thought reasoning rotates the verbalized direction further off ("reasoning contamination") [34]. An untrained model literally cannot speak its own calibrated estimate, so decoupling the confidence optimization from the reasoning policy is the mechanistically-indicated fix, not merely an empirical hope. The community has spent its effort making the *internal* signal better and almost none making the *expressed* signal trustworthy — and that asymmetry is the opportunity: the open frontier is **communication, not detection**.

The instinctive fix — rescale the bad signal — provably fails on the axis that matters. In the base study, isotonic and Platt scaling (two standard post-hoc recalibration maps) *improve* Expected Calibration Error (ECE — the average gap between stated confidence and empirical accuracy) while *degrading* AUROC (isotonic 0.668→0.587; Platt →0.386) [1]. Spiess names the mechanism: "bucket collapse," where Platt scaling squashes a poorly-aligned signal into a single bucket at the base correctness rate, producing deceptively low ECE while a skill-score reveals zero predictive utility [2]. Roelofs et al. compound the warning: ECE computed with fixed-width binning is a biased estimator whose apparent gains can be pure artifact [16]. The field is converging on a methodological principle — **report discrimination (AUROC), not just calibration (ECE), because a near-constant predictor games ECE** — and that principle is itself a positioning wedge.

### B. Train-time verbalized calibration works — everywhere except code

The most important cluster for a *prescriptive* paper is the train-time verbalized-confidence line, because it proves the expressed channel is fixable by training rather than rescaling. Rewarding Doubt trains a verbalized confidence token via a log-scoring-rule reinforcement-learning (RL) reward on Llama-3-8B with 4-bit LoRA on a single A40 GPU, reaching TriviaQA AUROC 0.859 — and explicitly beats a lower-ECE trained probe on discrimination, arguing that ECE alone "can hide a model that assigns moderate confidence everywhere" [4]. ConfTuner's tokenized Brier score is a proper scoring rule at the token level, needs zero extra sampling, converges at ~2,000 examples on 7–8B models in minutes, and delivers up to +14.4% relative AUROC [5]. RLCR proves any bounded proper scoring rule yields simultaneously accurate-and-calibrated models, and warns via a Toy-Arm experiment that an *unbounded* log-score reward-hacks into abstention collapse — boundedness is mandatory [6]. CAPO proves GRPO's (Group Relative Policy Optimization's) implicit advantage is an AUC-*inconsistent* surrogate and supplies a consistent replacement, formalizing why accuracy-only RL degrades relative calibration [7].

Every one of these — Rewarding Doubt, ConfTuner, RLCR, CAPO, ORCE [22] — is QA or math. None touches code. The mechanism is proven; the domain transfer is not. That is the frontier's single largest structural gap, and code is precisely the domain where the training labels are cheapest and most reliable.

### C. DCPO: the one method that crossed into code, and its scalar ceiling

DCPO is the pivot point of the entire analysis [3]. It is the only method in the corpus with direct code-domain AUROC evidence for a *trained, decoded* confidence signal: a `<conf>` block trained via masked-gradient decoupled GRPO against a hybrid instance-plus-group execution-pass-rate target, reaching AUROC 0.796 (LiveCodeBench v5), 0.845 (v6), and 0.914 (HumanEval+) on Qwen3-8B, beating the joint-reward RLCR baseline on both AUROC and accuracy. Its Proposition 4.2 formally proves the gradient conflict — that for an overconfident model with positively-correlated confidence features, the accuracy and calibration gradients are strictly anti-aligned — which names exactly the mechanism behind the base study's SFT collapse [1][3]. DCPO substantially settles the *scalar* expressed-confidence-for-code question.

Its structural limitation is the opening: **DCPO emits one number for the whole solution.** It cannot tell you *which* requirement will fail. Neither can Rewarding Doubt, RLCR, or ConfTuner — they are scalar-per-answer by construction, forced by a QA/math substrate where correctness is a single bit [3][4][5][6]. That residual is code-unique, and it is where the paper must live. Read as a whole, the frontier says: the internal signal is solved, the expressed-scalar-for-code cell is freshly occupied by DCPO, the mechanism is cheap and portable, and the deployment literature is quietly skeptical that a numeric verbalized confidence is even what practitioners want [26].

## 2. Caveats, Holes, and Open Problems at the Frontier

### A. The scalar cell is occupied; the structured cell is empty

The most consequential caveat is one only a full-text reading of DCPO surfaces: the naive headline — "train a code LLM to verbalize a calibrated, discriminative *scalar* confidence, measured by AUROC" — is no longer novel [3]. But DCPO's own architecture reveals the hole. Because code execution yields a *vector* of per-test outcomes, there exists a fine-grained confidence target that scalar methods physically cannot express and whose free ground-truth labels — the per-test pass rates already computed for the 0.987 oracle — are sitting unused [1]. No published work trains a code LLM to self-report per-test-case or per-requirement confidence and evaluates it by discrimination. This is the empty cell, and it is the novelty payload.

### B. The near-misses fail systematically, one axis each

The frontier's holes are systematic, and the discipline is to keep only the holes a reviewer cannot close in one sentence. Post-hoc code-calibration work (Multicalibration for code [28], fine-grained code-revision calibration [38]) measures ECE and skill-score but never AUROC, so it *cannot* demonstrate discrimination — a real gap and a positioning wedge, but not by itself a headline, since Spiess and CAPO already argue ECE is gameable [2][7]. Train-time verbalized work measures AUROC but only on QA/math [4][5][6][7][22]. Code-native execution work either improves the *internal* signal without ever expressing it [11][17][18] or produces a signal from a *separate* model rather than the coder's self-report [10][27]. Structured-confidence work exists only outside code, verified by noisy fact-checkers [8][23]. **No single work combines self-expression, per-test granularity, execution ground truth, and the code domain** — and each near-miss fails on exactly one axis, which is what makes the wedge defensible rather than lucky.

Two hazards must be pre-empted or they become fatal reviewer objections. First, ECE is gameable — the base study's own collapse (mean confidence 0.900 everywhere, low ECE, no discrimination) is the proof [1], reinforced by CAPO's formal argument and Roelofs's bias result [7][16]; the paper must make per-test AUROC primary and report debiased/adaptive ECE secondarily.

Second, HumanEval's near-perfect oracle risks being a contamination artifact; Riddell et al. show HumanEval/MBPP carry both surface and semantic contamination, with models scoring higher on contaminated subsets [15]. Primary evaluation must run on LiveCodeBench (problems time-windowed to post-training-cutoff dates) with HumanEval as legacy comparison only [14]; ConSelf's replication of the disconnect on LiveCodeBench is direct evidence the finding survives decontamination [17]. Both hazards convert into robustness pillars if handled explicitly.

## 3. Where the Current Calibration-Disconnect Paper Falls Short of Main-Track

The existing manuscript is a strong diagnostic, and diagnostics do not win AAAI main track. Its three shortfalls are precisely what the extension repairs.

**It is descriptive, not prescriptive.** The finding — "no expressed interface communicates the internal signal" [1] — is a negative result. Main-track methodology rewards a *positive* mechanism that closes the gap, and AAAI's routing rules push measurement/application studies toward IAAI or AI-for-Social-Impact [33]. The contribution must be a training method.

**Its one intervention failed in the most instructive way.** The 274-example LoRA supervised fine-tuning (SFT) cut verbal ECE by 64% but produced "calibration without discrimination" — mean confidence 0.900 on both easy and hard problems [1]. This is a diagnosis, not a dead end: DCPO's Proposition 4.2 explains it as a gradient conflict from a joint objective [3], and the coarse 1–5 bins and 274 examples explain the rest. The extension replaces coarse joint SFT with a decoupled, continuous-target, proper-scoring objective — turning the failed intervention into the ablation that motivates the method.

**It is scoped to a scalar interface the field has since occupied.** Even a successful scalar result would now be measured against DCPO [3], and two models on one contamination-exposed benchmark (HumanEval, 164 problems, skewed easy) cannot carry a confident headline. The only way up is to change *what is expressed*, not merely to express it better — and the structured per-test interface is that change, native to code in a way no competing domain can replicate. Put plainly: the current paper is a strong *motivation section*, not a paper. It supplies the 0.987 oracle, the free per-test labels, the documented collapse to explain, and the deployment framing — but on its own it is workshop-grade.

## 4. Candidate Directions, Scored on Novelty and Confidence

Four directions were weighed on the binding priority order, scored 1–5 on each axis, with deliberately harsh risk-first commentary.

| Direction | Novelty | Confidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Scalar expressed code confidence (DCPO-lite on LoRA)** | 2 | 5 | Reject as headline; keep as baseline — occupied by DCPO [3] |
| **2. Structured per-test / per-requirement expressed confidence** | 5 | 3 | **Commit** — empty four-axis cell, free labels, comparable cost |
| **3. Risk-controlled selective generation on the internal signal** | 2.5 | 5 | Demote to guaranteed floor — occupied by CIC [12], CODEREFUSER [13], Sharma & David [11] |
| **4. Complexity-routed estimator (self-report vs. judge vs. probe)** | 3.5 | 2.5 | Reject — needs a repo-level benchmark beyond single-GPU budget [27] |

**Direction 1** would almost certainly hit AUROC 0.80–0.90 on LoRA single-GPU [3][4], but DCPO already published this exact result on code: high confidence, low novelty, fails Priority 1. **Direction 3** is nearly guaranteed to land 70–90% coverage at low error, but CIC, CODEREFUSER, and Sharma & David already occupy the space, and a UT-Dallas paper titled "Beyond Execution: Uncertainty Estimation and Abstention in LLM-Based Code Generation" is under review on this cell [29] — not "extremely novel," so it becomes the fallback floor. **Direction 4** — AutoProbe's judge-vs-probe reversal (external judge 0.94 on simple code, internal probe 0.82 on repo-level) — is real but asymmetric, concerns third-party assessment not self-report, and requires an out-of-budget repo-level benchmark [27]; it survives as a one-paragraph robustness ablation.

**Direction 2** is the only cell that is simultaneously novel (Priority 1) and de-riskable with a guaranteed Direction-3 floor (Priority 2). The decisive move is that the composed paper scores ≥4 on *both* axes only when Directions 3 and 1 sit *underneath* Direction 2 as guaranteed and DCPO-backed tiers — so the paper's survival and the paper's novelty live in different tiers. That composition is the one paper.

## 5. The One Paper: Committed Recommendation

**Title (working):** *"Which Test Will I Fail? Teaching Code LLMs to Localize Their Own Errors with Calibrated Per-Requirement Confidence."*

**The committed thesis.** A code LLM can be trained to express calibrated, discriminative confidence at per-test-case / per-requirement granularity — to point at the specific requirement its solution will violate — a capability enabled by code's per-test execution verifiability and structurally impossible for the scalar methods (DCPO [3], RLCR [6], Rewarding Doubt [4]) that define the current frontier. Call the method **Decoupled Per-Requirement Confidence (DPRC)**.

**The mechanism, committed.** Take Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct. The solution is generated first in a *frozen* forward pass; then a second, confidence-only turn — the same model, LoRA-adapted, conditioned on the now-fixed generated code — emits a structured confidence block: for each requirement/test-group $g$, a verbalized confidence token $c_g \in [0,100]$ predicting the probability its solution passes that group. This stage-decoupling (ORCE-style [22]) keeps the coder's own expressed voice while making the masked-gradient isolation coherent — the confidence turn's gradients physically cannot reach the frozen generation policy. Warm-start the block by SFT with a ConfTuner-style tokenized-Brier loss against the *continuous* per-group pass rate [5]. Then run a short refinement with a **decoupled masked-gradient objective** — DCPO's recipe [3] — so the confidence-token advantage never backpropagates into the generation policy, mechanically preventing the Proposition 4.2 gradient conflict that produced the base study's collapse [1][3].

The reward per confidence token is a **bounded Brier proper scoring rule** — concretely DCPO's confidence reward $R_c = -|c_g - R_{IG}|$, a bounded absolute-error term against a $\lambda$-interpolated instance-plus-group target $R_{IG} = \lambda\,\tilde{R}_G + (1-\lambda)\,R(o_r)$ (never unbounded log-score, per RLCR's collapse warning [6]) — with the per-group confidence advantage $A_c$ masked so it touches only the confidence tokens while the reasoning tokens carry the binary-correctness advantage $A_r$ [3][6]. Everything is LoRA rank 16, single-GPU. Yaldiz et al. [19] establish a feasibility floor for LoRA-scale decoupling *in general*, but their mechanism is a single final-decision-token position decoupling inside an otherwise joint loss — structurally distinct from DCPO's advantage-masking of a full confidence block; no source evaluates block-masked-gradient decoupling under LoRA on code, which is the method's single largest evidence gap and exactly what the Day-2/3 pilot resolves (§7).

**Why this exact design.** Three convergent findings force it. First, self-report fails only when *untrained or naively joint-SFT'd* and succeeds under proper-scoring training — so keep the coder's own voice but decouple the *optimization* [4][5][22]. Choosing self-report over a hidden-state probe is a deliberate Priority-1 (novelty) bet carried at a real Priority-2 cost: an external probe is the lower-risk, likely-higher-AUROC route (external probes drive the strongest localized results [9], while same-model self-judgment can be catastrophic at AUROC 0.27–0.73 [27]), so a per-group probe on the confidence turn's hidden states is held as a pre-registered fallback *interface* if the decoded self-report under-discriminates — not only ORCE-style separate weights [22][27]. Second, DCPO's decoupling is the only code-validated recipe with a formal anti-collapse guarantee — not merely Proposition 4.2 naming the gradient conflict but Theorem 5.1 proving the decoupled estimator is *statistically consistent* ($\mathbb{E}[c \mid q] = \mathbb{E}_y[R(y)]$, the confidence converging to the true conditional pass probability) precisely because it is optimized independently of the reasoning policy — and continuous instance-level targets (not coarse tiers) supply the discrimination gradient — DCPO's own ablation loses 0.081 ECE when instance labels are removed [3].

Third, ConfTuner's tokenized-Brier loss on the existing pass-rate labels is single-GPU cheap and converges at 2,000 examples [5]. DPRC is the intersection: DCPO's architecture, ConfTuner's loss, the base study's free labels, extended from a scalar to a per-requirement vector.

**Requirement granularity, committed.** "Per-requirement" is operationalized as per-test-*group*, where tests are clustered by the requirement they exercise (public examples, edge cases, hidden functional groups) — not per-individual-hidden-test — keeping the target dimension modest (typically 3–6 groups) and the labels dense. On dense benchmarks (TACO ~50–200 tests/problem [20]; LiveCodeBench ~17 [14]) the grouping is data-driven; on sparse ones (HumanEval ~7 [14]) it degrades gracefully to public-vs-hidden. The numeric $c_g \in [0,100]$ is a *training* target, not necessarily the user-facing readout: the strongest deployment evidence finds developers prefer non-numerical, color-coded reliability cues over numeric confidence [26], and a per-requirement confidence vector maps directly onto a categorical per-requirement flag (green/amber/red per requirement), so the headline's numeric interface survives that preference rather than contradicting it.

## 6. Headline Claim and the Exact Experiment That Produces It

### A. The single headline number

> **A 7B code LLM trained with DPRC self-reports which requirement it will fail at a target per-requirement AUROC of ~0.75–0.85 (pilot-set; plausibly ~0.65–0.85) on contamination-controlled LiveCodeBench — versus ~0.52–0.56 for prompted per-requirement self-report and with no prior method able to produce the number at all — the first demonstration that a code model can localize its own functional failures in its own voice.**

The comparison anchor is deliberate: there is *no incumbent per-test AUROC* to beat, so the paper wins on the novelty of the capability, not on out-scoring DCPO's scalar number. The untrained baseline (~0.52–0.56) is the base study's own verbalized failure [1], replicated at the per-requirement level; the trained result is the contribution. The exact band is fixed by the Day-2/3 pilot (§8), which is the honest posture given no prior number exists. The upper end (~0.85) is optimistic: the closest structured self-report precedent, CURE, reached only 0.667 [8], and §8B rates a 0.65–0.72 landing the single largest risk — so ~0.65–0.85 is the honest span the pilot must resolve, not a floor of 0.75.

### B. The exact experiment

- **Data.** Assemble 2,000–4,000 problems from MBPP, a ≥3-test-case-filtered APPS/TACO subset (density guarantees per-test label spread), and a LiveCodeBench training window; hold out HumanEval (legacy) and a disjoint LiveCodeBench window (primary, contamination-controlled) [14][15][20]. Cluster each problem's tests into requirement groups. TACO (~50–200 tests/problem [20]) and CodeContests+ (~25–98 tests, test-suite noise repaired [21]) are the density workhorses.
- **Free labels.** For each problem, sample $n=10$ solutions from Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct and record the per-test pass matrix. The per-group pass rate is the continuous confidence target; the per-solution pass rate is the scalar target. This reuses the exact computation that produced 0.987 — no new labeling design, only more test suites executed against the same completions [1]. Per-group labels do inherit the test suite's own validity limits — flaky or nondeterministic tests and weak LLM-generated suites inject noise into the pass-rate targets, and label quality is bottlenecked by the verifier's own strength, not merely test count [44] — so the pool controls this directly: deterministic-execution sandboxing, ≥3-test-case filtering, discarding nondeterministic tests, and test-suite-repaired sources like CodeContests+ [21], treating label validity, not just density, as a first-class control.
- **Train DPRC.** Warm-start tokenized-Brier SFT on the confidence block, then masked-gradient decoupled refinement (bounded Brier confidence advantage, binary correctness reasoning advantage), LoRA rank 16 on one A100-40GB/GH200 [3][5][19].
- **Headline metric.** Per-requirement AUROC: pool all (problem, group) pairs, label = did the solution pass that group, score = self-reported $c_g$. Report bootstrap 95% CIs resampling problems (not test-cases) to respect the dependence structure. Secondary: localization precision@1 (is the model's least-confident group the one that fails?).
- **Baselines on the same axis.** Prompted per-group self-report (untrained); coarse joint-SFT (the base study's failed recipe, replicated to exhibit the collapse [1]); per-group log-prob; per-group AST/consistency proxy.

## 7. Why the Headline Will Likely Land: Feasibility on Single-GPU 7B Compute

### A. The mechanism is proven three times over, just never combined

The confidence in Priority 2 rests on borrowed evidence. DCPO already got a *decoded, trained* confidence signal on code with an execution-pass-rate target to the 0.80–0.91 band — the scalar special case of DPRC — so the reward shape and target are validated in-domain [3]. Rewarding Doubt got AUROC 0.859 with a genuinely verbalized token under LoRA on a *single A40* [4], and ConfTuner got +14.4% AUROC with a proper-scoring loss on 7–8B models converging at 2,000 examples [5] — so the training mechanism is single-GPU feasible.

CURE got the *structured, per-unit, self-reported, anti-collapse* result at AUROC 0.667 on a **noisier** oracle (VeriScore, a retrieval fact-checker) in a **harder** domain (long-form factuality) [8] — so the structured extension is demonstrably trainable, and a deterministic execution oracle should beat 0.667, not fall below it. And there is a *code-native* precedent that a constrained self-report already discriminates well: Functional Entropy's structured P(True) readout reaches AUROC 0.82–0.87 on Python/SQL against the same model's free-text verbalized confidence at only 0.54–0.80 [18] — direct evidence that the verbal collapse is an artifact of the free-text/Likert elicitation format, not of the self-referential act itself, which is exactly what a tokenized per-group readout is designed to escape. Every ingredient has a positive precedent; the paper is an assembly, not a gamble.

### B. Compute fits with wide margin

Label generation reuses the existing $n=10$ sampling (~1.7–6.7 GPU-hours for a 2,000–4,000-problem pool); LoRA warm-start training is minutes-to-under-an-hour (ConfTuner's ~4 minutes at 2,000 examples; Raschka's 1.85h/50k extrapolation puts a few-thousand-example run well under an hour [5][35]); one clean end-to-end pass — labels, training, eval, ablations, baselines — is ~15–25 GPU-hours, ~45–75 with a 3× debugging buffer [19][25]. ConSelf is the exact-hardware precedent: single-GPU LoRA on the identical model family (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B), and it showed curated subsets beat unfiltered ones, so more data is not needed [17]. Yaldiz et al. show LoRA rank-16 decoupling survives at ~500 GPU-hours across three model sizes (~150/model) [19]; throughput is no bottleneck (vLLM aggregate throughput on 7–8B A100-class GPUs; single-stream ~77 tok/s is the pessimistic floor [36]). The named risk is *wall-clock*, not GPU-hours: 40,000–800,000 sandboxed test executions need a harness stood up on Day 1.

### C. The expected number, calibrated honestly

DCPO's scalar band and the internal-signal ceiling (0.987) bracket the plausible per-requirement result [1][3], but the tighter, more honest bound is the single-pass informational ceiling: because the expressed signal reads off a *single* generation, AutoProbe's internal one-pass code-correctness probe (~0.63–0.71 on independent-unit functionality, up to ~0.77 with richer structure [27]) marks roughly where a cheap single-pass signal saturates — well below the 0.987 sampling oracle. Per-requirement discrimination is also a harder target than scalar (fewer effective samples per group, higher label noise per cell), so a realistic landing is AUROC ~0.75–0.85, with 0.65–0.72 a genuinely plausible lower outcome (§8B) — below scalar DCPO and only modestly above CURE's 0.667, since execution is a cleaner oracle than fact-checking but a single decoded pass is a real constraint [3][8][27]. The band is optimistic-but-defensible because training plus execution-grounded per-group labels are what would push the expressed signal toward the top of that single-pass regime — which the pilot resolves. Crucially, *any* number in that band is a first. The single biggest unvalidated leap is whether masked-gradient block-decoupling survives *LoRA rank 16* on code — DCPO demonstrated it under full fine-tuning [3] — and that is exactly what the pilot resolves. If LoRA capacity is too low to hold two structurally different objectives in one adapter, the fallback is ORCE-style fully-separate confidence weights at higher inference cost [22].

## 8. Supporting Results, Failure Mode, and Pre-Registered Fallback

### A. Supporting results that make the headline defensible

(1) **Scalar replication** reproducing DCPO's band on LoRA single-GPU, proving the recipe before extending it [3]. (2) **The collapse-and-repair ablation:** the base study's coarse joint-SFT re-run to exhibit the mean-0.900 collapse, then DPRC's decoupled continuous-target objective repairing it — a direct mechanistic before/after [1][3]. (3) **Decoupling ablation** (DPRC vs. bounded joint reward à la Rewarding Doubt) isolating whether decoupling is necessary or merely safer [4]; DCPO's own decoupling ablation is prior evidence it favors decoupling — removing it drops accuracy 60.8→57.3 and roughly doubles ECE (0.128→0.258) [3]. (4) **Continuous-vs-coarse-tier ablation** pinning the collapse on coarse labels, not self-report per se [1]. (5) **Sample-count sweep** ($n \in \{5,10,20,50,100\}$) showing code's execution signal saturates near $n=10$, a cheap panel rebutting "just use Uncertainty Distillation's $n=100$" [25]. (6) **Contamination control:** LiveCodeBench primary, HumanEval legacy [14][15]. Each rebuts a specific reviewer objection and is single-GPU-cheap.

### B. The most likely failure mode, named precisely

The single largest risk is that DPRC *under-discriminates* — per-test AUROC lands ~0.65–0.72, real signal but not a clean headline — because per-test correctness within one solution is *correlated* (a broken function fails many tests together), so per-test labels carry less independent information than the per-solution label, and the model may learn a near-constant "this solution is X% good" confidence smeared across tests rather than genuine localization [1][3]. A secondary failure mode is CIC-style infeasibility of the α=0.05 floor at small calibration-set size [12].

### C. The pre-registered fallback (the guaranteed floor)

If the structured signal underperforms, the paper pivots its headline to a **risk-controlled selective-generation result wrapped on the untouched internal sampling signal — never on the collapsed verbal output**, which lacks the score spread any threshold search needs [11][12]. Concretely: a Clopper-Pearson upper-confidence-bound policy (an exact binomial risk bound) at a pre-registered primary **α = 0.10**, with **α = 0.05 as a stretch goal** and calibration-pool expansion (HumanEval+MBPP, ~400+ problems, inference-only) as the fallback-to-the-fallback if α=0.05 returns infeasible on a large fraction of splits — CIC returns no valid threshold on 34–100 of 100 calibration splits for weaker models at α=0.05, exactly this documented failure mode [12]. The target is 70–90% coverage at ≤2–5% accepted-output error, anchored to Sharma & David's 77.8–88% coverage at ≤0.02% false-positive rate on LiveCodeBench and CODEREFUSER's ~3.5× coverage over a perplexity baseline at matched risk [11][13]. Because the 0.987 signal is a stronger discriminator than anything inside CIC's own tests, this floor should land at or above the Sharma & David ceiling [1][11][12].

That guarantee is coverage-conditional, not unconditional: the internal sampling signal itself can silently collapse to confident-but-wrong on ambiguous or underspecified problems — ~3% of HumanEval, 10–16% of MBPP, and 18–32% of LiveCodeBench items, rising up to 5.5× under injected ambiguity [37] — so the floor's coverage/error bound holds only once such items are filtered or flagged, which the selective-generation abstention partly does by declining low-agreement inputs but does not fully neutralize. It is publishable-grade insurance — reframed as "decoupling the *action* problem (solvable) from the *communication* problem (still hard)," aligned with the in-IDE finding that developers prefer categorical/color-coded cues over numeric confidence [26] — but its own novelty is modest, so it is the floor, never the headline.

### D. The de-scope ladder, pre-registered with numeric gates

| Level | Headline | Gate to earn it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 (bold) | Per-**test-case** confidence, AUROC 0.75–0.85 | Day-3 pilot per-test AUROC > 0.65 on ~300 problems | Target |
| L2 (safe-bold) | Per-**category** confidence (grouped slots) + localization precision@1 | L1 fails but per-category AUROC > 0.65 | Fallback-1 |
| L3 (floor) | Risk-controlled selective generation, 70–90% cov @ ≤2–5% err | Both above fail; wrapper on internal signal | Guaranteed |

Every level is publishable, and L3 is guaranteed by off-the-shelf mechanisms on a signal known to be strong [11][12][13]. This is the structural reason the paper cannot come out *incomplete*: the novel tier can miss entirely and a complete AAAI-27 submission still remains. Completeness is not main-track acceptance, though — L3's own novelty is modest (§4 scores it 2.5; the cell is occupied by CIC [12], CODEREFUSER [13], and Sharma & David [11], with a UT-Dallas paper under review [29]), so demoting all the way to L3 could still miss the AAAI novelty bar.

### E. The 10-day calendar, the venue pivot, and the strategic outlook

> **TIMELINE — decision-critical.** Today is 18 July 2026. AAAI-27 abstracts close **21 July**, full papers **28 July**, code **31 July** [33] — a ~10-day window. The entire contribution is *venue-agnostic*: if the pilot is not at least amber by 20 July, do **not** force the AAAI submission — take the identical paper to ICLR-27 (September), where six extra weeks convert a coin-flip into a safe bet. A rushed L1 result at a mediocre number is worse than a clean L1+L2+L3 paper six weeks later.

- **Day 1 — timing pilot.** Stand up the sandbox execution harness; time 50 completions plus a full test-suite batch on the target GPU; finalize the problem pool. *Gate: if 40k–800k executions exceed ~2 days wall-clock even parallelized, de-scope the pool now.*
- **Days 2–3 — collapse-check pilot.** ~300-problem warm-start SFT at per-test granularity; read per-test AUROC. *Gate: >0.65 → proceed L1; ≤0.65 → try per-category (L2); both ≤0.65 → commit to L3.*
- **Days 4–6 — full run.** Full-pool warm-start SFT; eval on held-out LiveCodeBench + HumanEval + TACO; bootstrap CIs across ≥3 seeds.
- **Days 7–8 — ablations + fallback.** Joint-vs-decoupled, continuous-vs-tier, N-sweep, self-report-vs-head; the risk-coverage curve (runs regardless of L-level).
- **Days 9–10 — write + reproducibility checklist + code freeze.** Code and data must be *submitted*, not promised — AAAI treats "released after acceptance" as no evidence of reproducibility [33].

Looking past this cycle, the strategic bet is that *structured, self-expressed* confidence is the next durable frontier: as code models move into agentic, multi-file, and IDE-embedded workflows where a scalar "I'm 80% sure" is unusable but "I'm unsure about the empty-input case" is actionable, a model that localizes its own failures in its own voice becomes the interface deployment actually needs.

Deployment currently lacks exactly this: today's shipped assistants expose no graded confidence at all (Copilot at ZoomInfo — 400+ developers, 33% acceptance, zero confidence signal surfaced [41]), and human-trust studies show a confident delivery *tone* suppresses developers' ability to catch reasoning errors while sustaining reliance [42], so a per-requirement, failure-localizing, categorically-surfaceable signal is precisely the trust-calibrating interface the deployment loop is missing. DCPO closed the scalar question in one cycle; the per-test question is likely to be closed within the next two, which is precisely why moving now — at either venue — matters. The pre-registered levels, α-thresholds, AUROC-primary metric, LiveCodeBench-primary evaluation, requirement-grouping rule, and fallback triggers are all fixed *before* the runs, satisfying AAAI-27's mandatory reproducibility checklist [33].

## 9. Novelty Defense Against the Closest Prior Work

The wedge is the **conjunction of four axes**, and every near-miss fails exactly one (all full-text verified). The one-sentence defense: *no prior work has a code LLM express its own confidence, at per-test-case / per-requirement granularity, trained against the code's own deterministic execution oracle, evaluated by discrimination.*

**Axis 1 — self-expressed, not an external probe or separate model.** FLARE localizes bugs at line level via a *separate diagnostic transformer* trained from scratch, and reports Top-k localization accuracy — never AUROC or any calibration metric [10]. Gros & Devanbu's "Localized Calibrated Uncertainty in Code LMs" — the closest *title* and sharpest threat — assigns calibrated per-line edit probability via an *external white-box probe* on internals, not the model's own voice, against a curated Minimal-Intent-Aligning-Patches dataset rather than free labels [9]. AutoProbe is likewise a probe, its in-house LLM-as-judge catastrophic at 0.27–0.73 [27]; RLCM's confidence is a decoupled hidden-state probe whose gradients never enter the policy [31]. The single-pass distillation line — Semantic Entropy Probes [39] and Dist2ill [40] — is the sharpest version of this contrast: both distill sampling-quality uncertainty into a near-zero-overhead *single* forward pass, but each yields a *scalar internal probe* signal, neither self-expressed, per-requirement, nor execution-grounded on code; that they obtain a cheap one-pass signal at all corroborates that a cheap expressed signal is attainable, and that they do it through a probe rather than the model's voice is exactly the axis DPRC claims. DPRC is the model's own decoded output.

**Axis 2 — per-test-case / per-requirement, not scalar, not per-line, not per-NL-claim.** DCPO emits one scalar per solution; RLCR, Rewarding Doubt, ConfTuner, CAPO, ORCE all emit one scalar per answer [3][4][5][6][7][22]. Gros & Devanbu and FLARE localize *spatially* (which line), a fundamentally different structuring than *functionally* (which requirement) [9][10] — and spatial per-line localization is not just a different axis but a hard, already-benchmarked one (Collu-Bench: 13,234 line-labeled instances, baseline localization only 22–33% [43]), so the functional per-test axis is unoccupied, not merely un-benchmarked. CURE and Atomic Calibration decompose into *NL claims* [8][23]. The per-test-case functional axis is unoccupied.

The sharpest reviewer objection — "this is DCPO with a vector output" — is answered not by the interface but by the empirical difficulty it hides: N correlated per-test targets do not decouple into N independent scalars, and the central risk (§8B) is precisely that the model smears a near-constant per-solution confidence across tests instead of *localizing*; preserving genuine per-group discrimination under correlated targets and grouped labels is the non-trivial contribution, not the extra tokens [3].

**Axis 3 — trained against the code's own execution oracle, not a noisy verifier.** CURE's labels come from VeriScore, capping it at AUROC 0.667 [8]; Atomic Calibration uses an external NLI/LLM fact-checker [23]; Gros & Devanbu build a curated patch dataset [9]. DPRC's oracle is deterministic per-test execution — free, exact, and the very reason code's internal signal reaches 0.987 [1]. The semantic-equivalence literature confirms this is code's structural advantage: generic NLI/embedding proxies collapse on code (the base study's AST proxy reaches only 0.689), and only execution restores signal [1][11].

**Axis 4 — code, not QA/math.** Rewarding Doubt, RLCR, ConfTuner, CAPO, ORCE, Uncertainty Distillation, CURE, Atomic Calibration — all QA or math [4][5][6][7][8][22][23][25]. VeRPO is the sharpest same-domain distractor: it turns per-test partial success into a *correctness reward*, never a confidence output, confirming the "free per-test labels for *confidence*" cell is unoccupied [24]. Two named comparators complete the defense in this section rather than only in §2: Campos et al.'s multicalibration for code [28] and the fine-grained code-revision line [38] optimize ECE/skill-score, never AUROC, so they cannot demonstrate discrimination; and UQ4CT is a code-domain uncertainty method built on chain-of-thought uncertainty, not a self-expressed per-test-case confidence readout — a distinct CoT-uncertainty axis that misses the four-axis wedge exactly as the other near-misses do.

**The honest scoop caveat.** Two under-review UT-Dallas papers (Rathnasuriya & Yang: "Beyond Confidence…", "Beyond Execution…") have on-topic titles; their public ICSE-NIER short paper is *scalar* per-prediction abstention, partial and distinct from the per-test self-expressed axis [29]. But "Beyond Execution" by its title targets execution-based uncertainty/abstention for code generation and could occupy an *adjacent* execution-grounded cell — a substantive overlap risk, not merely an unknown, and the group works on the same 7B model family and has independently reproduced the post-hoc ranking-failure finding [29]. Mitigation: move fast and frame the four-axis conjunction sharply — but AAAI's contemporaneous-work policy (anything posted fewer than two months before 28 July need not be addressed) gives no protection if the paper surfaces publicly before submission [33], so the ICLR-27 pivot, not the policy, is the real hedge against being scooped.

One framing objection must also be answered head-on: a recent line (CSR [30]) argues verbalized-token confidence is unstable and the field should abandon it; the paper must *show* a verbalized interface matches probe-level discrimination on code, not assume it. Novelty confidence: high, with this one named caveat.

## 10. AAAI-27 Topic Alignment: Primary and Secondary Keywords

**Primary keyword (verbatim):** **ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification** [33]. There is no separate "ML: Calibration" keyword in the AAAI-27 list; calibration and discriminative uncertainty estimation fall squarely under this one. The paper's main contribution is a *training method for calibrated, discriminative uncertainty quantification* — a general ML/UQ advance whose novelty (structured, decoupled, proper-scoring confidence with a formal anti-collapse motivation) is evaluable by the UQ community. Under AAAI's own rule — file under the subarea of the main methodological contribution, and an ML method demonstrated on code goes under ML unless the contribution is fundamentally about code-specific structure [33] — this is the correct home. The method *exploits* code's execution verifiability, but the contribution is the UQ training objective, not a code-generation advance per se.

There is a genuine counter-argument to pre-empt: the per-test structure *is* code-specific, which under the same rule could push toward NLP: Code Generation / Program Synthesis. Keep ML-UQ primary, because the methodological core (decoupled masked-gradient training + proper-scoring calibration + risk-controlled selective generation) is domain-general and only *instantiated* on code; filing under NLP/code risks the paper being read as an application/measurement study and redirected toward IAAI/AI-for-Social-Impact, which the brief explicitly warns against [33].

**Secondary keywords:** **NLP: Code Generation / Program Synthesis** (the application substrate and source of the free per-test labels) and **ML: Evaluation, Benchmarking, Datasets & Analysis** (the AUROC-primary evaluation, contamination control, and debiased-ECE methodology) [33].

**One-line scope statement.** The paper's main contribution is a decoupled, execution-grounded training objective for calibrated per-requirement uncertainty quantification; it sits in ML: Bayesian Learning & Uncertainty Quantification and will not drift into a code-generation framing, because the headline is a UQ capability (failure localization by calibrated self-report), not a pass@k improvement. No developer user study is required — this is a methodological, AUROC-primary contribution, and a field study is deliberately out of single-GPU 7B scope; the in-IDE deployment finding [26] already supplies the motivation that a per-requirement interface is what practitioners want. **Format:** AAAI-27 allows 7 pages of technical content (9 max) plus reference/checklist-only pages; the one-headline, three-panel, five-ablation scope fits one tight story, and the reproducibility checklist and code must be submitted at submission time [33].

## 11. The Shortlist: 5-10 Load-Bearing Papers

1. **DCPO — Decoupling Reasoning and Confidence** [3]. *Contributes:* the decoupled masked-gradient objective, the Proposition 4.2 gradient-conflict proof, and the only code-domain trained-confidence AUROC (0.796–0.914). *Leaves open:* everything is scalar — one number per solution. **The method's parent and the scalar baseline to position against; owns the cell the paper must not claim.**
2. **CURE — Think-Through-Uncertainty** [8]. *Contributes:* the closest methodological analog — self-reported, per-claim, discriminative, AUROC-evaluated confidence with a three-stage anti-collapse pipeline. *Leaves open:* general long-form factuality with a noisy VeriScore oracle (AUROC 0.667), no code, no execution. **The must-distinguish paper; the argument is that execution beats fact-checking.**
3. **Rewarding Doubt** [4]. *Contributes:* proof that a genuinely verbalized confidence token, RL-trained on a single A40 LoRA, is *more discriminative* than a lower-ECE probe (AUROC 0.859). *Leaves open:* QA only, single scalar. **The single-GPU feasibility existence proof and the joint-vs-decoupled ablation.**
4. **The base "Calibration Disconnect" manuscript** [1]. *Contributes:* the 0.987 internal / 0.668 log-prob / 0.52–0.56 verbal disconnect, the post-hoc AUROC-degradation result, and the free per-test labels the method reuses. *Leaves open:* it is diagnostic and its one intervention collapsed. **The foundation the paper extends.**
5. **Gros & Devanbu — Localized Calibrated Uncertainty in Code LMs** [9]. *Contributes:* the closest published title — calibrated *localized* code uncertainty. *Leaves open:* spatial (per-line) not functional (per-test), external probe not self-report, curated dataset not free labels. **The prominent cite-and-distinguish on the "localized" framing.**
6. **ConfTuner** [5]. *Contributes:* tokenized Brier proper-scoring loss, zero extra sampling, 2,000-example convergence on 7–8B. *Leaves open:* QA only, scalar. **The warm-start loss and the feasibility floor.**
7. **RLCR — Beyond Binary Rewards** [6]. *Contributes:* the bounded-proper-scoring-rule theorem and the Toy-Arm proof that unbounded log-score collapses. *Leaves open:* QA/math, scalar. **The reason the reward must be bounded Brier.**
8. **FLARE** [10]. *Contributes:* fine-grained (line-level) execution-guided code diagnosis. *Leaves open:* separate diagnostic model, line-level not per-test, never reports AUROC/calibration. **The execution-plus-fine-grained near-miss on the self-expression axis.**
9. **Sharma & David — Assessing Correctness via Uncertainty Estimation** [11]. *Contributes:* symbolic-execution abstention at 77.8–88% coverage, ≤0.02% FPR on LiveCodeBench. *Leaves open:* internal signal, not a trained expressed interface. **The fallback ceiling to benchmark.**
10. **Spiess et al. — Calibration and Correctness of LMs for Code** [2] (ICSE 2025). *Contributes:* the bucket-collapse critique and the ECE-is-insufficient lesson. *Leaves open:* older non-instruction-tuned models, no train-time method. **The anchor for the AUROC-over-ECE positioning.**
