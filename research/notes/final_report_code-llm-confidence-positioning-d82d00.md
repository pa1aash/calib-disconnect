# Confidence estimation for code language models against execution ground truth: occupancy, residuals, and the strongest TMLR contribution

**Executive summary.** The headline that a hidden-state probe knows more about code correctness than the model says is taken. Ribeiro, Spiess, Devanbu, and Nadi (ICSE 2026) show that a fitted probe beats likelihood and zero-shot verbal confidence at whole-solution granularity for four 7-8B instruct models, by +13.3 to +29.0 points on BigCodeBench and +8.7 to +26.2 points on HumanEval (Section 4.2.1) [[on-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]]. A paper that leads with that claim would incorrectly claim novelty, which TMLR's editorial policy names as grounds for rejection. What is not taken is the controlled form of the claim, and the evidence says the controlled form is the more interesting one. Three documented confounds plausibly inflate the uncontrolled margin: between-problem difficulty, a fitted probe set against zero-shot readouts, and a design that contrasts human reference solutions with other models' failures. Code comparisons that score a probe against the model's own confidence within problem on self-generated samples find ties under shift and on small samples, with one in-distribution exception, so the controlled margin in AUROC (area under the ROC curve: the probability that a correct sample is ranked above an incorrect one) looks small and model-dependent [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]] [[a-heckler-in-the-hidden-statecorrectness-signals-in-diffusion-language-models]]. A concurrent code paper, CREDO, now shows that training stated confidence on execution labels raises its AUROC from 0.690 to 0.850, though without a probe arm [[beyond-verbalized-confidence-calibrating-reasoners-with-differentiable-readouts]].

The strongest defensible TMLR contribution is therefore one execution-graded measurement paper. Its headline is a controlled test: a hidden-state probe against supervision-matched own readouts (including trained stated confidence), zero-shot verbal confidence, token probability, and per-candidate sampling agreement, with input-only baselines, scored pooled and within problem on the model's own samples. The test rests on an L1 sensitivity protocol (estimator set, original-versus-augmented test oracle, per-model release-date split) and carries an offline L3 routing replay as secondary analysis. Base-versus-instruct and reasoning decompositions become a conditional extension, run only if the controlled gap is measurably non-zero. F supplies vocabulary only; L4 and the legacy `src/v4_dprc` pipeline stay out. The design is publishable whichever way its headline falls, which is what TMLR's first criterion rewards.

Verdicts in brief:

- **L1:** OCCUPIED (union) as a comparison; NARROW as a protocol; test insufficiency (Q10) and contamination effects on calibration (Q13) CLEAN (provisional).
- **L2:** OCCUPIED as an uncontrolled headline; NARROW as the controlled test, the factorial decomposition, and trained stated confidence on code.
- **L3:** NARROW for routing by elicited confidence; CLEAN (provisional) for updating after a shown failing test; OCCUPIED for selective prediction and repository-agent uncertainty.
- **L4:** NARROW, with line-level localization OCCUPIED.
- **F:** OCCUPIED as a framing; its vocabulary, including metrology-proper terms, is used, not contested.

## 1. Scope, instruments, and verdict rules

### 1.1 What is judged, and under which rules

The question covers January 2022 to November 2026, with emphasis on 2025 and 2026, and asks which confidence-estimation contribution for code language models, graded by execution (pass or fail on test suites), remains open and defensible at TMLR. L1 is an execution-graded measurement foundation with release-date contamination control. L2 locates the disconnect between hidden-state probes (classifiers fit on a model's internal activations) and the model's verbal and sampled readouts. L3 is verification utility: confidence-routed execution budgets, and whether stated confidence updates after an observed test failure. L4 is per-requirement confidence, localization, and targeted repair. F is a measurement-instrument, or metrology, framing of calibration.

Each candidate and component receives one of five verdicts under the brief's definitions:

- **OCCUPIED:** a fetched work makes the core claim at comparable breadth on every dimension the claim names.
- **OCCUPIED (union):** at most three fetched works together cover every dimension, and the only remaining step is running them on the same problems (amendment A20).
- **NARROW:** a precisely stated residual survives the residual rule (amendment A21). Clause 1 requires a new factor, granularity, label protocol, or use, not merely new models or benchmarks. Clause 2 requires a stated reason the code result could differ from the closest general-NLP result.
- **CLEAN:** nothing relevant was found after every fixed query ran on every validated instrument, forward and backward screens of the three closest works were completed, and the last ten screens added nothing.
- **CLEAN (provisional):** CLEAN except for an instrument gap that bears on the claim.

General-NLP work counts only as the comparison side of a residual, and only for L2 and F; pre-2022 metric works are foundations, not occupants. Status tags (amendment A23) are published, preprint only, or concurrent, where "concurrent" means first posted after 2026-05-10, a six-month window that awaits the operator's confirmation. Verdicts rest on full texts, cited by section, table, or equation. ABSTRACT-ONLY marks the two load-bearing records whose vault copy is an abstract: NeuroSym-Cal, whose section-level facts come from a separate PDF read (Section 10) [[neurosym-cal-bridging-the-reasoning-execution-gap-in-code-generation-via-hierarc]], and 2609.00854, which bears on L4 Q2.

### 1.2 TMLR's two criteria as design constraints

TMLR asks whether claims are supported by accurate, convincing, and clear evidence, "the most important criterion," which authors may meet by choosing to "adjust (reduce) their claims." It also asks whether some of its audience would be interested, and tells reviewers: "Do not penalize modest significance or lack of novelty." The editorial policy rejects papers that "incorrectly claim novelty over existing published work" or "merely re-implement an idea that has already been reproduced before."

Read as a specification, these rules demand a claim whose evidence the paper generates itself, a framing honest about Ribeiro and the concurrent citers, and a result informative whether positive or null. Two consequences shape every verdict below. At TMLR, OCCUPIED constrains wording, not acceptance: an occupied component can appear as a replication or baseline. And because the novelty clause names "existing published work," concurrent preprints bear mainly on the accuracy of claims. A paper that ignored Campos et al. would not falsely claim novelty over published work, but a sentence saying "no study reports within-problem AUROC for code probes" would simply be false.

### 1.3 Instruments, and why every CLEAN is provisional

The search instruments failed in specific, documented ways; Section 10 gives each gap, the verdicts it weakens, and the action that would close it. Every negative that depends on body-text search is therefore an instrument gap, never a zero, and no CLEAN verdict here is unconditional.

### 1.4 Four switches that decide contested verdicts

Several verdicts flip on judgment calls the literature cannot settle:

| Switch | Setting A | Setting B | Components it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 concurrency window | Preprints after 2026-05-10 count as occupants | They count only as related work | L1 Q4; L2 Q2a, Q11, Q13; L3 Q1, Q3; the evidence for the expected size of the controlled gap |
| S2 instrument gaps | A gap is read as absence | A gap is read as a possibly hidden occupant | Every CLEAN (provisional) cell |
| S3 A21 code-specific reason | Accepted | Rejected | The L2 factorial residuals (Q2a, Q2b, Q4, Q8); L4 Q1 |
| S4 controlled-test outcome | Probe keeps a within-problem margin over a trained own readout | Tie, or the trained readout closes the gap | Which L2 headline is true; whether the factorial and routing arms are worth running |

S1 through S3 are judgments about the literature; S4 is empirical and the only switch the paper can set itself. The concurrency switch does not simply turn occupancy up or down. The within-problem ties that most weaken the probe headline (Campos et al., Miglani et al.) and the difficulty identity that explains them (Bulut) are all concurrent [[its-the-problem-not-the-pathbudget-and-difficulty-confounds-in-llm-reasoning-tra]]. Excluding them widens the residual but strips the paper of its published reason to expect a small controlled gap, and reviewers will know those papers anyway. This report treats concurrent works as occupants and records where a verdict would move without them (Section 8). The contribution recommended in Section 9 stays NARROW under every setting of S1 through S3.

Two definitional choices also hide inside the rules. If verbal P(True) (the probability the model assigns to "True" when asked whether its answer is correct) counts as stated confidence, the L1 four-family union is easy to assemble; if only numeric or categorical verbal confidence counts, the stated family on code is covered only by Ribeiro (two zero-shot formats), Tuerlings and Valdenegro-Toro (verbal only), and Gros and Devanbu (line level) [[verbalized-uncertainty-in-coding-llms-contamination-aware-calibration-uncertainl]] [[localized-calibrated-uncertainty-in-code-language-models]]. And if L1 were read as granularity-free, Gros and Devanbu would nearly occupy it alone: their Tables 3 and 4 compare token probability, multisampling, line-verbalized confidence, and probes on the same code at line level with BSS (Brier skill score: improvement in Brier score over a base-rate forecast), ECE (expected calibration error: the binned gap between confidence and accuracy), and AUC. This report reads L1 as program-level, keeping Gros and Devanbu as the L4 occupant; the paper must still state that a four-family line-level comparison exists, or a reviewer will read the omission as overclaimed novelty.

## 2. L1: Execution-graded measurement foundation

### 2.1 The four-family comparison is occupied by union

L1's core claim is that comparing sampling agreement, token probability, stated confidence, and hidden-state probes against execution labels shows which methods track correctness on code, across three or more model families, two or more scales, and three or more benchmarks. As a comparison, this is OCCUPIED (union), and the union does not depend on concurrent work.

NeuroSym-Cal (Findings of ACL 2026; vault ABSTRACT-ONLY) covers token probability (AvgLogit and Platt scaling), verbal P(True) and self-verification, sampling agreement (self-consistency and AST consensus over K=10 samples), and IGLB, a multicalibration baseline. It runs DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct on LiveCodeBench v2406 to v2506 (400 problems), HumanEval-Pro, and DS-1000 (Sections 3, 4.1, 4.2), reporting ECE "by 37.4% compared to Self-Consistency (0.072 vs. 0.115)" and LiveCodeBench AUROC 0.805 against 0.798 for IGLB (Section 5.1). It has no probe [[neurosym-cal-bridging-the-reasoning-execution-gap-in-code-generation-via-hierarc]]. Ribeiro et al. (published) supply probe, verbal, and likelihood readouts, so two published works already span all four families [[on-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]]. Campos, Klassert, and Ulges (concurrent) add a third route: Yes/No token confidence, EigenScore (agreement of sampled answers measured in hidden-state space), linear probes and LAT directions (Representation Engineering's principal-component direction over hidden-state differences), and causal predictors built by distributed alignment search (a learned rotation that localizes a causal variable in hidden states), on Qwen3-Coder-30B and gpt-oss-20b with a problem-clustered bootstrap (Sections III to VI) [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]].

Breadth occupants fill in the rest. Code-MUE (ISSTA 2026) spans eight models and four benchmarks with one execution-agreement family [[code-mue-measuring-code-llms-uncertainty-through-execution-based-semantic-intera]]. Functional Entropy covers five closed models on LiveCodeBench, MultiPL-E Java, and LiveSQLBench with token, sampling, and reflexive scorers (Section 4.1, Table 1) [[functional-entropy-predicting-functional-correctness-in-llm-generated-code-with]]. Rathnasuriya and Yang (concurrent) evaluate 17 generation-stage and 6 prefill-stage token and sampling metrics on four instruct models, with best AUC 0.598 to 0.696 (Table 3) [[characterizing-overconfident-failure-in-llm-based-code-generation-full-text]]. Siddiq et al. run four families with Docker execution labels, reporting verbalized security ECE from 0.247 to 0.481 [[an-empirical-study-of-security-calibration-in-large-language-models-for-code]].

The sampling family is crowded: execution-clustered agreement reaches ensemble AUROC 0.776 against 0.696 for the best natural-language baseline [[code-is-more-than-text-uncertainty-estimation-for-code-generation]], semantic-distance estimators exceed AUROC 0.8 in every setting [[using-semantic-distance-to-estimate-uncertainty-in-llm-based-code-generation]], and ensemble semantic entropy reaches accuracy 0.8247 against 0.6420 for single-model semantic entropy at 10% false-positive rate [[ensemble-based-uncertainty-estimation-for-code-correctness-estimation]]. Test-free agreement and execution or symbolic clustering (Q7, Q8), release-window benchmarks used for calibration (Q9), and self-judgment as confidence (Q12) are all OCCUPIED.

Nobody needs another code-confidence leaderboard. A paper claiming "we compared the four families" would re-implement a union that published works already span, and would have to beat NeuroSym-Cal's breadth on the families they share.

### 2.2 What is not occupied: the instrument under the comparison

The open territory is whether family rankings survive changes to the measuring instrument. Three controls matter, and each nearest occupant holds at most one.

**Estimator (Q5).** Tuerlings and Valdenegro-Toro (UncertaiNLP 2026) is the only code study showing a ranking change by metric. In Table 1 the lowest ECE (0.013, CodeLlama-7B Linguistic) comes with AUROC 0.491, while the highest AUROC (0.841, Qwen-32B Linguistic) comes with ECE 0.216; ECE and AUROC "do not correlate across cells," and 8 of 18 cells collapsed (Section 4.6) [[verbalized-uncertainty-in-coding-llms-contamination-aware-calibration-uncertainl]]. The pipeline moves rankings too. Switching the parse policy from parse-repair to a fallback of 50 moves CodeLlama-7B CoT ECE from 0.680 to 0.445 while AUROC barely changes, 0.556 to 0.546 (Section 4.4). Linguistic has the lowest ECE in 4 of 6 models, but 3 of those 4 cells are collapsed, with CodeLlama-7B Linguistic putting 359 of 365 answers at confidence 10 (Sections 4.3 and 4.6). Direct is best on PRR for 4 of 6 models (Table 2). Outside code, ACE finds raw ECE gaps tracking accuracy gaps (rho -0.670) and 265 ranking reversals under accuracy control, so the protocol also needs an accuracy-matched comparison [[when-calibration-rankings-reverse-accuracy-controlled-evaluation-for-fair-compar]].

Tuerlings and Valdenegro-Toro use ten equal-width bins (Section 3.5), and no code study compares equal-mass, debiased, smooth, or kernel estimators. Gros and Devanbu flag ECE as misleadingly low under bucket collapse [[localized-calibrated-uncertainty-in-code-language-models]], and Ghosh et al. show outside code that verbal confidence "looks well calibrated because it clusters near the base rate while discriminating poorly" [[measurement-validity-and-representation-dynamics-ofconfidence-signals-in-large-l]]. Metric decoupling is NARROW; the estimator-variant sub-part is CLEAN (provisional).

**Label oracle (Q10).** EvalPlus showed that 80 times more tests cut pass@k (the share of problems solved within k attempts) by 19.3% to 28.9% across 26 models and can mis-rank models [[is-your-code-generated-by-chatgpt-really-correct-rigorous-evaluation-of-large-la]]. SWE-ABS rejects 19.78% of previously passing patches, moves the top agent from 78.80% to 62.20%, and produces 30 rank changes in the top 30 [[swe-abs-adversarial-benchmark-strengthening-exposes-inflated-success-rates-on-te]]. Liu et al. (concurrent) hold the oracle swap for confidence, but in text-to-SQL: a stronger oracle raises held-out conformal risk by 2.73 to 10.23 points, and under blinded expert labels a certificate at nominal 0.10 carries 20.0 and 17.2 points of risk [[certified-against-which-oracleexecution-labels-set-the-reported-riskof-conformal]].

All of these rank models, agents, or certificates. No code study recomputes a confidence-method ranking under original versus augmented tests; Rathnasuriya and Yang use only the already-strengthened HumanEval+ and MBPP+ and concede they "may still miss untested" behavior [[characterizing-overconfident-failure-in-llm-based-code-generation-full-text]]. The closest code-side partial occupant is selective generation, whose false-discovery-rate guarantee tightens as more fuzz-generated tests label the outputs (Section 5.2.2) [[towards-functional-correctness-of-code-models-with-selective-generation]], label-quantity sensitivity of a guarantee rather than of a cross-family ranking. Q10 is CLEAN (provisional).

**Contamination (Q3, Q13).** NeuroSym-Cal holds out a per-model post-cutoff LiveCodeBench subset (Section 4.1). Tuerlings uses one shared window, 2024-09-19 to 2025-04-30, for the verbal family only (Section 3.1). Campos, Kuschnereit, and Ulges use LiveCodeBench release_v6 (1,055 problems, May 2023 to April 2025) as a whole-benchmark leakage control [[multicalibration-for-llm-based-code-generation]]. ExeCRE checks that its 182-problem window is cutoff-clean for Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct (cutoff 2024-06) and LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (2023-12) but not for GPT-5.2 (2025-08) [[execre-execution-consistency-guided-reliability-estimation-for-self-correcting-c]]. Campos et al. use 454 problems "released between October 2024 and May 2025" for language shift, not as a contamination contrast [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]]; the Klassert thesis "prioritizes ... over strict contamination control" [[abstract]]; and Lugoloobi et al. use release-date splits to predict accuracy, not to contrast calibration [[llms-encode-their-failures-predicting-success-from-pre-generation-activations]].

LiveCodeBench supplies the windows that make a contrast possible [[livecodebench-holistic-and-contamination-free-evaluation-of-large-language-model-2]], but no fetched work, and none of 2,100 LiveCodeBench citers screened by title and abstract, compares calibration on pre-cutoff versus post-cutoff problems. A held-out window is hygiene; a contrast is a measurement. Q3 is NARROW and Q13 CLEAN (provisional); one title-only TSE 2026 record on contamination in code generation (Crossref 10.1109/tse.2026.3713834) was not fetched and must be checked for a confidence analysis before a Q13 novelty sentence is written.

### 2.3 Label independence is a live defect

Code-MUE's AUROC label "Solved" means at least one of the N sampled candidates passes all tests, so the label is computed from the same samples whose agreement the method scores (Section 5.2.1) [[code-mue-measuring-code-llms-uncertainty-through-execution-based-semantic-intera]]. Liu et al. name the mechanism: "Every execution-consistency score looks better under the labels of the oracle that built its clusters, in 16 of 16 combinations," so "a consistency score should be evaluated under an oracle that did not build it" [[certified-against-which-oracleexecution-labels-set-the-reported-riskof-conformal]]. Good practice exists but is never tested as a factor: Tuerlings freezes code before elicitation so that "correctness labels are determined independently of any confidence estimation" (Section 3.3), and Code Is More Than Text keeps self-generated tests apart from the label suite "since otherwise the functional signal would be trivially perfect by construction" [[code-is-more-than-text-uncertainty-estimation-for-code-generation]].

A strict reader calls this Liu's mechanism moved from SQL to Python, a benchmark change that fails A21 clause 1. Code, however, supplies direct evidence the SQL paper does not: fuzz inputs seeded from benchmark tests raise semantic-distance agreement AUROC by 0.014 to 0.087 over seed-free inputs (Table 6) [[using-semantic-distance-to-estimate-uncertainty-in-llm-based-code-generation]]. Because benchmark tests drive both the label and every test-execution agreement score, a thin suite yields false passes on plausible but wrong programs and inflates exactly the families that track test-style agreement. A base-versus-augmented relabeling could therefore reorder families, not just shift pass rates. Q4 is NARROW.

### 2.4 Recalibration separates mapping from information

Platt and isotonic maps (monotone functions fit from scores to observed accuracy) cut CodeLlama's Brier score from 0.459 to 0.248 while BSS stays at about 0.001 (Rathnasuriya and Yang, Section 6.1, Table 5) [[characterizing-overconfident-failure-in-llm-based-code-generation-full-text]]. A monotone map cannot change AUROC, so recalibration fixes calibration and never discrimination. Multicalibration (calibration enforced across intersecting subgroups) lifts LiveCodeBench BSS from -0.543 for uncalibrated Qwen3 to 0.480 under IGLB [[multicalibration-for-llm-based-code-generation]], and the Klassert thesis reports a Qwen3-Coder probe at BSS 0.5888 against 0.4680 for IGLB (Table 5.7) [[abstract]]. But the multicalibration gain comes from group features (complexity, code length, prompt length, language), problem-level covariates outside the model's readout, so part of the reported skill is difficulty information. Recalibration is OCCUPIED as a control at token and line level (Q6), and multicalibration for token likelihood (Q11); across families both are NARROW. They impose two design requirements: AUROC beside BSS for every recalibrated readout, and a covariate-only predictor so a recalibration gain is not credited to the readout.

### 2.5 The strict reading, and the verdict

A strict reader rates each L1 control OCCUPIED on its own and dismisses the protocol as bundling, which A20 discounts as "running them on the same problems." A second objection holds that standard practice is already adequate: Functional Entropy finds its calibration results "broadly consistent with the AUROC findings" across five closed models [[functional-entropy-predicting-functional-correctness-in-llm-generated-code-with]], and Code Is More Than Text declines to report ECE because rank-normalized scores "have no meaningful absolute calibration" [[code-is-more-than-text-uncertainty-estimation-for-code-generation]]. The dispute is an untested assumption, not a factual disagreement: no code study has checked that concordance on open models, under strengthened-test relabeling, or across benchmark families. The bundling objection is right that bundling alone is not novelty and misses why the bundle matters. The instrument fragilities that could reorder an L1 leaderboard would also move every L2 and L3 number: label flips under HumanEval+ and MBPP+ change which candidates count as discordant pairs, and collapsed verbal distributions change both ECE and within-problem AUROC. An L2 headline reported without these controls invites the question of whether it survives a stronger oracle, and the paper would have no answer.

L1 is OCCUPIED (union) as a comparison and NARROW as a protocol, with Q10 and Q13 CLEAN (provisional). The protocol belongs as the paper's measurement layer, not a standalone benchmark. Q10 and Q13 are cheap inside the same design and yield two findings that hold whatever the probe result; if rankings prove invariant, the paper reports "rankings are stable under these controls" rather than dropping them.

## 3. L2: Locating the disconnect between internal state and output

### 3.1 The uncontrolled headline is occupied, and firmly

Ribeiro et al. fit a LAT direction at the last token and use it to pick the correct implementation among four candidates for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct, OpenCoder-8B-Instruct, and CodeLlama-7B-Instruct, on HumanEval (151 tasks) and BigCodeBench (457 tasks). "LAT consistently outperforms all baselines" by the margins quoted above (Section 4.2.1; the takeaway box says +13.3 to +28.8 points, a small internal inconsistency). In-distribution LAT accuracy on BigCodeBench is 50.3% to 56.3% against 27.5% random for Mistral and 23.7% to 41.1% for verbal baselines (Table 3). The verbal readouts are zero-shot, and their formats disagree: Mistral scores 40.6% with the seven-level scale and 15.3% with True/False on HumanEval [[on-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]].

L2 Q1 is OCCUPIED by Ribeiro alone for probe versus verbal, and OCCUPIED (union) with Campos et al. once sampling is added. Prior art makes this firmer. Openia trained last-token, last-layer probes on DeepSeek-Coder, CodeLlama, and Magicoder, with "up to a 2X improvement in standalone code generation and a 3X enhancement in repository-specific scenarios" [[correctness-assessment-of-code-generated-by-large-language-models-using-internal]], and AutoProbe's attention-weighted probe reaches 0.63 to 0.71 functionality accuracy against 0.32 to 0.61 for the generator judging itself (Table 5) [[model-agnostic-correctness-assessment-for-llm-generated-code-via-dynamic-interna]]. Probes for code correctness, transfer, and other white-box readouts (Q5 to Q7) are OCCUPIED by these works, by Campos et al. under shift, by sparse-autoencoder (SAE) directions [[mechanistic-interpretability-of-code-correctness-in-llms-via-sparse-autoencoders]], by a pre-generation probe [[code-correctness-is-linearly-decodable-from-llm-hidden-states-before-generation]], and by diffusion-model probes [[probing-functional-correctness-in-diffusion-language-models-full-text]]. A paper whose contribution is "the probe-verbal gap on more models or benchmarks" fails both A21 clause 1 and TMLR's re-implementation clause.

### 3.2 Three confounds between the occupant's margin and "unexpressed knowledge"

The occupied claim is real, but it is a fact about a supervised reader, not yet evidence that the model holds information it does not express. Three confounds, each documented in a fetched full text, stand between them.

**Difficulty.** Bulut's Equation 1 decomposes pooled AUROC as lambda × within-problem AUROC + (1 − lambda) × between-problem AUROC, where lambda is the share of discordant pairs from the same problem. "Every question-only predictor has AUROC_within = 1/2 exactly," and with one attempt per problem, "lambda = 0." A published early-window probe on MATH, reconstructed, gives pooled AUROC 0.849 [0.735, 0.919] but within-problem 0.496 (Section 4.4) [[its-the-problem-not-the-pathbudget-and-difficulty-confounds-in-llm-reasoning-tra]]. In a balanced design of 164 problems with 10 samples each (5 pass, 5 fail), only about 0.6% of pairs fall within a problem, so a pooled code AUROC is about 99% a between-problem number; agent studies report more than 99.93% cross-unit pairs, with a run-blind difficulty oracle reaching AUROC 0.945 [[disentangling-task-difficulty-from-run-level-failure-in-agent-failure-prediction]].

On code the effect is measured directly. A Campos et al. probe at the last prompt token, which sees only the problem, has pooled AUROC 0.891 and within-problem 0.514, leading the authors to conclude that "much of what the methods predict is thus the problem, not the candidate" (Table V) [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]]. On LiveCodeBench a TF-IDF classifier on prompt text recovers 0.84 to 0.86 of the 0.90 to 0.91 probe AUROC for Qwen2.5-Coder and beats the probe for R1-Distill (Table 2) [[llms-encode-their-failures-predicting-success-from-pre-generation-activations]]. Di Cicco's pre-generation probe reaches 0.881 ± 0.008, or 0.842 after residualizing on prompt length against a 0.657 length baseline, but scores one attempt per task and so carries zero within-problem information [[code-correctness-is-linearly-decodable-from-llm-hidden-states-before-generation]].

The same identity settles whether verbal confidence on code is useful or near chance. Tuerlings elicits confidence on one greedy generation per problem, and Functional Entropy scores one original response per prompt [[functional-entropy-predicting-functional-correctness-in-llm-generated-code-with]], so Qwen-32B's 0.841 and the verbal range of 0.597 to 0.795 are between-problem numbers: they measure whether the model knows which problems it fails, at strict pass rates of 8.22% to 29.86% in Tuerlings. Ribeiro's near-chance reflective readouts (about 7% for Qwen on HumanEval against 25% chance) come from within-task selection. Both are right about different components. No fetched code study reports within-problem AUROC for numeric verbal confidence on self-generated samples, the most important gap for this project.

**Supervision asymmetry.** Ribeiro fits LAT on 10% of tasks and validates on another 10% (Section 4.1.2), while verbal readouts stay zero-shot. Liu, Casper, Hadfield-Menell, and Andreas named the asymmetry in 2023: "probes are trained many-shot while queries use the model zero-shot." With a fine-tuned query, probes led by 1.7% on BoolQ but queries led by 6.9% and 4.0% on SciQ and CREAK (Section 3), so the winner flipped on two of three datasets [[cognitive-dissonance-why-do-language-model-outputs-disagree-with-internal-repres]]. The only partial code control is Gros and Devanbu's Platt scaling of every readout. Platt cannot change AUC, so their Qwen line-level gap, where the probe reads the generator's own states (0.83 against line-verbalized 0.71 AUC; 0.33 against 0.10 Platt-scaled BSS), survives it. For GPT-4o, where the "probe" is a separate 0.5B or 7B reader, the margin is only 0.78 against 0.75 AUC and 0.22 against 0.17 BSS. No trained verbal head is present [[localized-calibrated-uncertainty-in-code-language-models]].

**Provenance.** Ribeiro's correct class is the human reference, and the three incorrect candidates come from other models (Llama-3.3-70B, GPT-4o, and Gemini 2.0 Flash on BigCodeBench; Section 4.1.1). The intrinsic readout picks the reference only "around 5-6%" of the time against 25% chance, so likelihood is strongly anti-correlated with the label and any supervised reader that learns the sign wins. The authors' follow-up names the confound ("variable names or implementation style") and finds that a direction fit on fault-isolating mutation pairs "separates them with high accuracy in isolation, yet does not transfer," dropping 37.1 points on BigCodeBench against 5.2 on HumanEval [[on-the-robustness-of-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]].

The counter-evidence is real: a DiffuCoder probe still separates pass and fail mutants that differ by small edits, with AUC 0.670 on 392 MBPP+ mutants, 0.692 on 334 HumanEval+ mutants, and 0.774 on mutations of the model's own code [[a-heckler-in-the-hidden-statecorrectness-signals-in-diffusion-language-models]], and on math a first-step probe beats a TF-IDF text-surface classifier, 0.79 to 0.59 [[hidden-error-awareness-in-chain-of-thought-reasoningthe-signal-is-diagnostic-not]]. Provenance is a plausible but unproven explanation of Ribeiro's margin; the sign-reversed likelihood baseline in Section 9.1 item 4 decides it. Ribeiro's cleaner RQ2, which ranks ten self-generated samples within a task, shows small gains (Mistral 41.3% at rank 1 on HumanEval against 34.7% pass@1 and 35.5% for RankEF), and "only a few comparisons reached statistical significance" [[on-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]].

### 3.3 What the existing controlled comparisons say

Three code comparisons in two papers score a probe against the model's own output confidence within problem on self-generated samples. Under shift and on the small diffusion and autoregressive samples, all tie or favor the readout; the one exception, gpt-oss in distribution, follows below. Miglani et al. (Section 4.2, Table 1) find a DiffuCoder-7B probe at 0.842 against negative token entropy at 0.838 (19 mixed problems), and a Qwen2.5-Coder probe at 0.677 against completion log-probability at 0.703 (56 mixed problems, 448 attempts): "Model confidence is thus a competitive baseline ... Reading hidden states provides little apparent gain" [[a-heckler-in-the-hidden-statecorrectness-signals-in-diffusion-language-models]]. Campos et al. report that "under language shift, within-problem AUROC lies at 0.58-0.64 for all methods and both models," against pooled 0.67 to 0.81, and that "for Qwen3-Coder confidence performs best within problem (0.629)," with the last-code-token probe at 0.620 (Section VI-C) [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]].

Even pooled, the Qwen3-Coder probe edge over confidence is small: +0.029 on LiveCodeBench (.844 against .815), +0.006 on BigCodeBench (.656 against .650), and +0.119 in distribution on the language sets, shrinking to +0.035 under language shift (Table IV). Candidate-aware probes fall sharply from pooled to within: 0.886 to 0.553 on the judging pass, and 0.895 to 0.620 on the model's own generation (Table V).

The exceptions matter. For gpt-oss in distribution, Campos's trained methods reach 0.69 to 0.78 within problem, an advantage lost under shift, and a probe on the causally localized subspace stays 0.09 AUROC ahead of output confidence for Qwen3-Coder under language shift, without generalizing to gpt-oss or benchmark shift. Diffusion models give the strongest supervision-matched probe edge (Dream 0.879 against 0.776 for logistic regression on entropy on MBPP), but pooled [[probing-functional-correctness-in-diffusion-language-models-full-text]].

The best estimate is therefore that the controlled within-problem gap is likely within about 0.05 AUROC and possibly zero, held at about 65% confidence, which is why 0.05 is the overturning threshold in Section 9.5. The samples are small (19 and 56 mixed problems), and none of the comparisons includes a verbal readout, a trained own readout, or per-candidate sampling agreement. EigenScore scores the problem, not the candidate, so its within-problem AUROC is 0.5 by construction.

### 3.4 Trained stated confidence is now a concrete threat on code

Pivot criterion A14 asks whether an elicitation, stated-confidence training, or recalibration method that a fair comparison must include closes the gap. On code it is not triggered, but the threat is concrete. CREDO (concurrent) trains stated confidence with execution labels on DeepCoder with Qwen3-8B, reporting in-domain code AUROC of 0.850 against 0.815 for RLCR, 0.776 for DCPO, 0.753 for GRPO, and 0.690 for the untrained base (Table 1), with no probe arm [[beyond-verbalized-confidence-calibrating-reasoners-with-differentiable-readouts]]. EpiCaR reports MBPP AUROC 0.538 out of domain, also without a probe (Table 4) [[epicar-knowing-what-you-dont-know-mattersfor-better-reasoning-in-llms]]. ExecuCritic trains a separate critic on the coder's execution rollouts, reaching ECE 0.061 and AUC 72.8 against 0.213 and 60.4 for a frozen prompted critic (Table 5), again with no probe arm; a trained external critic is a third supervised readout class the design must keep distinct from a trained own readout [[execucritic-calibrated-critic-shaping-forcode-generation-with-verifiable-rewards]]. L2 Q11 is therefore NARROW, not CLEAN (provisional): the residual is trained stated confidence against a probe on the same code generations, scored within problem.

General NLP points the same way. Probe-SD distills a probe into the verbal channel and lifts Qwen3-14B's verbal AUROC on TriviaQA from .825 to .928 in domain against a probe at .948, while isotonic recalibration cuts ECE from .178 to .013 but leaves AUROC at .825 (Tables 1 and 2) [[calibration-not-answer-selection-distilling-internal-confidence-in-reasoning-mod]]. RLCR, which adds a Brier reward to RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR), reaches verbal AUROC 0.69 against 0.55 for a final-layer probe on the RLVR model on HotpotQA, and 0.67 against 0.65 on Big-Math (Table 1) [[beyond-binary-rewards-training-lms-to-reason-about-their-uncertainty-2]]. ConfTuner reports AUROC gains of up to 14.4% [[conftuner-training-large-language-models-to-express-their-confidence-verbally]].

The opposing evidence is as specific. When Words Fall Short shows that estimator overfitting at a single training endpoint "can bias comparisons in favor of verbalized confidence"; with validation-based checkpoint selection, a hidden-state estimator beats RL-trained verbal confidence by 0.161 AUROC in domain and 0.078 out of domain (Section 2.2) [[when-words-fall-short-iterative-synergy-between-verbalized-reasoning-and-hidden]]. A pre-registered confidence fine-tuning lowered verbal AUROC2 from 0.554 to 0.509 and cost 7.6 accuracy points [[distilling-self-consistency-into-verbal-confidence-a-pre-registered-negative-res]]. On LiveCodeBench, RLCM's probe-read confidence has ECE 0.051 against 0.218 for RLCR's verbal confidence, but that column reports ECE only and the model was trained on math [[process-supervision-of-confidence-margin-for-calibrated-llm-reasoning]].

These sides define the experiment rather than cancel. RLCR and When Words Fall Short disagree over the same comparison, so general NLP has not settled whether training closes the discrimination gap. Code adds a concrete reason the answer could differ: execution labels let every readout be trained on exactly the probe's supervision at no annotation cost, which judge- or string-match-labeled NLP comparisons lack. The design therefore requires a supervision-matched arm (Section 9.1 item 2). CREDO also strengthens the case that this arm may close the gap, and the paper must say so in advance and treat closure as a finding.

### 3.5 The strongest objection: the controlled test is nearly a union

A strict reader argues that the controlled test is OCCUPIED (union) except for one arm: Campos supplies within-problem scoring and an input-only baseline, Ribeiro supplies within-task verbal comparison, and Miglani supplies within-problem centering on an autoregressive code model. What remains, the trained own readout, is the arm CREDO and Probe-SD expect to close the gap.

The objection is right about size and wrong about classification. A20 requires the works together to cover every dimension of the core claim, and none of Ribeiro, Campos, or Miglani covers numeric verbal confidence scored within problem on self-generated samples, or a supervision-matched own readout on those samples. Ribeiro's within-task verbal comparison uses provenance-confounded candidates in RQ1 and reports pass@rank, with no AUROC and no trained readout, in RQ2. The residual is a label protocol and a set of controls, so clause 1 holds; clause 2 holds through three code-specific reasons (symmetric supervision is free under execution labels, function-level difficulty is recoverable from prompt text, and the occupant's contrast classes differ in provenance). Q1c is NARROW under every setting of S1 and S3. The objection survives in the form the recommendation adopts: the residual is small, and its most informative arm is the one most likely to return a null. A forward screen of 14 citers of Ribeiro, one of its follow-up, and four of Di Cicco found none running a supervision-matched control.

### 3.6 Each outcome of the controlled test yields a paper

| Outcome | What the paper can claim | What becomes worth running |
|---|---|---|
| (α) Probe keeps a within-problem margin over a trained own readout, with a problem-clustered CI excluding zero | The disconnect is unexpressed information on code beyond difficulty and supervision | The factorial decomposition; L3 routing by the probe |
| (β) Probe matches a trained own readout but beats zero-shot verbal | The disconnect is an elicitation and supervision gap that closes once the output channel is trained on execution labels; CREDO's result with a probe arm | Trained-readout routing in L3; the factorial run becomes optional |
| (γ) Every readout sits near 0.5 to 0.65 within problem, and pooled differences are difficulty | Code-correctness readouts at this scale mostly detect hard problems; the pooled probe advantage in the literature is mostly between-problem | L3 replay, predicted to fail in the hard stratum |

Outcome β does not trigger A14, which is defined over a fetched record showing the gap closes; the criterion protects against building on a refuted claim, not against the paper refuting it. All three outcomes satisfy TMLR's first criterion if the design is right. On the second, α and β interest the probing and calibration audiences, and γ interests anyone who reports pooled AUROC for code correctness, as the occupants themselves do.

### 3.7 The factorial residuals: real, but dependent on the controlled result

**Base versus instruct (Q2a).** Rathnasuriya and Yang (Section 5.5) give the only code comparison of base and instruct models under execution labels, at token level only. DeepSeekCoder's base confidence AUC is 0.4391, meaning certainty is anti-aligned with correctness, rising to 0.5654 after tuning (ΔAUC +0.1263, CI [0.0447, 0.2089]); OpenCoder moves by -0.0136 (CI [-0.0911, 0.0652]) while failing-program confidence rises from 0.8685 to 0.9224 [[characterizing-overconfident-failure-in-llm-based-code-generation-full-text]]. SAE directions trained on Gemma-2-2b base remain effective after instruction tuning (feature F1 0.821 to 0.772), with no verbal readout [[mechanistic-interpretability-of-code-correctness-in-llms-via-sparse-autoencoders]]. Outside code, Miao and Ungar find that "base models verbalize poorly calibrated confidence despite encoding well-calibrated accuracy information internally," with accuracy and verbal-confidence directions at cosine similarity below 0.04 (Section 3.3) [[closing-the-confidence-faithfulness-gap-in-large-language-models]]; the GPT-4 report finds the pre-trained model "highly calibrated" [[gpt-4-technical-report]]; and instruction tuning raises ECE by 13.1% on average across six base and instruct pairs [[large-language-models-are-overconfident-in-their-own-responses]].

The direction reverses by readout, however: Tian et al. find verbal confidence from RLHF models better calibrated than their token probabilities, often cutting ECE by a relative 50% (abstract-level) [[230514975-just-ask-for-calibration-strategies-for-eliciting-calibrated-confidenc-3]], and the three-stage math study finds that post-training "does not uniformly" improve calibration [[post-training-shifts-confidence-a-three-stage-analysis-of-how-sft-rl-and-opd-sha]]. This readout-dependent, contested general-NLP direction carries A21 clause 2 for Q2a. The code reason is that an anti-aligned base readout inverts the usual story that tuning corrupts a calibrated base, so Q2a is NARROW. The strict objection carries weight: the reason rests on one pair, for token readouts only, and says nothing yet about the probe-minus-verbal gap. There is also a design hazard: Ghosh et al. pre-registered a base-versus-instruct test and declined to run it because the 7B base model's verbal output used "essentially two labels" [[measurement-validity-and-representation-dynamics-ofconfidence-signals-in-large-l]]. Base models may need a P(True)-style format.

**Reasoning (Q2b, Q9, Q10).** Campos prefills gpt-oss so it answers without reasoning; only a construct-validity check shows medium-effort reasoning raising Yes-probability AUROC "from 0.68 to 0.73, still below the trained internal methods," and the probe is never read under reasoning (Sections V-A, VIII). Barkan et al. toggle reasoning budgets for in-advance verbal confidence on BigCodeBench and find "reasoning LLMs perform comparably to or worse than non-reasoning LLMs" [[do-large-language-models-know-what-they-are-capable-of]]. Open the Oyster compares different weights (DeepSeek-Reasoner and DeepSeek-Chat) on code-reasoning tasks, with ECE 0.066 against 0.143 (Section 5.1.1) [[open-the-oyster-empirical-evaluation-and-improvement-of-code-reasoning-confidenc]].

A strict reader rates the same-weights toggle OCCUPIED by analogy under A21 clause 2. That fails because the general-NLP direction is contested: Yoon et al. find reasoning models better calibrated "in 33 out of the 36 settings" [[reasoning-models-better-express-their-confidence]], while Barkan finds no gain and Miao and Ungar find that joint solve-and-rate prompting inverts the confidence relation from +0.26 to -0.63 (Section 3.4). Probes lead verbal output in reasoning models outside code (GLM-4.7-Flash AUROC 0.768 against 0.669) [[what-llm-forecasters-know-but-dont-say-probing-internal-representations-for-cali]]. Code adds an operational obstacle: Zeng et al. excluded code because "most models struggled to output both code snippets and confidence simultaneously" [[thinking-out-loud-do-reasoning-models-know-when-theyre-right]].

A same-weights toggle on code with the probe read under reasoning is NARROW. Q10 is NARROW on the RLCM column; Q9 is NARROW for post-hoc stated confidence and CLEAN (provisional) for trace signals beating the final number on code; outside code, probes on R1-distilled models verify intermediate math answers [[reasoning-models-know-when-theyre-right-probing-hidden-states-for-self-verificat]] and trace length works as an uncertainty signal [[trace-length-is-a-simple-uncertainty-signal-in-reasoning-models]].

**Format (Q3).** Within verbal readouts on code, format is OCCUPIED: Tuerlings' three formats give Qwen-32B AUROC 0.834, 0.810, and 0.841, and DeepSeek-6.7B 0.518, 0.716, and 0.498 [[verbalized-uncertainty-in-coding-llms-contamination-aware-calibration-uncertainl]]. Only the format-by-readout interaction is NARROW, and it belongs in the design as a nuisance factor, tested against prompt-wording noise as in a calibrated invariance audit of 30.73 million executed generations [[identity-or-prompt-noise-a-calibrated-invariance-audit-of-llm-code-generation]].

**Post-training stages (Q8) and location (Q4).** A stage decomposition exists on math only, with Pre-CoT AUROC 0.594 (Instruct), 0.526 (SFT, supervised fine-tuning), 0.320 (RL), and 0.644 (OPD, on-policy distillation) from one backbone [[post-training-shifts-confidence-a-three-stage-analysis-of-how-sft-rl-and-opd-sha]]. Q8 is a weak NARROW whose code reason is a hypothesis; Q4 is NARROW.

These residuals survive A21 if its code reasons are accepted, but none should lead. Decomposing a gap not yet shown to exist under controls partitions noise. The factorial arms belong in a pre-registered conditional extension, run if the controlled gap clears a stated margin with an interval excluding zero and reported as exploratory otherwise.

### 3.8 Causal location (Q12)

On code, causal work targets output correctness, not stated confidence. SAE steering fixes 4.04% of errors but corrupts 14.66% of correct code [[mechanistic-interpretability-of-code-correctness-in-llms-via-sparse-autoencoders]], and steering along Miglani's probe direction lowers pass@1 from 0.433 to 0.388 toward pass and to 0.298 toward fail [[a-heckler-in-the-hidden-statecorrectness-signals-in-diffusion-language-models]]. Outside code, activation patching shows a post-answer-newline probe causally sufficient to rescue error detection (AUROC .986 against .908 for the best behavioral baseline) [[how-llms-detect-and-correct-their-own-errors-the-role-of-internal-confidence-sig]], and circuit-level intervention cuts inflated verbal ECE from 0.647 to 0.068 [[wired-for-overconfidence-a-mechanistic-perspective-on-inflated-verbalized-confid]]. Q12 is NARROW and pivot A15 is not triggered, but causal work multiplies the method surface without strengthening the headline.

### 3.9 Pivot criteria and verdict on L2

- **Pivot 1 (A20 union across probe, stated, sampling, base-instruct, and reasoning):** not triggered under any switch setting; the stated-confidence side and the factor side never meet on code.
- **Pivot 2 (A21, residual is only a model change):** triggered for the uncontrolled headline; never for the controlled test; for the factorial versions only if S3 rejects the code reasons.
- **Pivot 3 (A14, trained stated confidence closes the gap on code):** not triggered, because CREDO has no probe arm, but concrete; the design must include the arm that tests it.
- **Pivot 4 (A15, causal localization on code):** not triggered.

Whole L2 is NARROW, conditional on the controlled test being the headline. The residual: a same-sample comparison on self-generated function-level code, under execution labels, scored pooled and within problem, of a probe, a supervision-matched own readout, zero-shot numeric and categorical verbal confidence, token probability, and per-candidate sampling agreement, with prompt-only and code-text-only baselines. Closest code work: Ribeiro (zero-shot verbal, provenance-confounded) and Campos (pooled versus within, no verbal, no trained readout). Closest NLP work: Liu et al. 2023 (supervision-matched, no execution labels). Because L2 does not fail the pivot criteria, the brief's replacement order is not invoked; had it been, the A25-gated order would run L1 plus L3 (NARROW, eligible), then L4 (NARROW, needing an operator scope decision), then F (OCCUPIED, ineligible), and A26 does not apply because not every candidate is OCCUPIED.

## 4. L3: Verification utility and confidence updating

### 4.1 Selective prediction and repository agents are occupied

CodeRefuser (concurrent) gives risk-controlled abstention through Learn-Then-Test (a procedure that picks an abstention threshold with a statistical risk guarantee) over execution-cluster agreement, with "an average of 26.5% and 11.9% absolute improvements w.r.t. abstention precision and F1-score"; at alpha 0.2 on MBPP with Qwen2.5-Coder-32B it abstains on about 30% of tasks against more than 80% for perplexity (Table 1, Figures 2 and 3) [[260517029-task-abstention-for-large-language-models-in-code-generation-full-text]]. Selective generation with false-discovery-rate control [[towards-functional-correctness-of-code-models-with-selective-generation]] and a deferral framework with selective accuracy over 70% at 80% coverage on MBPP+ [[when-to-answer-and-when-to-defer-a-decision-framework-for-reliable-code-predicti]] keep Q3 OCCUPIED even without concurrent work. Repository-agent uncertainty (Q6) is OCCUPIED by Kaddour et al. alone, who elicit stated success probability before, during, and after execution on SWE-bench Pro and find post-execution predictions of about 73% success against 35% true for GPT-5.2-Codex [[agentic-uncertainty-reveals-agentic-overconfidence]].

### 4.2 Routing: positives and negatives use different gates and budget units

Every positive code result for confidence-routed compute uses a learned or proxy gate. Heteroskedastic Signals replays MBPP with a logit-margin proxy: at beta 10% for Qwen3-8B, Random scores .551, Threshold .636, cost-stratified thresholding .671, and Oracle 1.00 (Table 1), after the authors "add 10% Bernoulli label noise"; the oracle is the label itself, and the gain over thresholding has CI [-0.2, +7.0] [[heteroskedastic-signals-in-budgeted-llm-verificationstructural-heterogeneity-lim]]. SWE-Verifier gates step sampling with a trained Qwen3.5-4B scorer, moving MiniMax-M2.7 from 75.47 ± 1.40 to 77.20 ± 0.87 at 1.09x cost; its "Oracle@3" (82.20 at 3.00x) is best-of-three rollouts, not a routing oracle, and no random gate is reported (Tables 1 and 2) [[261030284-swe-verifier-selective-verification-for-coding-agents-full-text]]. SWE-Router's trained value head reaches Route-AUC 0.780 above a random-assignment band but routes between models with no oracle arm [[swe-router-routing-in-multi-turn-agentic-software-engineering-tasks]]. Capability self-assessment beats random routing "by about 8.2 percentage points at matched cloud-call budgets," on math [[capability-self-assessment-in-large-language-models]].

The comparator design itself is published on code: TRIAGE (concurrent) scores a model's self-written token-budget plan on LiveCodeBench between a random-permutation reference and a knapsack oracle built from the model's own solvability and cost (Section 3, Equations 1 to 3), but its gate is a plan, not elicited confidence or a probe, and its budget unit is generation tokens, not execution [[260513414-triage-evaluating-prospective-metacognitive-control-in-llms-under-reso]].

The negatives use other budget units. Give Up to Get More shows that difficulty-proportional rules capture "at most 13%" of oracle gain on hard workloads even with true pass rates, and only 1% on CodeContests (Llama-3-8B, mean pass rate 0.021); a prior must reach a rank correlation of about 0.3 to 0.55 before Thompson sampling (posterior-sampling allocation) beats round-robin, and LLM-derived priors reach Spearman 0.10 to 0.46 (Tables 1 and 3) [[give-up-to-get-more-priors-not-policies-govern-test-time-compute-allocation]]. Uncertainty-triggered regeneration lowers pass@1 in five of six configurations [[1introduction]]. Learned stopping beats matched random restarting by at most 1.98 points, entirely through between-task allocation, with within-task AUC 0.50 [0.48, 0.51] [[continue-or-restart-the-limits-of-early-failure-prediction-in-coding-agents]], and trajectory predictors reach about 0.62 AUROC against 0.92 for an issue-difficulty reference [[hard-issue-weak-model-or-bad-runrethinking-failure-prediction-for-coding-agents]].

Adjacent works gate without random or oracle arms, such as staged verification at 11.64x throughput for 8.26% accuracy loss [[1introduction-3]] and CoTT, which cuts test-generation cost from $0.077 to $0.065 [[confidence-gated-transductive-test-generation-for-code-reranking]]. Execution-agreement reranking (CodeT, LEVER) selects among candidates rather than allocating an execution budget [[codet-code-generation-with-generated-tests]] [[lever-learning-to-verify-language-to-code-generation-with-execution]]. Agreement-based stopping saves up to 3.4x samples on code-davinci-002 and confidence-driven early stopping works on math, both without random or oracle routing arms [[lets-sample-step-by-stepadaptive-consistency-for-efficient-reasoning-and-coding]] [[efficient-test-time-scaling-via-self-calibration]], and cascade routing gains 14% on SWE-Bench with quality estimates simulated by Gaussian noise, not model confidence [[a-unified-approach-to-routing-and-cascading-for-llms-full-text]]. None subsumes the residual; they belong in related work.

"Gating helps" and "gating does not help" are both right because they test different gate classes and budget units. Give Up supplies the joining quantity: a gate helps only when its within-pool rank correlation clears that 0.3 to 0.55 band and mean pass rate exceeds about 0.1. The residual is the gate and the unit, not the comparator: elicited verbal or probe confidence as the gate, execution budget per candidate as the unit, on unmodified execution labels. Matched-budget random and label-sorted oracle arms, following TRIAGE, are required design rather than novelty. It is NARROW.

A strict reader rates routing OCCUPIED (union), since switching from a logit proxy to an elicited score changes the signal, not the factor or use, and the outcome is predictable from Give Up's threshold. The reading is not adopted: no fetched work allocates an execution budget per candidate by elicited verbal or probe confidence on clean labels, and the code reason is concrete, since pass rates on hard code sit in the regime where difficulty rules fail. The objection does bound the result. L3's gain is capped by the readout quality L2 measures; if within-problem AUROC is 0.6 to 0.7 at best, a within-problem gate can gain little, and the hard stratum is predicted to fail for every gate. Without concurrent work, routing would move toward CLEAN (provisional), since every positive and the main negative are concurrent, but that would make L3 look cleanest exactly when it is least informative.

### 4.3 Updating after a shown failing test

No fetched record measures stated confidence before and after the model sees a failing test on its own program, tied to the repair outcome, with valid and spurious failures contrasted. The nearest works miss the condition instructively. Kaddour et al. find that agents "develop 'cold feet': confidence decreases as they observe their partial work, but this doubt is uninformative," with mid-execution AUROC about 0.53 for GPT, 0.49 to 0.64 for Gemini, and 0.62 to 0.52 for Claude, yet the agent "cannot execute code, run tests" (Section 2.2.1). Barkan et al. update confidence after earlier contract outcomes, not test failures on the same code. XConf builds confidence from graded past episodes across tasks [[confidence-comes-from-experience-experiential-confidence-estimation-from-reasoni]].

Resist and Update supplies an evidence-versus-pressure design outside code and finds confidence "a weaker instrument" than answer and caveat coordinates [[260712985-resist-and-update-counterfactual-report-coordinates-full-text]]. Iscan's pre-registered placebo design finds "blind resampling beat bare-code retry by +18 net unlocks" (p=0.0021) but never elicits confidence [[falsification-not-exposure-placebo-controlled-decomposition-of-self-repair-feedb]]. Not-One-Belief compares verbal confidence, likelihood, a frozen hidden-state probe, and P(True) under conversational feedback outside code and finds the four signals dissociate [[not-one-belief-answer-payloads-persistence-and-dissociated-confidence-signals-in]]. LADDER, read in full, is the closest analogue to the spurious-failure arm, contrasting valid and noisy feedback across 18 models, but its feedback is simulated user confirmation or correction on non-code QA with no executed tests (Section 3), so it is adjacent rather than an occupant [[ladder-diagnosing-domain-level-self-assessment-under-noisy-feedback]]. 2610.19226 probes reconsideration of stated feasibility after a single challenge on Terminal-Bench (abstract only, not read) and must be read in full before any novelty sentence on this component.

The code reason here is the strongest in the study: code is where the evidence shown to the model is executable and verifiable, and where a spurious failure can be built by mutating a test, a contrast QA cannot supply with ground truth. The component is CLEAN (provisional) and the most fragile CLEAN in the study (Section 10).

### 4.4 Verdict on L3

L3 is NARROW and belongs in the paper as a secondary analysis, since it costs almost nothing once L1 and L2 generations exist: an offline replay at budgets of 10%, 20%, 30%, and 50%, ten or more seeds, paired gains with 95% CIs, stratification by mean pass rate (below 0.1 versus above 0.4), and unmodified labels. A replay that confirms the hard-stratum prediction is still a finding. The update-after-failure test needs a new elicitation protocol (genuine, spurious, and placebo failures) and fits only as a small, hedged add-on.

## 5. L4: Per-requirement confidence and localization

Gros and Devanbu (preprint, first posted 2025-12-31, so not concurrent) occupy line- and token-level localization. Their labels are "Minimal Intent Aligning Patches" verified by hidden tests, on GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct across HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench, and RepoCod. For Qwen at line level (Table 4), token probability has BSS -1.18, ECE 0.54, and AUC 0.55; multisampling 0.01, 0.17, and 0.72; line-verbalized confidence -0.72, 0.43, and 0.71; and Probe-7B 0.29, 0.08, and 0.83. For the Qwen generator the probe reads the generator's own states, so four families have been compared on the same code at line level; for GPT-4o the "probe" reads a separate 0.5B or 7B supervisor [[localized-calibrated-uncertainty-in-code-language-models]].

FLARE localizes from generator token probabilities with 67% Top-1 accuracy and gains 1.72% to 7.42% absolute pass@1 over NL-Debugging, without a matched resampling arm (Table 4) [[flare-fine-grained-diagnostic-feedback-for-llm-code-refinement-2]]. PtTrust assesses line-level risk from internal states [[risk-assessment-framework-for-code-llms-via-leveraging-internal-states]]; agent-step probes decode test-pass, partial-correctness ("the number of failing tests is strictly less than at the beginning"), and regression labels with AUC up to 0.83 [[260705188-latent-programming-horizons-in-coding-agents-full-text]]; and TokenRepair loses 20.6% when its uncertainty-guided localization is removed [[enhancing-automated-program-repair-via-faulty-token-localization-and-quality-awa]]. Highlighting tokens by generation probability "does not provide any benefit over the baseline with no highlighting" [[generation-probabilities-are-not-enough-uncertainty-highlighting-in-ai-code-comp]]. Q4 is OCCUPIED.

Three residuals survive. Per-test-group confidence as localization of the failing requirement (Q1) is NARROW, because requirement units are specification-level and need no patch oracle. Targeted repair versus matched-budget regeneration (Q2) is NARROW: Olausson et al. find "pass rates are higher or equally high with i.i.d. sampling (without repair), especially when the budget is small" [[is-self-repair-a-silver-bullet-for-code-generation]], Siddiq et al. find calibration-guided repair gives "only limited improvements while frequently introducing functional regressions" [[an-empirical-study-of-security-calibration-in-large-language-models-for-code]], and Iscan finds blind resampling beating bare-code retry (discordant pairs 25 to 7, p=0.0021), with code-plus-facts feedback tied with resampling at 26 unlocks each [[falsification-not-exposure-placebo-controlled-decomposition-of-self-repair-feedb]].

FLARE and TokenRepair lack the matched arm, but the matched arm now exists with a negative result: in 2609.00854 (concurrent, ABSTRACT-ONLY), spectrum-based localization plus span infilling loses to blind resampling at a matched attempt count (3:40, p = 3.0 x 10^-9) across three 26-32B models and three benchmarks, against a random-span placebo [[260900854-does-fault-localization-beat-a-fresh-attempt-placebo-controlled-test-g]], and in small code models blind resampling beats or ties execution-feedback repair, with conditioning on the failed attempt costing 6.1 points at 1.5B [[try-again-dont-look-backblind-resampling-outperforms-self-repair-in-small-code-m]]. The Q2 residual shrinks to localization derived from the model's own per-requirement confidence, and the prior that it wins is lower. Even a strict reader who calls Q2 a union of FLARE and Olausson would face a repair-method comparison, not a calibration claim.

Self-written test adequacy against own confidence (Q3) is CLEAN (provisional): the nearest works measure adequacy without confidence, with 19.7% of LLM-written tests failing to execute and 15.6% of failures wrong-oracle [[261032548-beyond-coverage-and-mutation-a-blind-spot-analysis-of-llm-generated-un]], or weight tests by leave-one-out AUC without calibration [[aces-who-tests-the-tests-leave-one-outauc-consistency-for-code-generation]]. The closest work, ValTest, scores the validity of model-written tests from semantic-entropy features [[241108254-valtest-semantic-entropy-test-case-validation-full-text]], and CodeT uses self-written tests as an agreement signal [[codet-code-generation-with-generated-tests]]; neither relates the generator's confidence in its own program to the adequacy of its own tests, which is the residual.

L4 is NARROW but the wrong bet for a first TMLR paper, and what blocks it is readiness, not the literature. The per-test pipeline in `src/v4_dprc` is frozen legacy code never run beyond a stub, the test-group label unit is noisier than published patch labels, and Gros and Devanbu's leave-one-dataset-out collapse (Qwen Probe-7B BSS -0.01 on LiveCodeBench, Table 7) warns that fine-grained probes transfer badly. The paper should cite the line-level occupant, name the test-group unit as future work, and leave `src/v4_dprc` out.

Circularity decides the case. On code, the "internal reference" is a probe trained on the same execution labels it would referee, so any quantity defined relative to internal state collapses into a contrast of two ordinary resolutions. The one quantity beyond Murphy, an item-by-format variance component, is already a general-NLP statistic with no stated code reason to differ, so it fails A21.

The vocabulary is in use too. "Measurement validity" frames Ghosh's audit; "SDT is applied here as a measurement framework" appears in the signal-detection study; "instrument" appears in a reactivity argument that the rating question "displace[s] the probed state" [[dont-ask-understanding-what-numeric-self-reports-measure-in-language-models]]; and construct-validity framing already covers coding agents, with GPT-5 submitting 100% of tasks against a 44% resolve rate [[confident-and-wrong-silent-semantic-failures-in-coding-agents]].

Metrology-proper terms are in use as well, at conceptual depth. Zabolotnii, Holinko, and Antonenko (2604.26671, preprint, April 2026; no experiments) apply the VIM (International Vocabulary of Metrology) and GUM (Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement) terms measurand and traceability to clinical AI (Section 1) and treat stated-confidence calibration error as "a direct analog of the systematic error of a measuring instrument" (Section 10) [[260426671-from-black-box-confidence-to-measurable-trust-in-clinical-ai-full-text]]; TRACE maps agentic-AI trust metrics, Calibration Error among them, to GUM, VIM, and ISO 17025 [[trace-a-metrologically-grounded-engineering-frameworkfor-trustworthy-agentic-ai]]. The two formal metrology papers treat classifiers, one explicitly excluding generative ML [[a-metrological-framework-for-uncertainty-evaluation-in-machine-learning-classifi]] [[uncertainty-evaluation-bridging-machine-learning-and-measurement-science-full-te]]. Vocabulary already in use cannot be a contribution, and any exposition using "measurand" or "readout" must cite 2604.26671 and TRACE.

F is OCCUPIED. It earns a place as reporting discipline: separate bias, resolution, and noise, and report Murphy terms and Brier skill beside AUROC, which the L1 protocol already requires.

## 9. Ranked recommendation for TMLR

### 9.1 Rank 1: the controlled disconnect test on an L1 measurement layer, with an L3 replay

**Contribution sentence.** "On self-generated function-level code with execution labels, we test whether hidden-state probes carry correctness information that the model's own readouts lack. Every readout is also given a version trained on the same labels. Within-problem and between-problem discrimination are scored separately, and every ranking is checked against the calibration estimator, a stronger test oracle, and a per-model release-date split."

**Evidence the paper must generate.** Every number comes from the new pipeline; the legacy bundle supplies none.

1. **Multiple samples per problem:** at least 10, so that lambda in Bulut's Equation 1 is not zero, with at least 100 mixed-outcome problems per model and a problem-clustered paired bootstrap for every difference. The OpenCoder interval of [-0.0911, 0.0652] shows how wide uncontrolled intervals run; resolving differences near the 0.05 threshold of Section 9.5 likely needs about 400 or more problems per cell, so 100 mixed problems is a minimum, not a target. At per-problem pass rate p and k samples, the share of mixed problems is 1 − p^k − (1 − p)^k, an illustrative formula rather than a measured number, so hard benchmarks need more problems.
2. **Readouts on identical samples:** a last-token and a prompt-final probe; a trained own readout (verbal head or logit-feature head on the same execution labels); zero-shot numeric and categorical verbal confidence; token probability; per-candidate sampling agreement built only from tests disjoint from the label suite; and prompt-only and code-text-only classifiers. Validation-based checkpoint selection for every fitted reader, plus an isotonic-verbal control.
3. **The measurement layer:** pooled, within-problem, and between-problem AUROC; Brier skill with reliability intervals beside AUROC for every recalibrated readout, plus a covariate-only predictor; ECE under equal-width, equal-mass, and debiased estimators, with smooth ECE, TCE, and consistent proper-calibration-error estimators cited as foundations [[smooth-ece-principled-reliability-diagramsvia-kernel-smoothing]] [[tce-a-test-based-approach-to-measuring-calibration-error]] [[consistent-and-asymptotically-unbiased-estimation-of-proper-calibration-errors-f]], and an accuracy-matched comparison after ACE; labels under HumanEval and MBPP versus HumanEval+ and MBPP+; and LiveCodeBench windows split around each model's cutoff with a pre-cutoff comparison group.
4. **A provenance check on the occupant:** a sign-reversed or fitted likelihood baseline on Ribeiro's public data, to test how much of the reported margin the reference-versus-other-model design explains.
5. **An offline L3 routing replay** gated by elicited verbal or probe confidence over per-candidate execution budgets, with random and label-sorted oracle arms at matched budgets (a comparator that follows TRIAGE), stratified by pass rate.
6. **A pre-registered conditional extension:** at least three base and instruct pairs from different families, with a base-compatible readout (P(True)-style or few-shot numeric) and a guard requiring at least three distinct values; and a same-weights reasoning toggle (for example Qwen3 thinking on and off), with the probe read at prompt-final and answer-boundary positions and a prompt-length residualization control. Run only if the controlled gap clears the stated margin.

**TMLR fit.** Criterion 1 is met by construction (Section 3.6). A null says the published probe advantage on code is a supervised, between-problem effect, and that the model's own confidence, given the same labels, ranks its candidates as well as a probe; that would revise how Ribeiro, Openia, Di Cicco, and the Klassert thesis are read. A positive result says information is lost between state and output even under matched supervision. Criterion 2 is met because ML calibration and SE confidence audiences are both active (ICSE 2026, ISSTA 2026, Findings of ACL 2026, UncertaiNLP 2026, COLM 2026, and many concurrent preprints). Novelty is claimed only for the controlled test and the protocol, written as "we found no prior code study that ..." with the instrument gaps named.

**Main risks.** A scoop is the first: Campos et al. already recommend within-problem reporting, and their group holds the models, probes, and labels, so a trained own-readout arm is all it needs [[abstract]]. Even then, the verbal arms, oracle swap, and release contrast would stand. The second is that the trained arm closes the gap, as CREDO and Probe-SD suggest; the mitigation is to frame that result as the finding (outcome β). The third is verbal collapse on small instruct models, as in Tuerlings; report collapse as a validity outcome with a distinct-values guard and rely on the trained head for the matched comparison.

### 9.2 Rank 2: the L1 label-oracle and estimator protocol alone

**Contribution sentence.** "The ranking of four confidence families on code does, or does not, change when benchmark labels are replaced by augmented-test labels, when the estimator moves from equal-width ECE to equal-mass and debiased ECE, Brier skill, and AUROC, and when problems are split around each model's release cutoff." This is the fallback if the controlled test is scooped or the probe arms prove infeasible. It claims novelty only for the protocol (NARROW) and for Q10 and Q13 (CLEAN (provisional)), never for the comparison.

**Evidence.** Items 1 to 3 of Section 9.1, with the oracle swap, the estimator set, and the release split as the core.

**TMLR fit.** Good on criterion 1 (factual, checkable claims) and modest on criterion 2 (mainly code-model evaluators).

**Main risk.** A null: Functional Entropy reports calibration "broadly consistent with the AUROC findings," so concordant rankings are the default expectation (Section 2.5). Liu et al. predict the direction of any oracle effect, and invariant rankings would be a robustness finding, but a less interesting one.

### 9.3 Rank 3: stated-confidence update after a shown failing test

**Contribution sentence.** "We measure whether a code model's stated confidence changes after it is shown a genuinely failing test, a spuriously failing test, or a placebo, and whether the change predicts repair success."

**Evidence.** Paired programs in each arm, following Resist and Update's evidence-versus-pressure design and Iscan's placebo logic, with the change in stated confidence, its AUROC against repair success, and problem-clustered intervals.

**TMLR fit.** Criterion 1 is good for a small, well-controlled study; criterion 2 is moderate, mainly the agent community.

**Main risk.** It has the strongest code-specific reason of any component and the highest instrument exposure. It fits as a small, hedged section of the Rank 1 paper if compute allows, and should rise only after 2610.19226 is read in full and completed forward screens and a working full-text search find no occupant.

### 9.4 Rank 4: L4, out; F as exposition

L4 is NARROW but needs a new pipeline and an operator scope decision, adds a repair-method claim rather than a calibration claim, and its repair residual has a lower prior after 2609.00854; as Section 5 argues, the blocking risk is readiness, not occupancy. F is OCCUPIED; use its vocabulary as exposition with 2604.26671 and TRACE cited, report Murphy terms and meta-I, and claim nothing for it.

### 9.5 What would overturn this ranking

- A pilot showing a within-problem probe margin above 0.05 over the trained readout, with an interval excluding zero, would revive "locating the disconnect" as a positive spine and promote the factorial extension.
- A trained own readout that still trails the probe by at least 0.08 within-problem AUROC would make the contradiction with Probe-SD the headline.
- A working full-text search that finds no occupant for the update-after-failure test would raise Rank 3.
- A hidden occupant behind the gaps, combined with rejection of the A21 code reasons, would push the factorial and routing components to OCCUPIED, but Rank 1 would survive as a claims-reduced study with a correct-by-construction design.

### 9.6 Forward-looking implications

The field is converging on one measurement correction from several directions. Bulut's identity, the agent-difficulty decompositions [[disentangling-task-difficulty-from-run-level-failure-in-agent-failure-prediction]] [[hard-issue-weak-model-or-bad-runrethinking-failure-prediction-for-coding-agents]] [[continue-or-restart-the-limits-of-early-failure-prediction-in-coding-agents]], Campos's within-problem recommendation, and Liu's oracle-independence rule all say that most published confidence numbers measure the problem and the oracle, not the candidate. Reviewers at ML and SE venues are likely to start asking for within-problem scores and oracle swaps as a matter of course, much as EvalPlus made augmented tests standard for accuracy. Meanwhile training-side work (CREDO, Probe-SD, RLCR) is moving from calibrating the verbal channel toward improving its discrimination, so the first code paper to put a trained readout beside a probe will occupy the controlled test. The window is real but short, and speed of submission is itself a positioning variable. If trained readouts close the gap, the practical message for tool builders is to train the output channel on execution labels rather than ship probes, and confidence-routed execution becomes a question about trained heads. If the gap survives within problem under matched supervision, the case for white-box confidence in code tools becomes far stronger than current evidence supports, and the factorial decomposition is the natural follow-up.
