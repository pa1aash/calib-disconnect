  review synthesizing the emerging ''LLM Psychometrics'' field (applying psychometric
  theory/instruments -- IRT, personality/values inventories, validity theory -- to

  scope, methodology refinement, result validation, and capability enhancement. LOAD-BEARING
  GAP STATEMENT (corroborated by a parallel WebSearch synthesis of this and adjacent
  papers): most LLM-IRT work to date uses only standard unidimensional models (1PL/2PL/3PL)
  treating benchmark items independently; polytomous, hierarchical, and nested/multidimensional
  response models -- the psychometric machinery designed for exactly the ''testlet''
  structure where several correlated sub-items (here: unit-test-level requirements)
  share a single stem/context (here: one coding task/prompt) -- remain largely unexplored
  in the LLM evaluation literature. This is the single strongest piece of evidence
  that requirement-level (sub-task, testlet-structured) IRT applied to code benchmarks
  is an open methodological gap rather than reinvented prior art.'

Abstract:
The advancement of large language models (LLMs) has outpaced traditional evaluation methodologies. This progress presents novel challenges, such as measuring human-like psychological constructs, moving beyond static and task-specific benchmarks, and establishing human-centered evaluation. These challenges intersect with psychometrics, the science of quantifying the intangible aspects of human psychology, such as personality, values, and intelligence. This review paper introduces and synthesizes the emerging interdisciplinary field of LLM Psychometrics, which leverages psychometric instruments, theories, and principles to evaluate, understand, and enhance LLMs. The reviewed literature systematically shapes benchmarking principles, broadens evaluation scopes, refines methodologies, validates results, and advances LLM capabilities. Diverse perspectives are integrated to provide a structured framework for researchers across disciplines, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of this nascent field. Ultimately, the review provides actionable insights for developing future evaluation paradigms that align with human-level AI and promote the advancement of human-centered AI systems for societal benefit.
