summary: 'Position paper (Karl, Kemeter, Dax, Sierak, arXiv Jun 2024, unpublished
  venue-wise per available metadata) arguing that ML publications are over-indexed
  on beating SOTA predictive-performance benchmarks, which discourages publishing
  negative results (method fails to beat baselines) even though such results carry
  scientific value (identifying unsolved problems, preventing redundant effort). Argues
  null-hypothesis framing (''method does not significantly outperform existing methods
  on a relevant problem subset'') is itself informative and proposes concrete community
  measures (dedicated negative-results tracks/venues) to normalize publishing such
  findings. Directly relevant to DPRC''s framing question: this paper argues a ''doesn''t
  work here'' finding is scientifically valuable in principle, but also implicitly
  concedes such papers currently struggle for acceptance at mainstream top venues
  absent dedicated tracks -- meaning DPRC''s competitive-code collapse cannot stand
  alone as a contribution; it needs to be paired with the positive functional-code
  result and a mechanistic explanation (all-or-nothing vs partial-credit correctness)
  to be reviewer-legible as a scope-condition finding rather than a bare negative
  result.'

Abstract:
Publications proposing novel machine learning methods are often primarily rated by exhibited predictive performance on selected problems. In this position paper we argue that predictive performance alone is not a good indicator for the worth of a publication. Using it as such even fosters problems like inefficiencies of the machine learning research community as a whole and setting wrong incentives for researchers. We therefore put out a call for the publication of "negative" results, which can help alleviate some of these problems and improve the scientific output of the machine learning research community. To substantiate our position, we present the advantages of publishing negative results and provide concrete measures for the community to move towards a paradigm where their publication is normalized.
