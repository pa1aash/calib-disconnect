# Confidence estimation for code language models against execution ground truth: occupancy, residuals, and the strongest TMLR contribution

**Executive summary.** The headline that a hidden-state probe knows more about code correctness than the model says is taken. Ribeiro, Spiess, Devanbu, and Nadi (ICSE 2026) show that a fitted probe beats likelihood and zero-shot verbal confidence at whole-solution granularity for four 7-8B instruct models, by +13.3 to +29.0 points on BigCodeBench and +8.7 to +26.2 points on HumanEval (Section 4.2.1) [[on-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]]. A paper that leads with that claim would incorrectly claim novelty, which TMLR's editorial policy names as grounds for rejection. What is not taken is the controlled form of the claim, and the evidence says the controlled form is the more interesting one. The uncontrolled margin is plausibly inflated by three documented confounds: between-problem difficulty, a fitted probe set against zero-shot readouts, and a design that contrasts human reference solutions with other models' failures. Every code comparison that scores a probe against the model's own confidence within problem on self-generated samples finds a tie or a small, model-dependent margin [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]] [[a-heckler-in-the-hidden-statecorrectness-signals-in-diffusion-language-models]]. And a concurrent code paper, CREDO, now shows that training stated confidence on execution labels raises its AUROC from 0.690 to 0.850, though with no probe arm [[beyond-verbalized-confidence-calibrating-reasoners-with-differentiable-readouts]].

The strongest defensible TMLR contribution is therefore one execution-graded measurement paper. Its headline is a controlled test: a hidden-state probe against supervision-matched own readouts (including trained stated confidence), zero-shot verbal confidence, token probability, and per-candidate sampling agreement, with input-only baselines, scored pooled and within problem on the model's own samples. The test rests on an L1 sensitivity protocol (estimator set, original-versus-augmented test oracle, per-model release-date split) and carries an offline L3 routing replay as a secondary analysis. The base-versus-instruct and reasoning decompositions become a conditional extension, run only if the controlled gap is measurably non-zero. F supplies vocabulary only; L4 and the legacy `src/v4_dprc` pipeline stay out. The design is publishable whichever way its headline falls, which is exactly what TMLR's first criterion rewards.

Verdicts in brief:

- **L1:** OCCUPIED (union) as a comparison; NARROW as a protocol; test insufficiency (Q10) and contamination effects on calibration (Q13) CLEAN (provisional).
- **L2:** OCCUPIED as an uncontrolled headline; NARROW as the controlled test, the factorial decomposition, and trained stated confidence on code.
- **L3:** NARROW for routing by elicited confidence; CLEAN (provisional) for confidence updating after a shown failing test; OCCUPIED for selective prediction and repository-agent uncertainty.
- **L4:** NARROW, with line-level localization OCCUPIED.
- **F:** OCCUPIED as a framing; its vocabulary is used, not contested.

## 1. Scope, instruments, and verdict rules

### 1.1 What is judged, and under which rules

The question covers January 2022 to November 2026, with emphasis on 2025 and 2026, and asks which confidence-estimation contribution for code language models, graded by execution (pass or fail on test suites), remains open and defensible at TMLR. Five candidates are judged. L1 is an execution-graded measurement foundation with release-date contamination control. L2 locates the disconnect between hidden-state probes (classifiers fit on a model's internal activations) and the model's verbal and sampled readouts. L3 is verification utility: confidence-routed execution budgets, and whether stated confidence updates after an observed test failure. L4 is per-requirement confidence, localization, and targeted repair. F is a measurement-instrument, or metrology, framing of calibration.

Each candidate and component receives one of five verdicts under the brief's definitions:

- **OCCUPIED:** a fetched work makes the core claim at comparable breadth on every dimension the claim names.
- **OCCUPIED (union):** at most three fetched works together cover every dimension, and the only remaining step is running them on the same problems (amendment A20).
- **NARROW:** a precisely stated residual survives the residual rule (amendment A21). Clause 1 requires the residual to be a new factor, granularity, label protocol, or use, not merely a change of models or benchmarks. Clause 2 requires a stated reason the code result could differ from the closest general-NLP result.
- **CLEAN:** the search conditions are met and nothing relevant was found.
- **CLEAN (provisional):** CLEAN except for an instrument gap that bears on the claim.

General-NLP work counts only as the comparison side of a residual, and only for L2 and F. Pre-2022 metric works are foundations, not occupants. Status tags follow amendment A23: published, preprint only, or concurrent, where "concurrent" means first posted after 2026-05-10. That six-month window is a proposal awaiting the operator's confirmation. Verdicts rest on full texts, cited by section, table, or equation. ABSTRACT-ONLY marks the one load-bearing record whose vault copy is an abstract: NeuroSym-Cal, whose section-level facts come from a separate read of the ACL Anthology PDF [[neurosym-cal-bridging-the-reasoning-execution-gap-in-code-generation-via-hierarc]].

### 1.2 TMLR's two criteria as design constraints

TMLR asks two questions. First, are the claims supported by accurate, convincing, and clear evidence? This is "the most important criterion," and authors may meet it by choosing to "adjust (reduce) their claims." Second, would some of the TMLR audience be interested? Reviewers are told: "Do not penalize modest significance or lack of novelty," and papers "should be accepted if they meet the criteria, even if the contribution or significance of the work is modest." The editorial policy adds two grounds for rejection: papers that "incorrectly claim novelty over existing published work," and papers that "merely re-implement an idea that has already been reproduced before."

Read as an engineering specification, these rules demand three properties of the winning contribution: a claim whose evidence the paper generates itself; a framing honest about Ribeiro and the concurrent citers; and a result that is informative whether it comes out positive or null. Two further consequences matter for every verdict below. At TMLR, OCCUPIED constrains wording, not acceptance: an occupied component can appear as a replication or baseline. And because the novelty clause names "existing published work," concurrent preprints bear mainly on the accuracy of claims rather than on the novelty clause. A paper that ignored Campos et al. would not falsely claim novelty over published work, but a sentence saying "no study reports within-problem AUROC for code probes" would simply be false.

### 1.3 Instruments, and why every CLEAN is provisional

The search instruments failed in specific, documented ways. arXiv full-text search failed validation: `searchtype=ft` is silently ignored and the full-text host is off the network allowlist. OpenAlex full-text search found one of two known positives, and its daily budget ran out at about 05:05 IST. Semantic Scholar keyword search returned HTTP 429 on every attempt. The arXiv export API returned HTTP 429 to several queries, and OpenReview forum pages and PDFs sit behind a browser challenge. Every negative that depends on body-text search is therefore an instrument gap, never a zero, and no CLEAN verdict in this report is unconditional. Section 10 lists each gap, the verdicts it weakens, and the action that would close it.

### 1.4 Four switches that decide contested verdicts

Several verdicts flip depending on judgment calls that the literature cannot settle. Four switches capture them:

| Switch | Setting A | Setting B | Components it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 concurrency window | Preprints after 2026-05-10 count as occupants | They count only as related work | L1 Q4; L2 Q2a, Q11, Q13; L3 Q1, Q3; the evidence for the expected size of the controlled gap |
| S2 instrument gaps | A gap is read as absence | A gap is read as a possibly hidden occupant | Every CLEAN (provisional) cell |
| S3 A21 code-specific reason | Accepted | Rejected | The L2 factorial residuals (Q2a, Q2b, Q4, Q8); L4 Q1 |
| S4 controlled-test outcome | Probe keeps a within-problem margin over a trained own readout | Tie, or the trained readout closes the gap | Which L2 headline is true; whether the factorial and routing arms are worth running |

S1 through S3 are judgments about the literature; S4 is empirical and is the only switch the paper can set itself. The concurrency switch does not simply turn occupancy up or down. The within-problem ties that most weaken the probe headline (Campos et al., Miglani et al.) and the difficulty identity that explains them (Bulut) are all concurrent [[its-the-problem-not-the-pathbudget-and-difficulty-confounds-in-llm-reasoning-tra]]. Excluding concurrent work widens the residual but strips the paper of its published reason to expect a small controlled gap, and a reviewer will know those papers regardless. This report therefore treats concurrent works as occupants and records where a verdict would move without them. The one contribution recommended in Section 9 stays NARROW under every setting of S1 through S3.

Two definitional choices hide inside the rules. If verbal P(True) counts as "stated confidence," the L1 four-family union is easy to assemble; if only numeric or categorical verbal confidence counts, the stated family on code is covered only by Ribeiro (two zero-shot formats), Tuerlings and Valdenegro-Toro (verbal only), and Gros and Devanbu (line level) [[verbalized-uncertainty-in-coding-llms-contamination-aware-calibration-uncertainl]] [[localized-calibrated-uncertainty-in-code-language-models]]. And if L1's core claim were read as granularity-free, Gros and Devanbu would nearly occupy it alone, since their Tables 3 and 4 compare token probability, multisampling, line-verbalized confidence, and probes with BSS (Brier skill score), ECE (expected calibration error), and AUC on the same code at line level. This report reads L1 as program-level, which keeps Gros and Devanbu as the L4 occupant and an L1 neighbor; the paper must still state that a four-family comparison exists at line level, or a reviewer will read the omission as overclaimed novelty.

## 2. L1: Execution-graded measurement foundation

### 2.1 The four-family comparison is occupied by union

L1's core claim is that comparing sampling agreement, token probability, stated confidence, and hidden-state probes against execution labels shows which methods track correctness on code, across three or more model families, two or more scales, and three or more benchmarks. As a comparison, this claim is OCCUPIED (union), and the union does not depend on concurrent work.

NeuroSym-Cal (Findings of ACL 2026; vault ABSTRACT-ONLY, full text read from the PDF) covers token probability (AvgLogit and Platt), verbal P(True) and self-verification, sampling agreement (self-consistency and AST consensus over K=10 samples), and a multicalibration baseline (IGLB). It runs DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct on LiveCodeBench v2406 to v2506 (400 problems), HumanEval-Pro, and DS-1000 (Sections 3, 4.1, 4.2). It reports ECE "by 37.4% compared to Self-Consistency (0.072 vs. 0.115)" for DeepSeek-R1 and LiveCodeBench AUROC 0.805 against 0.798 for IGLB (Section 5.1). It has no probe [[neurosym-cal-bridging-the-reasoning-execution-gap-in-code-generation-via-hierarc]]. Ribeiro et al. (published) supply probe, verbal, and likelihood readouts, so the two published works already span all four families [[on-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]]. Campos, Klassert, and Ulges (concurrent) add a third route: Yes/No token confidence, EigenScore sampling, linear probes and LAT, and causal DAS-based predictors on Qwen3-Coder-30B and gpt-oss-20b, with a problem-clustered bootstrap (Sections III to VI) [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]].

Breadth occupants fill in the rest. Code-MUE (ISSTA 2026) covers eight models and four benchmarks with a single execution-agreement family [[code-mue-measuring-code-llms-uncertainty-through-execution-based-semantic-intera]]. Functional Entropy covers five closed models over LiveCodeBench, MultiPL-E Java, and LiveSQLBench with token, sampling, and reflexive scorers (Section 4.1, Table 1) [[functional-entropy-predicting-functional-correctness-in-llm-generated-code-with]]. Rathnasuriya and Yang (concurrent) evaluate 17 generation-stage and 6 prefill-stage token and sampling metrics on four instruct models, with best AUC 0.598 to 0.696 (Section 4, Table 3) [[characterizing-overconfident-failure-in-llm-based-code-generation-full-text]]. Siddiq et al. run four families on three models with Docker execution labels, reporting verbalized security ECE from 0.247 to 0.481 [[an-empirical-study-of-security-calibration-in-large-language-models-for-code]]. The sampling family is especially crowded: execution-clustered agreement reaches ensemble AUROC 0.776 against 0.696 for the best natural-language baseline [[code-is-more-than-text-uncertainty-estimation-for-code-generation]], semantic-distance estimators exceed AUROC 0.8 in every setting [[using-semantic-distance-to-estimate-uncertainty-in-llm-based-code-generation]], and ensemble semantic entropy reaches accuracy 0.8247 against 0.6420 for single-model semantic entropy at 10% false-positive rate [[ensemble-based-uncertainty-estimation-for-code-correctness-estimation]]. Test-free agreement and execution or symbolic clustering (Q7, Q8) are OCCUPIED, as are self-judgment as confidence (Q12) and release-window benchmarks used for calibration (Q9).

Nobody needs another code-confidence leaderboard. A paper whose claim is "we compared the four families" would re-implement a union that published works already span, and it would also have to beat NeuroSym-Cal's breadth on the families they share.

### 2.2 What is not occupied: the instrument under the comparison

The open territory is whether family rankings survive changes to the measuring instrument. Three controls matter, and each nearest occupant holds at most one of them.

**Estimator (Q5).** Tuerlings and Valdenegro-Toro (UncertaiNLP 2026) is the only code study showing a ranking change by metric. In Table 1, the lowest ECE (0.013, CodeLlama-7B Linguistic) comes with AUROC 0.491, while the highest AUROC (0.841, Qwen-32B Linguistic) comes with ECE 0.216; the authors state that ECE and AUROC "do not correlate across cells," and 8 of 18 cells collapsed (Section 4.6) [[verbalized-uncertainty-in-coding-llms-contamination-aware-calibration-uncertainl]]. But the paper uses ten equal-width bins (Section 3.5), and no code study compares equal-mass, debiased, smooth, or kernel estimators. Gros and Devanbu flag ECE as misleadingly low under bucket collapse and prefer Brier skill [[localized-calibrated-uncertainty-in-code-language-models]]. Outside code, Ghosh et al. show verbal confidence "looks well calibrated because it clusters near the base rate while discriminating poorly" [[measurement-validity-and-representation-dynamics-ofconfidence-signals-in-large-l]]. The metric-decoupling fact is NARROW; the estimator-variant sub-part is CLEAN (provisional).

**Label oracle (Q10).** EvalPlus showed that 80 times more tests cut pass@k by 19.3% to 28.9% across 26 models and that test insufficiency can mis-rank models [[is-your-code-generated-by-chatgpt-really-correct-rigorous-evaluation-of-large-la]]. SWE-ABS strengthens 50.2% of 500 SWE-bench Verified instances, rejects 19.78% of previously passing patches, moves the top agent from 78.80% to 62.20%, and produces 30 rank changes in the top 30 [[swe-abs-adversarial-benchmark-strengthening-exposes-inflated-success-rates-on-te]]. Liu et al. (concurrent) hold the oracle-swap result for confidence, but in text-to-SQL: swapping the shipped database for a multi-instance test suite raises held-out conformal risk by 2.73 to 10.23 points, and under blinded expert labels a certificate at nominal 0.10 carries 20.0 and 17.2 points of risk [[certified-against-which-oracleexecution-labels-set-the-reported-riskof-conformal]]. All of these rank models, agents, or certificates. No code study recomputes a confidence-method ranking under original versus augmented tests; Rathnasuriya and Yang evaluate only on the already-strengthened HumanEval+ and MBPP+ (Section 3.1) and concede that strengthened suites "may still miss untested" behavior [[characterizing-overconfident-failure-in-llm-based-code-generation-full-text]]. Q10 is CLEAN (provisional).

**Contamination (Q3, Q13).** NeuroSym-Cal holds out a per-model post-cutoff LiveCodeBench subset (Section 4.1). Tuerlings uses one shared window, 2024-09-19 to 2025-04-30, for the verbal family only (Section 3.1). Campos, Kuschnereit, and Ulges take LiveCodeBench release_v6 (1,055 problems, May 2023 to April 2025) as a whole-benchmark leakage control [[multicalibration-for-llm-based-code-generation]]. ExeCRE checks that its 182-problem window is cutoff-clean for Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct (cutoff 2024-06) and LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (2023-12) but not for GPT-5.2 (2025-08) [[execre-execution-consistency-guided-reliability-estimation-for-self-correcting-c]]. Campos et al. use 454 LiveCodeBench problems "released between October 2024 and May 2025" for language shift, not as a contamination contrast [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]]. The Klassert thesis says it "prioritizes ... over strict contamination control" (Section 4.1) [[abstract]], and Lugoloobi et al. use release-date splits to predict accuracy, not to contrast calibration [[llms-encode-their-failures-predicting-success-from-pre-generation-activations]]. LiveCodeBench supplies the windows that make a contrast possible [[livecodebench-holistic-and-contamination-free-evaluation-of-large-language-model-2]], but no fetched work compares calibration or discrimination on pre-cutoff versus post-cutoff problems. A held-out window is hygiene; a contrast is a measurement. A screen of 2,100 LiveCodeBench citers by title and abstract found no such contrast. Q3 is NARROW and Q13 CLEAN (provisional).

### 2.3 Label independence is a live defect

Label independence sounds pedantic until it fails in print. Code-MUE's AUROC label "Solved" means at least one of the N sampled candidates passes all tests, so the label is computed from the same samples whose agreement the method scores (Section 5.2.1) [[code-mue-measuring-code-llms-uncertainty-through-execution-based-semantic-intera]]. Liu et al. name the general mechanism: "Every execution-consistency score looks better under the labels of the oracle that built its clusters, in 16 of 16 combinations," hence "a consistency score should be evaluated under an oracle that did not build it" [[certified-against-which-oracleexecution-labels-set-the-reported-riskof-conformal]]. Good practice exists but is never tested as a factor. Tuerlings freezes code before elicitation so that "correctness labels are determined independently of any confidence estimation" (Section 3.3) [[verbalized-uncertainty-in-coding-llms-contamination-aware-calibration-uncertainl]], and Code Is More Than Text keeps self-generated tests apart from the label suite "since otherwise the functional signal would be trivially perfect by construction" [[code-is-more-than-text-uncertainty-estimation-for-code-generation]].

The strict objection is that this is Liu's mechanism moved from SQL to Python, a benchmark change that fails A21 clause 1. The answer is that code supplies direct evidence the SQL paper does not: on the semantic-distance estimator, fuzz inputs seeded from benchmark tests raise agreement AUROC by 0.014 to 0.087 over seed-free inputs (Table 6) [[using-semantic-distance-to-estimate-uncertainty-in-llm-based-code-generation]]. Because benchmark tests drive both the label and every test-execution agreement score, a thin suite produces false passes on plausible but wrong programs and inflates exactly the families that track test-style agreement. A base-versus-augmented relabeling could therefore reorder families, not just shift pass rates. Q4 is NARROW; it would move toward CLEAN (provisional) if Liu et al. were excluded as concurrent.

### 2.4 Recalibration separates mapping from information

Rathnasuriya and Yang show the core fact on code: five-fold Platt and isotonic maps cut CodeLlama's Brier score from 0.459 to 0.248 while BSS stays at about 0.001 (Section 6.1, Table 5) [[characterizing-overconfident-failure-in-llm-based-code-generation-full-text]]. A monotone map cannot change AUROC, so recalibration fixes calibration and never discrimination. Multicalibration (calibration enforced across intersecting subgroups) lifts LiveCodeBench BSS from -0.543 for uncalibrated Qwen3 to 0.480 under IGLB [[multicalibration-for-llm-based-code-generation]], and the Klassert thesis reports a Qwen3-Coder probe at BSS 0.5888 against 0.4680 for IGLB (Table 5.7) [[abstract]]. But the multicalibration gain comes from group features (complexity, code length, prompt length, language), which are problem-level covariates outside the model's readout, so part of the reported skill is difficulty information. Recalibration as a control is OCCUPIED at token level and at line level (Q6), and multicalibration is OCCUPIED for token likelihood (Q11). Across families both are NARROW, and they impose two requirements on the design: report AUROC beside BSS for every recalibrated readout, and include a covariate-only predictor so that a recalibration gain is not credited to the readout.

### 2.5 The strict reading, and the verdict

A strict reader rates every L1 control OCCUPIED separately: the release split is "a family added to an occupied control," estimator variants are "a metric change," recalibration is "a fact of mathematics." On that reading the protocol is merely bundling, and A20 discounts "running them on the same problems." The objection is right that bundling alone is not novelty. It misses why the bundle matters. The same instrument fragilities that could reorder an L1 leaderboard would also move every L2 and L3 number: label flips under HumanEval+ and MBPP+ change which candidates count as discordant pairs, and collapsed verbal distributions change both ECE and within-problem AUROC. An L2 headline reported without the L1 controls invites a reviewer to ask whether it survives a stronger oracle, and the paper would have no answer.

L1 is therefore OCCUPIED (union) as a comparison and NARROW as a protocol, with Q10 and Q13 CLEAN (provisional). The protocol belongs as the paper's measurement layer, not as a standalone benchmark. Q10 and Q13 are cheap to answer inside the same design and give the paper two findings that hold whatever the probe result. If rankings prove invariant to all three controls, the paper should report "rankings are stable under these controls" rather than drop them.

## 3. L2: Locating the disconnect between internal state and output

### 3.1 The uncontrolled headline is occupied, and firmly

Ribeiro et al. fit a Representation Engineering direction (LAT: a principal-component direction over differences of last-token hidden states for correct and incorrect code) and use it to pick the correct implementation among four candidates. The models are Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct, OpenCoder-8B-Instruct, and CodeLlama-7B-Instruct, on HumanEval (151 tasks) and BigCodeBench (457 tasks). Section 4.2.1 reports that "LAT consistently outperforms all baselines" by the margins quoted above; the takeaway box says +13.3 to +28.8 points, a small internal inconsistency. In-distribution LAT accuracy on BigCodeBench is 50.3% to 56.3% against 27.5% random for Mistral and 23.7% to 41.1% for verbal baselines (Table 3). The verbal readouts are zero-shot (a seven-level scale and the True/False probability), and their formats disagree: Mistral scores 40.6% with the seven-level format and 15.3% with True/False on HumanEval [[on-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]].

L2 Q1 is OCCUPIED by Ribeiro alone for probe versus verbal at whole-solution granularity, and OCCUPIED (union) with Campos et al. once sampling is added. Prior art makes this firmer. Openia trained last-token, last-layer probes on DeepSeek-Coder, CodeLlama, and Magicoder, with "up to a 2X improvement in standalone code generation and a 3X enhancement in repository-specific scenarios" [[correctness-assessment-of-code-generated-by-large-language-models-using-internal]]. AutoProbe's attention-weighted probe reaches 0.63 to 0.71 functionality accuracy against 0.32 to 0.61 for the generator judging itself (Table 5) [[model-agnostic-correctness-assessment-for-llm-generated-code-via-dynamic-interna]]. Probes for code correctness, their transfer, and other white-box readouts (Q5 to Q7) are OCCUPIED by these works, by Campos et al. under shift, by sparse-autoencoder (SAE) directions [[mechanistic-interpretability-of-code-correctness-in-llms-via-sparse-autoencoders]], by a pre-generation probe [[code-correctness-is-linearly-decodable-from-llm-hidden-states-before-generation]], and by diffusion-model probes [[probing-functional-correctness-in-diffusion-language-models-full-text]]. A paper whose L2 contribution is "the probe-verbal gap on more models or benchmarks" fails both A21 clause 1 and TMLR's re-implementation clause.

### 3.2 Three confounds between the occupant's margin and "unexpressed knowledge"

The occupied claim is real, but it is a fact about a supervised reader, not yet evidence that the model holds information it does not express. Three confounds, each documented in a fetched full text, sit between Ribeiro's numbers and that stronger claim.

**Difficulty.** Bulut's Equation 1 makes the diagnosis exact: pooled AUROC = lambda × within-problem AUROC + (1 − lambda) × between-problem AUROC, where lambda is the share of discordant pairs drawn from the same problem. "Every question-only predictor has AUROC_within = 1/2 exactly," and "in the standard design with one attempt per problem (k_c = 1), lambda = 0." Bulut's reconstruction of a published early-window probe on MATH gives pooled AUROC 0.849 [0.735, 0.919] but within-problem 0.496 (Section 4.4) [[its-the-problem-not-the-pathbudget-and-difficulty-confounds-in-llm-reasoning-tra]]. At function-level scale, a balanced design of 164 problems with 10 samples each (5 pass, 5 fail) puts only about 0.6% of pairs within the same problem, so a pooled code AUROC is about 99% a between-problem number. The agent literature reports more than 99.93% cross-unit pairs, with a run-blind difficulty oracle reaching AUROC 0.945 [[disentangling-task-difficulty-from-run-level-failure-in-agent-failure-prediction]]. On code the effect is measured directly: a Campos et al. probe at the last prompt token, which sees only the problem, has pooled AUROC 0.891 and within-problem 0.514, and the authors conclude that "much of what the methods predict is thus the problem, not the candidate" (Table V) [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]]. On LiveCodeBench a TF-IDF classifier on prompt text recovers 0.84 to 0.86 of the 0.90 to 0.91 probe AUROC for Qwen2.5-Coder and beats the probe for R1-Distill (Table 2; column mapping read from the header) [[llms-encode-their-failures-predicting-success-from-pre-generation-activations]]. Di Cicco's pre-generation probe reaches 0.881 ± 0.008, or 0.842 after residualizing on prompt length against a 0.657 length baseline, but it scores one attempt per task, so by Equation 1 it carries zero within-problem information [[code-correctness-is-linearly-decodable-from-llm-hidden-states-before-generation]].

The same identity resolves the dispute over whether verbal confidence on code is useful or near chance. Tuerlings elicits confidence on one greedy generation per problem, and Functional Entropy scores one original response per prompt [[functional-entropy-predicting-functional-correctness-in-llm-generated-code-with]], so Qwen-32B's AUROC of 0.841 and the verbal range of 0.597 to 0.795 are between-problem numbers: they measure whether the model knows which problems it fails, at strict pass rates of 8.22% to 29.86% in Tuerlings. Ribeiro's near-chance reflective readouts (about 7% for Qwen on HumanEval against 25% chance) come from a within-task selection. Both sides are right about different components. No fetched code study reports within-problem AUROC for numeric verbal confidence on self-generated samples, which is the single most important gap for this project.

**Supervision asymmetry.** Ribeiro fits LAT on 10% of tasks and validates on another 10% (Section 4.1.2), while the verbal readouts are zero-shot. Liu, Casper, Hadfield-Menell, and Andreas named this asymmetry in 2023: "probes are trained many-shot while queries use the model zero-shot." When they fine-tuned the queried model, probes were 1.7% more accurate on BoolQ, but queries were 6.9% and 4.0% more accurate on SciQ and CREAK (Section 3); the winner flipped on two of three datasets [[cognitive-dissonance-why-do-language-model-outputs-disagree-with-internal-repres]]. On code, the only partial control is Gros and Devanbu's Platt scaling of every readout. Platt cannot change AUC, so the Qwen line-level gap (probe 0.83 against line-verbalized 0.71) survives it, but no trained verbal head is present [[localized-calibrated-uncertainty-in-code-language-models]].

**Provenance.** Ribeiro's correct class is the human reference solution, and the three incorrect candidates come from other models (Llama-3.3-70B, GPT-4o, and Gemini 2.0 Flash on BigCodeBench; Section 4.1.1). The intrinsic readout picks the reference only "around 5-6%" of the time on BigCodeBench against 25% chance, so model likelihood is strongly anti-correlated with this label, and any supervised reader that learns the sign wins. The authors' follow-up names the confound ("variable names or implementation style"), finds that a direction fit on fault-isolating mutation pairs "separates them with high accuracy in isolation, yet does not transfer," with a 37.1-point accuracy drop on BigCodeBench against 5.2 on HumanEval, and concludes that "no single configuration is best, and that isolating the fault does not help" [[on-the-robustness-of-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]]. Ribeiro's cleaner test, RQ2, ranks ten self-generated samples within a task against zero-shot verbal confidence. There Mistral reaches 41.3% at rank 1 on HumanEval against 34.7% pass@1 and 35.5% for RankEF, and "only a few comparisons reached statistical significance" [[on-llms-internal-representation-of-code-correctness]].

### 3.3 What the controlled comparisons that exist say

Three comparisons on code score a probe against the model's own output confidence within problem on self-generated samples. All three tie or favor the readout.

- Miglani et al. (Section 4.2, Table 1) find a DiffuCoder-7B probe at 0.842 against negative token entropy at 0.838 (19 mixed problems), and a Qwen2.5-Coder probe at 0.677 against completion log-probability at 0.703 (56 mixed problems, 448 attempts). Their reading: "Model confidence is thus a competitive baseline ... Reading hidden states provides little apparent gain" [[a-heckler-in-the-hidden-statecorrectness-signals-in-diffusion-language-models]].
- Campos et al. (Section VI-C) report that "under language shift, within-problem AUROC lies at 0.58-0.64 for all methods and both models," against pooled 0.67 to 0.81, and that "for Qwen3-Coder confidence performs best within problem (0.629)," with the probe at the last code token at 0.620 [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]].

The exception matters. For gpt-oss in distribution, Campos's trained methods reach 0.69 to 0.78 within problem, an advantage lost under shift, and a probe on the causally localized subspace stays 0.09 AUROC ahead of output confidence for Qwen3-Coder under language shift (0.11 in distribution), though this does not generalize to gpt-oss or benchmark shift. The strongest supervision-matched probe edge on code comes from diffusion models, where a probe beats supervised logistic regression on entropy (Dream 0.879 against 0.776 on MBPP), but that result is pooled [[probing-functional-correctness-in-diffusion-language-models-full-text]].

The committed prediction is that the controlled within-problem gap is small and model-dependent, held at medium-low confidence rather than as a single number. The samples behind it are small (19 and 56 mixed problems), Miglani's confidence intervals are not reported in the sections read, and none of the three comparisons includes a numeric or categorical verbal readout, a trained own readout, or per-candidate sampling agreement. Campos's EigenScore scores the problem, not the candidate, so its within-problem AUROC is 0.5 by construction.

### 3.4 Trained stated confidence is now a concrete threat on code

Pivot criterion A14 asks whether an elicitation method, a stated-confidence training method, or recalibration that a fair comparison must include closes the gap. On code it is not triggered, but the threat is no longer hypothetical. CREDO (concurrent) trains stated confidence with execution labels on DeepCoder with Qwen3-8B and reports in-domain code AUROC of 0.850, against 0.815 for RLCR, 0.776 for DCPO, 0.753 for GRPO, and 0.690 for the untrained base (Table 1). It has no probe arm [[beyond-verbalized-confidence-calibrating-reasoners-with-differentiable-readouts]]. EpiCaR reports AUROC 0.538 on MBPP out of domain, also with no probe (Table 4) [[epicar-knowing-what-you-dont-know-mattersfor-better-reasoning-in-llms]]. L2 Q11 therefore moves from CLEAN (provisional) to NARROW: the residual is trained stated confidence against a probe on the same code generations, scored within problem.

The general-NLP evidence points the same way. Probe-SD distills a probe into the verbal channel and lifts Qwen3-14B's verbal AUROC on TriviaQA from .825 to .928 in domain (.715 to .783 out of domain) against a probe at .948, while isotonic recalibration cuts ECE from .178 to .013 but leaves AUROC at .825 (Tables 1 and 2). It adds that "internal states answer 'how certain am I' well and 'which answer is right' poorly" [[calibration-not-answer-selection-distilling-internal-confidence-in-reasoning-mod]]. RLCR's trained verbal confidence reaches AUROC 0.69 against 0.55 for a final-layer probe on the RLVR model on HotpotQA, and 0.67 against 0.65 on Big-Math (Table 1) [[beyond-binary-rewards-training-lms-to-reason-about-their-uncertainty-2]]. ConfTuner reports AUROC gains of up to 14.4% [[conftuner-training-large-language-models-to-express-their-confidence-verbally]].

The opposing evidence is just as specific. When Words Fall Short shows that estimator overfitting at a single training endpoint "can bias comparisons in favor of verbalized confidence"; with validation-based checkpoint selection, a hidden-state estimator beats RL-trained verbal confidence by 0.161 AUROC in domain and 0.078 out of domain (Section 2.2) [[when-words-fall-short-iterative-synergy-between-verbalized-reasoning-and-hidden]]. A pre-registered confidence SFT lowered verbal AUROC2 from 0.554 to 0.509 and cost 7.6 accuracy points [[distilling-self-consistency-into-verbal-confidence-a-pre-registered-negative-res]]. On LiveCodeBench, RLCM's probe-read confidence has ECE 0.051 against 0.218 for RLCR's verbal confidence, but that column reports ECE only and the model was trained on math [[process-supervision-of-confidence-margin-for-calibrated-llm-reasoning]].

These sides do not cancel; they define the experiment. RLCR and When Words Fall Short disagree over the same comparison, so general NLP has not settled whether training closes the discrimination gap. Code adds a concrete reason the answer could differ: execution labels let every readout be trained on exactly the same supervision as the probe at no annotation cost, which the NLP comparisons, labeled by judges or string match, lack. A supervision-matched arm (a trained verbal head or a logit-feature head on the same execution labels), with validation-based checkpoint selection for every fitted reader and an isotonic-verbal control, is not optional. CREDO also strengthens the strict reading that the trained arm may close the gap. The paper must say so in advance and treat closure as a finding, not a failure.

### 3.5 The strongest objection: the controlled test is nearly a union

A strict reader argues that the controlled test is OCCUPIED (union) except for one arm. Campos supplies within-problem scoring of probe against own confidence and an input-only baseline; Ribeiro's four-candidate design and RQ2 supply within-task verbal comparison; Miglani supplies within-problem centering on an autoregressive code model. What remains, the trained own readout, is the arm whose expected result CREDO and Probe-SD point toward closing the gap.

The objection is strong about size and wrong about classification. Under A20, a union verdict requires the works together to cover every dimension of the core claim. Numeric verbal confidence scored within problem on self-generated samples, and a supervision-matched own readout on those same samples, are covered by none of Ribeiro, Campos, or Miglani. Ribeiro's within-task verbal comparison uses provenance-confounded candidates in RQ1 and reports pass@rank in RQ2, with no AUROC and no trained readout. The residual is a label protocol and a set of controls, so clause 1 holds, and clause 2 holds through three code-specific reasons: symmetric supervision is free under execution labels, function-level difficulty is recoverable from prompt text, and the occupant's contrast classes differ in provenance. Q1c is NARROW under every setting of S1 and S3. The objection survives in one form the recommendation adopts: the residual is small, and its most informative arm is the one most likely to return a null. A forward screen of 14 citers of Ribeiro, one of its follow-up, and four of Di Cicco found none running a supervision-matched control.

### 3.6 The controlled test is the hinge, and each outcome yields a paper

| Outcome of the controlled test | What the paper can claim | What becomes worth running |
|---|---|---|
| (α) Probe keeps a within-problem margin over a trained own readout, with a problem-clustered CI excluding zero | The disconnect is unexpressed information on code beyond difficulty and supervision; this corrects and strengthens Ribeiro's design | The factorial decomposition; L3 routing by the probe |
| (β) Probe matches a trained own readout but beats zero-shot verbal | The disconnect is an elicitation and supervision gap that closes once the output channel is trained on execution labels; the code analogue of Probe-SD and CREDO with a probe arm | Trained-readout routing in L3; the factorial run becomes optional |
| (γ) Every readout sits near 0.5 to 0.65 within problem, and pooled differences are difficulty | Code-correctness readouts at this scale mostly detect hard problems; the pooled probe advantage in the literature is mostly between-problem | L3 replay, predicted to fail in the hard stratum |

Outcome β does not trigger pivot A14, because A14 is defined over a fetched record showing the gap closes. The criterion protects against building on a claim the literature has already refuted; it does not stop the paper from refuting the claim itself. Under TMLR's first criterion all three outcomes are publishable if the design is right. Under its second, α and β interest the probing and calibration audiences directly, and γ interests anyone who reports pooled AUROC for code correctness, a practice the occupants themselves follow.

### 3.7 The factorial residuals: real, but dependent on the controlled result

**Base versus instruct (Q2a).** Rathnasuriya and Yang (Section 5.5) give the only code comparison of base and instruct models under execution labels, at token level only. DeepSeekCoder's base confidence AUC is 0.4391, meaning certainty is anti-aligned with correctness, rising to 0.5654 after tuning (ΔAUC +0.1263, CI [0.0447, 0.2089]); OpenCoder moves by -0.0136 (CI [-0.0911, 0.0652]) while failing-program confidence rises from 0.8685 to 0.9224 [[characterizing-overconfident-failure-in-llm-based-code-generation-full-text]]. Tahimic and Cheng find SAE directions trained on Gemma-2-2b base remain effective after instruction tuning (feature F1 0.821 to 0.772, Section 5.5), with no verbal readout [[mechanistic-interpretability-of-code-correctness-in-llms-via-sparse-autoencoders]]. The general-NLP side is crowded: Miao and Ungar find "base models verbalize poorly calibrated confidence despite encoding well-calibrated accuracy information internally," with accuracy and verbal-confidence directions at cosine similarity below 0.04 (Section 3.3) [[closing-the-confidence-faithfulness-gap-in-large-language-models]]; the GPT-4 report finds the pre-trained model "highly calibrated" (Figure 8) [[gpt-4-technical-report]]; six base and instruct pairs show instruction tuning raising ECE by 13.1% on average, with models assigning up to 26% higher confidence to their own responses [[large-language-models-are-overconfident-in-their-own-responses]]. The code-specific reason is that an anti-aligned base readout inverts the usual story that tuning corrupts a calibrated base, so Q2a is NARROW. The strict reader's objection carries weight: the reason rests on one pair whose interval excludes zero, for token readouts only, and says nothing yet about the probe-minus-verbal gap. There is also a design hazard: Ghosh et al. pre-registered a base-versus-instruct test and declined to run it because the 7B base model's verbal output used "essentially two labels" [[measurement-validity-and-representation-dynamics-ofconfidence-signals-in-large-l]]. Verbal readouts on base models may need a P(True)-style format.

**Reasoning (Q2b, Q9, Q10).** Campos prefills gpt-oss so that it answers without reasoning; only in a construct-validity check does medium-effort reasoning raise the Yes-probability AUROC "from 0.68 to 0.73, still below the trained internal methods," and the probe is never read under reasoning (Sections V-A and VIII) [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]]. Barkan et al. toggle reasoning budgets for in-advance verbal confidence on BigCodeBench and find "reasoning LLMs perform comparably to or worse than non-reasoning LLMs" [[do-large-language-models-know-what-they-are-capable-of]]. Open the Oyster compares different weights (DeepSeek-Reasoner and DeepSeek-Chat) on code-reasoning tasks, with CCP ECE 0.066 against 0.143 (Section 5.1.1) [[open-the-oyster-empirical-evaluation-and-improvement-of-code-reasoning-confidenc]]. Outside code, Yoon et al. find reasoning models better calibrated "in 33 out of the 36 settings" [[reasoning-models-better-express-their-confidence]]; probes lead verbal output in reasoning models, for example GLM-4.7-Flash AUROC 0.768 against 0.669 [[what-llm-forecasters-know-but-dont-say-probing-internal-representations-for-cali]], and 0.953 against 0.67 on MATH-500 [[hidden-error-awareness-in-chain-of-thought-reasoningthe-signal-is-diagnostic-not]]. A strict reader rates the same-weights toggle OCCUPIED by analogy under A21 clause 2. That fails because the general-NLP direction is contested: Yoon's improvement on one side, Barkan's null and Miao and Ungar's "reasoning contamination" on the other, where joint solve-and-rate prompting inverts the confidence relation from +0.26 to -0.63 (Section 3.4) [[closing-the-confidence-faithfulness-gap-in-large-language-models]]. Code adds an operational obstacle: Zeng et al. excluded code because "most models struggled to output both code snippets and confidence simultaneously" [[thinking-out-loud-do-reasoning-models-know-when-theyre-right]]. A same-weights reasoning toggle on code with the probe read under reasoning is NARROW. Q10 (probing reasoning models with reasoning on, on code) is NARROW on the RLCM column; Q9 is NARROW for post-hoc stated confidence and CLEAN (provisional) for trace signals beating the final number on code.

**Format (Q3).** Within verbal readouts on code, format is OCCUPIED: Tuerlings' three formats give Qwen-32B AUROC 0.834, 0.810, and 0.841, and DeepSeek-6.7B 0.518, 0.716, and 0.498 [[verbalized-uncertainty-in-coding-llms-contamination-aware-calibration-uncertainl]], and Ghosh reports cross-format Spearman correlations of 0.126, -0.069, and 0.247 outside code. Only the format-by-readout interaction is NARROW, and it belongs in the design as a controlled nuisance factor. A calibrated invariance audit of 30.73 million executed generations supplies the template: test whether a format effect exceeds what prompt-wording noise alone produces [[identity-or-prompt-noise-a-calibrated-invariance-audit-of-llm-code-generation]].

**Post-training stages (Q8) and where the gap arises (Q4).** A stage decomposition exists on math only, with Pre-CoT AUROC 0.594 (Instruct), 0.526 (SFT), 0.320 (RL), and 0.644 (OPD) from one backbone [[post-training-shifts-confidence-a-three-stage-analysis-of-how-sft-rl-and-opd-sha]], and base models are semantically calibrated before RL or DPO tuning breaks it [[trained-on-tokens-calibrated-on-conceptsthe-emergence-of-semantic-calibration-in]]. Q8 is a weak NARROW, its code reason a hypothesis; Q4 is NARROW.

All of these residuals survive A21 if its code reasons are accepted, but none should be the headline. Decomposing a gap that has not been shown to exist under controls partitions noise. The factorial arms therefore belong in a pre-registered conditional extension, run if the controlled gap clears a stated margin with an interval excluding zero and reported as exploratory otherwise.

### 3.8 Causal location (Q12)

On code, causal work targets output correctness, not stated confidence. SAE steering fixes 4.04% of errors but corrupts 14.66% of correct code [[mechanistic-interpretability-of-code-correctness-in-llms-via-sparse-autoencoders]], and steering along Miglani's probe direction lowers pass@1 from 0.433 to 0.388 toward pass and to 0.298 toward fail [[a-heckler-in-the-hidden-statecorrectness-signals-in-diffusion-language-models]]. Outside code, activation patching shows a post-answer-newline probe is causally sufficient to rescue error detection, reaching AUROC .986 within incorrect trials against .908 for the best behavioral baseline [[how-llms-detect-and-correct-their-own-errors-the-role-of-internal-confidence-sig]], and circuit-level intervention cuts inflated verbal ECE from 0.647 to 0.068 [[wired-for-overconfidence-a-mechanistic-perspective-on-inflated-verbalized-confid]]. Q12 is NARROW and pivot A15 is not triggered, but causal work multiplies the method surface without strengthening the headline and stays out of the first paper.

### 3.9 Pivot criteria and verdict on L2

- **Pivot 1 (A20 union across probe, stated, sampling, base-instruct, and reasoning):** not triggered under any switch setting. The stated-confidence side and the factor side never meet on code.
- **Pivot 2 (A21, residual is only a model change):** triggered for the uncontrolled headline; never triggered for the controlled test; triggered for the factorial versions only if S3 rejects the code reasons.
- **Pivot 3 (A14, trained stated confidence closes the gap on code):** not triggered, because CREDO has no probe arm, but concrete. The design must include the arm that tests it.
- **Pivot 4 (A15, causal localization on code):** not triggered.

Whole L2 is NARROW, conditional on the controlled test being the headline. The residual in one sentence: a same-sample comparison on self-generated function-level code, under execution labels, scored pooled and within problem, of a probe, a supervision-matched own readout, zero-shot numeric and categorical verbal confidence, token probability, and per-candidate sampling agreement, with prompt-only and code-text-only baselines. Closest code work: Ribeiro (zero-shot verbal, provenance-confounded) and Campos (pooled versus within, no verbal, no trained own readout). Closest NLP work: Liu et al. 2023 (supervision-matched, no execution labels).

## 4. L3: Verification utility and confidence updating

### 4.1 Selective prediction and repository agents are occupied

CodeRefuser (concurrent) gives risk-controlled abstention via Learn-Then-Test over execution-cluster agreement on four open code models, with "an average of 26.5% and 11.9% absolute improvements w.r.t. abstention precision and F1-score"; at alpha 0.2 on MBPP with Qwen2.5-Coder-32B it abstains on about 30% of tasks against more than 80% for perplexity (Table 1, Figures 2 and 3) [[260517029-task-abstention-for-large-language-models-in-code-generation-full-text]]. Selective generation with false-discovery-rate control uses fuzz-generated tests [[towards-functional-correctness-of-code-models-with-selective-generation]], and a deferral framework reports selective accuracy over 70% at 80% coverage on MBPP+ (Section 4.1) [[when-to-answer-and-when-to-defer-a-decision-framework-for-reliable-code-predicti]]. Q3 is OCCUPIED; without concurrent work it rests on the two pre-window works. Repository-agent uncertainty (Q6) is OCCUPIED by Kaddour et al. alone, who elicit stated success probability before, during, and after execution on SWE-bench Pro and find post-execution predictions of about 73% success against 35% true for GPT-5.2-Codex [[agentic-uncertainty-reveals-agentic-overconfidence]].

### 4.2 Routing: positives and negatives use different gates and budget units

Every positive code result for confidence-routed compute uses a learned or proxy gate:

- Heteroskedastic Signals replays MBPP with a logit-margin proxy. At beta 10% for Qwen3-8B, Random scores .551, Threshold .636, cost-stratified thresholding .671, and Oracle 1.00 (Table 1). The authors "add 10% Bernoulli label noise," the oracle is the label itself, and the gain over thresholding has CI [-0.2, +7.0] [[heteroskedastic-signals-in-budgeted-llm-verificationstructural-heterogeneity-lim]].
- SWE-Verifier gates step sampling with a trained Qwen3.5-4B scorer: MiniMax-M2.7 moves from 75.47 ± 1.40 to 77.20 ± 0.87 at 1.09x cost, verifying 3.43% of steps, against 75.27 at 3.35x for all-step self-verification. Its "Oracle@3" (82.20 at 3.00x) is best-of-three rollouts, not a routing oracle, and no random gate is reported (Tables 1 and 2) [[261030284-swe-verifier-selective-verification-for-coding-agents-full-text]].
- SWE-Router's trained value head reaches Route-AUC 0.780, +15.3 points over a non-temporal baseline, above a random-assignment band, but routes between models and has no oracle arm (Section 5.3) [[swe-router-routing-in-multi-turn-agentic-software-engineering-tasks]].
- Capability self-assessment beats random routing "by about 8.2 percentage points at matched cloud-call budgets," on math with code only as transfer [[capability-self-assessment-in-large-language-models]].

The negatives use other budget units. Give Up to Get More shows that difficulty-proportional rules capture "at most 13%" of oracle gain on hard workloads even with true pass rates; on CodeContests (Llama-3-8B, mean pass rate 0.021) the best rule captures 1%. A prior must reach a rank correlation of about 0.3 to 0.55 before Thompson sampling beats round-robin, and LLM-derived priors reach Spearman 0.10 to 0.46 with gains of -2.1 to -0.1 (Tables 1 and 3) [[give-up-to-get-more-priors-not-policies-govern-test-time-compute-allocation]]. Uncertainty-triggered regeneration lowers pass@1 in five of six configurations, with verbalized confidence correlating at -0.327, -0.090, and -0.114 with correctness on a held-out set of 31 [[1introduction]]. Learned stopping beats matched random restarting by at most 1.98 points, entirely through between-task allocation, with within-task AUC 0.50 [0.48, 0.51] [[continue-or-restart-the-limits-of-early-failure-prediction-in-coding-agents]], and trajectory predictors reach about 0.62 AUROC against 0.92 for an issue-difficulty reference [[hard-issue-weak-model-or-bad-runrethinking-failure-prediction-for-coding-agents]]. Adjacent works gate without random or oracle arms: staged verification gives 11.64x throughput at 8.26% accuracy loss [[1introduction-3]], and CoTT cuts test-generation cost from $0.077 to $0.065 [[confidence-gated-transductive-test-generation-for-code-reranking]].

"Gating helps" and "gating does not help" are both right because they test different gate classes and budget units. Give Up supplies the quantity that joins them: a gate helps only when its within-pool rank correlation with outcome clears roughly 0.3 to 0.55 and mean pass rate exceeds about 0.1. The residual is a gate built from the code model's elicited verbal or probe confidence, under unmodified execution labels, with matched-budget random and label-sorted oracle arms, and it is NARROW.

A strict reader rates routing OCCUPIED (union): switching the gate from a logit proxy to an elicited score is a change of signal, not of factor or use, and its outcome is predictable from the published threshold. The reading is not adopted, because no fetched work gates on elicited verbal or probe confidence with both random and label-sorted oracle arms on clean labels, and the code reason is concrete: pass rates on hard code are very low, the regime where difficulty rules fail. The objection does correctly bound the result. L3's gain is capped by the readout quality the L2 test measures, so if within-problem AUROC is 0.6 to 0.7 at best, a within-problem gate can gain little, and Give Up predicts the hard stratum fails for every gate. Routing would move toward CLEAN (provisional) if concurrent work were excluded, since every positive and the main negative are concurrent, but that would make L3 look cleanest exactly when it is least informative.

### 4.3 Updating after a shown failing test

No fetched record measures stated confidence before and after the model sees a failing test on its own program, tied to the repair outcome, with a contrast between valid and spurious failures. The nearest works miss the condition in instructive ways. Kaddour et al. find that agents "develop 'cold feet': confidence decreases as they observe their partial work, but this doubt is uninformative," with mid-execution AUROC about 0.53 for GPT, 0.49 to 0.64 for Gemini, and 0.62 to 0.52 for Claude, yet the agent "cannot execute code, run tests" (Section 2.2.1) [[agentic-uncertainty-reveals-agentic-overconfidence]]. Barkan et al. update confidence after outcomes of earlier contracts, not test failures on the same code [[do-large-language-models-know-what-they-are-capable-of]]. XConf builds confidence from graded past episodes across tasks [[confidence-comes-from-experience-experiential-confidence-estimation-from-reasoni]]. Resist and Update supplies an evidence-versus-pressure design and finds confidence "a weaker instrument" than answer and caveat coordinates, outside code [[260712985-resist-and-update-counterfactual-report-coordinates-full-text]]. Iscan's pre-registered placebo design finds "blind resampling beat bare-code retry by +18 net unlocks" (p=0.0021) but never elicits confidence [[falsification-not-exposure-placebo-controlled-decomposition-of-self-repair-feedb]].

The A21 reason here is the strongest in the study: code is the domain where the evidence shown to the model is executable and verifiable, and where a spurious failure can be built deliberately by mutating a test, a contrast QA cannot supply with ground truth. The component is CLEAN (provisional), and it is also the most fragile CLEAN in the study, because such a measurement could sit as one paragraph inside an agent or self-repair paper, invisible to the title and abstract search that worked. Forward screens of three close L3 occupants were not run.

### 4.4 Verdict on L3

L3 is NARROW. It belongs in the paper as a secondary analysis because it costs almost nothing once L1 and L2 generations exist: an offline replay at budgets of 10%, 20%, 30%, and 50%, with ten or more seeds, paired gains with 95% CIs, stratification by mean pass rate (below 0.1 versus above 0.4), and unmodified labels. A replay that confirms Give Up's hard-stratum prediction is still a finding. The update-after-failure test needs a new elicitation protocol (genuine, spurious, and placebo failures) and fits only as a small, hedged add-on.

## 5. L4: Per-requirement confidence and localization

Gros and Devanbu (preprint, first posted 2025-12-31, so not concurrent) occupy line- and token-level localization. Their labels are "Minimal Intent Aligning Patches" verified by hidden tests, and they compare token probability, multisampling, line-verbalized confidence, and probes on GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct across HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench, and RepoCod. For Qwen at line level (Table 4), token probability has BSS -1.18, ECE 0.54, and AUC 0.55; multisampling 0.01, 0.17, and 0.72; line-verbalized confidence -0.72, 0.43, and 0.71; and Probe-7B 0.29, 0.08, and 0.83. For the Qwen generator the probe reads the generator's own states, so four families have been compared on the same code at line level; for GPT-4o the "probe" reads a separate 0.5B or 7B supervisor and is not GPT-4o's own state [[localized-calibrated-uncertainty-in-code-language-models]]. FLARE localizes from generator token probabilities with 67% Top-1 accuracy and gains 1.72% to 7.42% absolute pass@1 over NL-Debugging, with no matched resampling arm (Table 4) [[flare-fine-grained-diagnostic-feedback-for-llm-code-refinement-2]]. PtTrust assesses line-level risk from internal states [[risk-assessment-framework-for-code-llms-via-leveraging-internal-states]]. Agent-step probes decode test-pass, partial-correctness ("the number of failing tests is strictly less than at the beginning"), and regression labels with AUC up to 0.83 [[260705188-latent-programming-horizons-in-coding-agents-full-text]]. TokenRepair fixes 88 bugs on Defects4J 1.2 and 139 on HumanEval-Java, and removing its uncertainty-guided localization costs 20.6% [[enhancing-automated-program-repair-via-faulty-token-localization-and-quality-awa]]. Two neighbors bound token signals: highlighting by generation probability "does not provide any benefit over the baseline with no highlighting" [[generation-probabilities-are-not-enough-uncertainty-highlighting-in-ai-code-comp]], and outside code a calibrated probe misses 81% of single-step unrecoverable failures [[effectiveness-of-hidden-state-probesfor-localizing-reasoning-failures]]. Q4 is OCCUPIED.

Three residuals survive. Per-test-group confidence as localization of the failing requirement (Q1) is NARROW: requirement units are specification-level and need no patch oracle. Targeted repair versus matched-budget regeneration (Q2) is NARROW: Olausson et al. find "pass rates are higher or equally high with i.i.d. sampling (without repair), especially when the budget is small" [[is-self-repair-a-silver-bullet-for-code-generation]], Siddiq et al. find calibration-guided repair gives "only limited improvements while frequently introducing functional regressions" [[an-empirical-study-of-security-calibration-in-large-language-models-for-code]], and the targeted-repair papers lack the matched arm. A strict reader calls Q2 a union of FLARE and Olausson; even granting that, it is a repair-method comparison, not a calibration claim. Self-written test adequacy against own confidence (Q3) is CLEAN (provisional): the nearest works measure adequacy without confidence, with 19.7% of LLM-written tests failing to execute and 15.6% of failures wrong-oracle [[261032548-beyond-coverage-and-mutation-a-blind-spot-analysis-of-llm-generated-un]], or weight tests by leave-one-out AUC with no calibration [[aces-who-tests-the-tests-leave-one-outauc-consistency-for-code-generation]].

L4 is NARROW but the wrong bet for a first TMLR paper. What blocks it is readiness, not the literature: the per-test pipeline in `src/v4_dprc` is frozen legacy code never run beyond a stub, the test-group label unit is noisier than published patch labels, and Gros and Devanbu's leave-one-dataset-out collapse (Qwen Probe-7B BSS -0.01 on LiveCodeBench, Table 7) warns that fine-grained probes transfer badly. The paper should cite the line-level occupant, name the test-group unit as untested future work, and leave `src/v4_dprc` out.

## 6. F: Measurement-instrument framing

F is the one candidate whose verdict moves under no switch. Every quantity it proposes maps onto an existing one:

| F quantity | Nearest existing quantity |
|---|---|
| Readout bias | Calibration-in-the-large, (mu_F − mu_Y)^2, in the Yates covariance decomposition, which Vieira rearranges into "a variance mismatch term, a correlation deficit term, and a calibration-in-the-large term" [[260305544-yates-covariance-decomposition-rearrangement-for-brier-score-full-text]]; or the signal-detection criterion c [[llms-as-signal-detectors-sensitivity-bias-and-the-temperaturecriterion-analogy]] |
| Resolution against an internal reference | A difference of Murphy resolutions: Ghosh et al. report 0.06371 for behavioral consensus, 0.05154 for the probe, and 0.03160 for verbal, a 2.02x ratio (Table 3) [[measurement-validity-and-representation-dynamics-ofconfidence-signals-in-large-l]]; or the grouping term, which post-hoc recalibration cannot fix [[decomposing-probabilistic-scores-reliability-information-loss-and-uncertaintysup]] |
| Cross-format noise | Pairwise Spearman of 0.126, -0.069, and 0.247 across three verbal formats (Ghosh), and AUPRC spread under paraphrase [[factual-confidence-of-llms-on-reliability-and-robustness-of-current-estimators]] |
| Metacognitive efficiency | meta-d' [[measuring-some-aspects-of-the-metacognition-of-ai]] and the model-free meta-I, after Cacioli's v3 retracts the M-ratio "because the meta-d' efficiency ratio is not well defined for open-ended QA" [[do-llms-know-what-they-know-measuring-metacognitive-efficiency-with-signal-detec]] |

Circularity decides the case. On code, the "internal reference" is a probe trained on the same execution labels it would referee, so any quantity defined relative to internal state collapses into a contrast of two ordinary resolutions. The one quantity beyond Murphy, an item-by-format variance component, is occupied as a statistic in general NLP and has no stated code reason to differ, so it fails A21.

The vocabulary is also in use. "Measurement validity" frames Ghosh's audit; "SDT is applied here as a measurement framework" appears in the signal-detection study; "instrument" appears in a reactivity argument that the rating question "displace[s] the probed state" [[dont-ask-understanding-what-numeric-self-reports-measure-in-language-models]]; and construct-validity framing is already applied to coding agents, with GPT-5 submitting 100% of tasks against a 44% resolve rate [[confident-and-wrong-silent-semantic-failures-in-coding-agents]]. Only metrology-proper terms (measurand, GUM uncertainty budget, traceability) are unused for LLM confidence, and the two formal metrology papers treat classifiers, one explicitly excluding generative ML [[a-metrological-framework-for-uncertainty-evaluation-in-machine-learning-classifi]] [[uncertainty-evaluation-bridging-machine-learning-and-measurement-science-full-te]]. Unused vocabulary is not a contribution.

F is OCCUPIED, and the framing is used rather than unused or contested. It earns a place as reporting discipline, not as a claim: separate bias, resolution, and noise; report Murphy terms and Brier skill beside AUROC. The L1 protocol already requires that discipline, so "readout" and "measurand" can serve as exposition inside L2.

## 9. Ranked recommendation for TMLR

### 9.1 Rank 1: the controlled disconnect test on an L1 measurement layer, with an L3 replay

**Contribution sentence.** "On self-generated function-level code with execution labels, we test whether hidden-state probes carry correctness information that the model's own readouts lack. Every readout is also given a version trained on the same labels. Within-problem and between-problem discrimination are scored separately, and every ranking is checked against the calibration estimator, a stronger test oracle, and a per-model release-date split."

**Evidence the paper must generate.** Every number comes from the new pipeline; the legacy bundle supplies none.

1. **Multiple samples per problem.** At least 10 per problem, so that lambda in Bulut's Equation 1 is not zero, with at least 100 mixed-outcome problems per model and a problem-clustered paired bootstrap for every difference. Rathnasuriya's OpenCoder interval of [-0.0911, 0.0652] shows how wide uncontrolled intervals run. The share of mixed problems at per-problem pass rate p and k samples is 1 − p^k − (1 − p)^k, an illustrative formula rather than a measured number, so hard benchmarks will need more problems.
2. **Readouts on identical samples:** a last-token probe and a prompt-final probe; a trained own readout (a verbal head or logit-feature head on the same execution labels); zero-shot numeric and categorical verbal confidence; token probability; per-candidate sampling agreement built only from tests disjoint from the label suite; and prompt-only and code-text-only classifiers. Validation-based checkpoint selection for every fitted reader, plus an isotonic-verbal control.
3. **The measurement layer:** pooled, within-problem, and between-problem AUROC; Brier skill with reliability intervals beside AUROC for every recalibrated readout; ECE under equal-width, equal-mass, and debiased estimators; labels under HumanEval and MBPP versus HumanEval+ and MBPP+; and LiveCodeBench windows split around each model's cutoff with a pre-cutoff comparison group.
4. **A provenance check on the occupant:** a sign-reversed or fitted likelihood baseline on Ribeiro's public data, to test how much of the reported margin the reference-versus-other-model design explains.
5. **An offline L3 routing replay** with random and label-sorted oracle arms at matched budgets, stratified by pass rate.
6. **A pre-registered conditional extension:** base-versus-instruct and same-weights reasoning arms, run only if the controlled gap clears the stated margin.

**TMLR fit.** Criterion 1 is met by construction: the headline is a controlled measurement reported whichever way it falls. A null says the published probe advantage on code is a supervised, between-problem effect and that the model's own confidence, given the same labels, ranks its candidates as well as a probe; that would revise how Ribeiro, Openia, Di Cicco, and the Klassert thesis are read. A positive result says information is lost between state and output even under matched supervision. Criterion 2 is met because the ML calibration and SE confidence audiences are both active (ICSE 2026, ISSTA 2026, Findings of ACL 2026, UncertaiNLP 2026, COLM 2026, and many concurrent preprints). Novelty is claimed only for the controlled test and the protocol, written as "we found no prior code study that ..." with the instrument gaps named, which keeps the paper clear of the incorrect-novelty clause.

**Main risks.** First, a scoop: Campos et al. already recommend within-problem reporting, and their group holds the models, probes, and labels, so a trained own-readout arm is all it needs [[which-signals-of-code-correctness-survive-distribution-shift-hidden-state-and-ca]] [[abstract]]. Even then, the verbal arms, the oracle swap, and the release contrast would stand. Second, the trained arm may close the gap, as CREDO and Probe-SD suggest; the mitigation is to frame that result as the finding (outcome β), which either matches or contradicts the NLP result on code. Third, verbal readouts collapse on small instruct models (8 of 18 cells in Tuerlings); report collapse as a validity outcome with a distinct-values guard and rely on the trained head for the matched comparison.

### 9.2 Rank 2: the L1 label-oracle and estimator protocol alone

**Contribution sentence.** "The ranking of four confidence families on code does, or does not, change when benchmark labels are replaced by augmented-test labels, when the estimator moves from equal-width ECE to equal-mass and debiased ECE, Brier skill, and AUROC, and when problems are split around each model's release cutoff." This is the fallback if the controlled test is scooped or the probe arms prove infeasible. It claims novelty only for the protocol (NARROW) and for Q10 and Q13 (CLEAN (provisional)), never for the comparison. Fit is good on criterion 1 and modest on criterion 2. Main risk: its direction is predicted by Liu et al., and rankings may prove invariant, which is still a robustness finding, but a less interesting one.

### 9.3 Rank 3: stated-confidence update after a shown failing test

A paired experiment with genuine, spurious, and placebo failing tests, following Resist and Update's evidence-versus-pressure design and Iscan's placebo logic, measuring the change in stated confidence and its AUROC against repair success [[260712985-resist-and-update-counterfactual-report-coordinates-full-text]] [[falsification-not-exposure-placebo-controlled-decomposition-of-self-repair-feedb]]. It has the strongest code-specific reason of any component and the highest instrument exposure. It fits as a small, hedged section of the Rank 1 paper if compute allows, and should rise only if completed forward screens and a working full-text search find no occupant.

### 9.4 Rank 4: L4, out; F as exposition

L4 is NARROW, but it needs the unrun `src/v4_dprc` pipeline and an operator scope decision, and it adds a repair-method claim rather than a calibration claim; `src/v4_dprc` does not belong in the paper. F is OCCUPIED; use its vocabulary as exposition with Murphy terms and meta-I reported, and claim nothing for it.

### 9.5 What would overturn this ranking

- A pilot showing a within-problem probe margin above 0.05 over the trained readout with an interval excluding zero would revive "locating the disconnect" as a positive spine and promote the factorial extension.
- A trained own readout that still trails the probe by at least 0.08 within-problem AUROC would make the contradiction with Probe-SD the headline.
- A working full-text search that finds no occupant for the update-after-failure test would raise Rank 3.
- A hidden occupant behind the gaps plus rejection of the A21 code reasons would push the factorial and routing components to OCCUPIED, but Rank 1 would survive as a claims-reduced study with a correct-by-construction design.

### 9.6 Forward-looking implications

The field is converging on one measurement correction from several directions. Bulut's identity, the agent-difficulty decompositions [[disentangling-task-difficulty-from-run-level-failure-in-agent-failure-prediction]] [[hard-issue-weak-model-or-bad-runrethinking-failure-prediction-for-coding-agents]] [[continue-or-restart-the-limits-of-early-failure-prediction-in-coding-agents]], Campos's within-problem recommendation, and Liu's oracle-independence rule all say that most published confidence numbers measure the problem and the oracle, not the candidate. Reviewers at ML and SE venues are likely to start asking for within-problem scores and oracle swaps as a matter of course, much as EvalPlus made augmented tests standard for accuracy. At the same time, training-side work such as CREDO, Probe-SD, and RLCR is moving from calibrating the verbal channel toward improving its discrimination, so the first code paper to put a trained readout beside a probe will occupy the controlled test. The window is real but short, and speed of submission is itself a positioning variable. If trained own readouts close the gap, the practical message for tool builders is to train the output channel on execution labels rather than ship probes, and confidence-routed execution becomes a question about trained heads. If the gap survives within problem under matched supervision, the case for white-box confidence in code tools becomes much stronger than current evidence supports, and the factorial decomposition is the natural follow-up. Either way, a paper that arrives with the measurement layer already applied becomes a reference point, while another pooled "probes beat verbal" table will meet a literature that has moved past it.
